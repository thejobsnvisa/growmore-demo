From 13 November 2026, separate Subclass 485 Subsequent Entrant visa applications will no longer be available under Australia's new Temporary Graduate visa rules. 

This is a significant change for international graduates who already hold a Subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa and were planning to bring their spouse, de facto partner or dependent children to Australia later. 

The Australian Government has introduced the Migration Amendment (Temporary Graduate Visa Reform) Regulations 2026, which was registered on 12 November 2026. The aim of this amendment is to tighten the eligibility requirements for family members applying for a Subclass 485 visa. 

This means that family members will generally need to apply together with the primary Subclass 485 applicant rather than lodge a separate application after the primary visa has been granted. 

But there is a catch. The new rules do not completely remove the option for family members to obtain a Subclass 485 visa. Only applicants who meet specific exceptions will remain eligible under the revised provisions. 

Here is what existing Subclass 485 visa holders and future applicants need to know. 

## What Has Changed for Subclass 485 Subsequent Entrants? 

Under the revised Migration Regulations 1994, a family member seeking to satisfy the secondary criteria for a Subclass 485 visa must generally lodge their application at the same time as the primary applicant. 

The new requirements include: 

* The primary applicant must be physically in Australia when lodging the Subclass 485 application, but must not be in immigration clearance. 
* The secondary applicant must meet at least one of the specified eligibility exceptions. 
* The primary and secondary applications must be lodged together as a combined application. 
* A secondary applicant can lodge from inside or outside Australia, but cannot apply while in immigration clearance. 

This means that a person who has already received their Subclass 485 visa will generally no longer be able to sponsor a spouse or dependent child through a separate 485 subsequent entrant application. 

## Who Can Still Be Included in a Subclass 485 Application? 

The new regulations retain six specific exceptions under which family members can apply as secondary applicants alongside the primary applicant. 

**1.Primary Applicant Holds an Eligible Passport** 

The primary applicant holds a passport from a Pacific-regional country or a country specified under subitem 1222(5A) of Schedule 1 of the Migration Regulations 1994. 

**2.Primary Applicant Has Completed a Doctoral Degree** 

The primary applicant's most recent Student visa was granted for a doctoral degree, and they have completed that degree. 

**3.Studies Were Fully Funded by a Foreign Government** 

The most recent student visa of the applicant was issued for one or more courses of study funded entirely by the foreign government. 

**4.Child Born After the Primary Applicant's Visa Grant** 

The secondary applicant is a child of the primary applicant and born after the time of receiving the most recent student visa or Subclass 485 visa. 

**5.Previously Held a Student Visa as a Dependant** 

The secondary applicant currently holds, or held within the six months immediately before lodging the application, a Student visa granted on the basis of being a family member of the primary applicant. 

The primary applicant must also hold or have previously held that Student visa as the primary applicant. 

**6.Previously Held a Subclass 485 Visa as a Dependant** 

The secondary applicant currently holds, or held within the six months immediately before lodging the application, a Subclass 485 visa granted on the basis of satisfying the secondary criteria. 

The primary applicant must also hold or have previously held that Subclass 485 visa on the basis of satisfying the primary criteria. 

## Additional Requirements for Secondary Applicants Already in Australia 

The new regulations also introduce specific visa-status requirements for secondary applicants who are in Australia when lodging their application. 

They must hold one of the following: 

* A substantive visa. 
* A Bridging Visa A (BVA) granted on the basis of a valid visa application. 
* A Bridging Visa B (BVB) granted on the basis of a valid visa application. 
* In certain circumstances, a favourable Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) decision setting aside and substituting a decision not to revoke the cancellation of their Student visa, with the relevant notification received within the prescribed 28-day period. 

These requirements are particularly important for family members currently in Australia on bridging visas or dealing with Student visa cancellation matters. 

## What Happens to Existing Subclass 485 Visa Holders? 

This is one of the most important aspects of the new regulations. 

If you already hold a Subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa and were planning to bring your spouse or dependent children to Australia later through a separate subsequent entrant application, the new rules significantly affect those plans. 

For example, if an international student was granted a Subclass 485 visa earlier and would like to bring their spouse from overseas. 

After 13 November 2026, the spouse will generally no longer be able to lodge a separate Subclass 485 subsequent entrant application, even if they comply with the relevant family member exception categories. 

The exceptions are intended for eligible family members applying together with the primary applicant, not as a new separate application after the primary applicant has already received their visa.
