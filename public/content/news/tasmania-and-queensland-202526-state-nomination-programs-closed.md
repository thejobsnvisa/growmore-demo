July marks the start of a new financial year in Australia and brings a range of changes across the country's immigration system. Alongside new migration settings and policy updates, states are also resetting their skilled migration programs for the 2026–27 program year.

As part of this transition, Tasmania and Queensland have officially closed their 2025–26 State Nominated Migration Programs (SNMP) after filling all available nomination places.

### **Here's what applicants need to know from each state.**

## 1. Tasmania Reaches Full Nomination Allocation

**Migration Tasmania has confirmed it has fully used its 2025–26 nomination allocation by nominating:**

- 1,200 applicants for the Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190)
- 650 applicants for the Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa (Subclass 491)

Although no nomination places remain for the current program year, Tasmania will continue assessing lodged nomination applications. Any applications approved from now on will be nominated only after the state receives its new 2026–27 nomination allocation from the Australian Government.

Plus, applicants who submitted a Registration of Interest (ROI) but were not invited before the program closed do not need to submit a new ROI immediately.

Existing ROIs will remain in the Application Gateway for up to six months and may be considered when the 2026–27 Tasmania nomination program opens.

## 2. Queensland Closes 2025–26 State Nomination Program

Queensland has also announced that its 2025–26 State Nominated Migration Program (SNMP) has reached capacity, with all available nomination places now filled.

The state has stopped accepting new Registrations of Interest (ROIs) for this program year. Once Queensland receives a new nomination allocation for 2026–27, eligible applications will be finalised without requiring applicants to take any further action.

## What This Means for Skilled Migration Applicants

The closure of both state nomination programs reflects continued strong demand for Australia PR pathways through state-nominated skilled migration. While no new nominations will be issued under the 2025–26 allocations in Tasmania or Queensland, eligible applicants already in the pipeline remain in consideration for nomination once fresh allocations become available.

Prospective migrants should also remember that these closures do not mean Australia PR opportunities have ended. Other migration pathways, including [employer-sponsored visas](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/employer-sponsored-visa/), regional visas, and state nomination programs in other Australian states and territories, may still be available for eligible applicants.
