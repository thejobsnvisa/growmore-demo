As the Federal Budget has now been released, Australia’s migration settings for 2026–27 have also been formally confirmed. This gives a clear direction on how the permanent Migration Program will be structured for the upcoming year, including the allocation of places across skilled, family, and special eligibility streams.

This year, the government has set the cap at **185,000 places** for the 2026–27 Permanent Migration Program, which is the same as the previous year. However, the focus has largely shifted towards onshore applicants, skilled migration, and workforce alignment with national labour shortages.

Here is the breakdown on how each migration stream is structured and what it means for applicants in practical terms.


# Overview of 2026–27 Migration Program

The program is divided into three main components:

- **Skilled Migration Program:** 132,240 places (≈70%)
- **Family Migration Program:** 52,460 places (≈30%)
- **Special Eligibility:** 300 places

# Key Observations from the 2026–27 Planning Levels

The biggest change is in the **Employer Sponsored** category, which has increased from **44,000 to 58,040 places**. This shows a stronger focus on employers hiring skilled workers and helping them move to permanent residency faster.

On the other hand, the **Regional visa (subclass 491)** allocation has dropped significantly from **33,000 to 14,110 places**. This means less emphasis on provisional regional migration compared to previous years.

The **Skilled Independent** category has increased from **16,900 to 21,090 places**, which is a positive sign for high-skilled applicants who do not need employer or state sponsorship.

The **State nomination program (subclass 190)** has slightly increased from **33,000 to 35,500 places**, which shows that states will still play an important role in selecting migrants based on local job demand.

The **Talent and Innovation** stream has reduced from **5,300 to 3,500 places**, which shows a more selective approach towards very high-level global talent.

Overall, the 2026–27 program is not about increasing numbers, but about shifting priority towards employer-sponsored and onshore applicants already working and contributing in Australia.



# What Applicants Should Consider Now

The 2026–27 migration program planning levels show that Australia is increasingly using migration as a targeted economic and workforce tool rather than maintaining a broad intake model. Allocation trends now indicate a stronger preference towards applicants who already demonstrate labour market value through employer demand, skilled employment, sector shortages, and long-term economic participation potential.

For applicants planning Australia PR, a proper assessment of occupation ceilings, nomination trends, points competitiveness, employer-sponsored eligibility, and state migration requirements can play a critical role in long-term visa outcomes. You can consult a Registered Migration Agent in Australia to strategically evaluate your profile under the current Migration Program framework.
