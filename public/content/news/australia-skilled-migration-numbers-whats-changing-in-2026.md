Australia's Skilled Migration Program has once again entered the spotlight, with recent statistics fuelling debate among policymakers, employers, migration professionals, and skilled visa applicants about the reality behind Australia's skilled migration numbers.

At the centre of the discussion is a simple but important question:

Is Australia's Skilled Migration Program bringing in as many genuinely skilled migrants as the public believes, or are the numbers being interpreted differently?

The debate follows a recent analysis by the Institute of Public Affairs (IPA), which argues that Australia's skilled migration figures may not accurately reflect the number of migrants who personally meet Australia's skilled migration requirements.

**Here's what you need to know.**

## **Why Australia's Skilled Migration Numbers Are Being Debated**

According to the Department of Home Affairs' 2024–25 Migration Program data analysed by the IPA:

- Australia granted 185,001 permanent migration visas during the 2024–25 financial year. Among them, 132,148 visas (71%) were allocated under the Skilled Migration Stream. However, only 60,474 visa holders individually satisfied Australia's skills requirements.
- This means the remaining visas within the Skilled Stream were granted to secondary applicants, who are typically the dependants of skilled migrants, including spouses, de facto partners, and dependent children included in the primary applicant's visa application.

## **Understanding Primary and Secondary Applicants**

Primary applicants are individuals who personally qualify for a skilled visa by meeting Australia's eligibility requirements, such as having an eligible occupation, a positive skills assessment, meeting English language standards, and satisfying visa-specific requirements.

While Secondary applicants are eligible family members, typically a spouse, partner, or dependent children who are included in the primary applicant's visa application. They are generally not required to complete a separate skills assessment but must still meet Australia's health and character requirements.

Importantly, both primary and secondary applicants are granted the same visa subclass and are counted under Australia's Skilled Migration Program in official statistics.

## **What Is the Institute of Public Affairs Claiming?**

The IPA argues that [Australia's Skilled Migration Program](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/general-skilled-visa/) can be misunderstood because the headline figures include both skilled workers and their accompanying family members.

**According to the research:**

- Since data collection began in 2012, there has not been a single year in which primary skilled migrants made up the majority of visas granted under the Skilled Migration Stream.
- Over the five years to June 2025, 307,575 permanent visas were granted to secondary applicants within the Skilled stream.
- During the same period, total family and dependent visa approvals reached 594,470, including those granted under both the Family stream and the Skilled stream.

## **Why Transparency in Skilled Migration Matters**

Australia's Skilled Migration Program plays a central role in addressing critical labour shortages, supporting economic growth, and meeting the country's long-term workforce needs. The number of skilled migrants is often used to shape the future migration policy, workforce planning, and broader public discourse.

The focus of this debate is on how skilled migration outcomes are reported. Critics argue that official statistics should more clearly distinguish between primary skilled applicants and their dependent family members to provide greater transparency and a more accurate understanding of the skilled workforce entering Australia.
