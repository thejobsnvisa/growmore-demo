Good news for skilled migrants who are waiting for Tasmania state nomination opportunities. The state has announced that it has been allocated 2,050 nomination places for the 2026–27 program year under its Skilled Migration State Nomination Program. This creates fresh opportunities for eligible applicants who may have missed their chance in the 2025–26 program year or are planning their Australian permanent residency pathway through state nomination.

Below is a breakdown of Tasmania's nomination allocations and important details for the 2026–27 program year.

## **Tasmania's 2026–27 State Nomination Allocations**

**Tasmania has been allocated a total of 2,050 nomination places, including:**

- Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190): 1,250 places
- Skilled Work Regional Visa (Subclass 491): 800 places

These nomination places are available for eligible skilled workers who meet Tasmania's state nomination requirements. One thing to note is that the 2026–27 allocation is slightly higher than the 1,850 nomination places allocated to Tasmania in the 2025–26 program year, which increases opportunities for skilled migrants.

## **Tasmania Skilled Migration State Nomination Program to Reopen Soon**

Migration Tasmania has confirmed that the Skilled Migration State Nomination Program will reopen to new Registrations of Interest (ROIs) in the coming weeks.

So, if you are planning to apply for the Subclass 190 (Skilled Nominated Visa) or Subclass 491 (Skilled Work Regional Visa), it is important to understand the updated nomination criteria before submitting your ROI. Make sure to review the 2026–27 program requirements and eligibility criteria once they are announced to stay informed of any changes from previous program years.

## **What If Your Nomination Application Is Still Being Processed?**

If you have already lodged a Tasmania state nomination application and are still waiting for an outcome, there is no need to reapply at this stage.

Migration Tasmania has confirmed that pending applications will continue to be assessed based on the requirements that were applicable when they were submitted. If it is approved, successful applicants will be nominated through SkillSelect.

This clears things for applicants who submitted their applications before the commencement of the new program year.

## **Important Update for Subclass 491 Applicants Seeking Subclass 190 Nomination**

Migration Tasmania has also clarified an important point for current Subclass 491 applicants.

If you applied for Subclass 491 in the 2025–26 program year but did not receive an invitation and now wish to apply for Subclass 190, your existing application will not be automatically considered for Subclass 190.

You will need to withdraw your current Subclass 491 nomination application and submit a new Registration of Interest (ROI) for Subclass 190 once the 2026–27 program opens.

Please note that submitting a new ROI does not guarantee an invitation. Nominations will depend on the availability of nomination places, your eligibility, and overall competitiveness.

## **What Does This Mean for Skilled Migrants?**

The increase in Tasmania's nomination places is positive news for skilled migrants who are planning to apply for Subclass 190 or Subclass 491 visas. However, state nomination remains competitive, so meeting the eligibility requirements alone may not be enough. Applicants should also ensure they have a strong and well-prepared application.

As there is time before Tasmania reopens its upcoming state nomination program, applicants are encouraged to make sure their skills assessment is valid, their Expression of Interest (EOI) is up to date, and all supporting documents are ready. Being prepared early can help you take advantage of the nomination opportunities when the program opens.
