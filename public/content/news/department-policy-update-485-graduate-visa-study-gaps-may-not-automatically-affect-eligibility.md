There is an important clarification for international graduates planning to rely on more than one qualification to meet the Australian Study Requirement for the Subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa. 

Earlier policy guidance from the Department of Home Affairs indicated that where an applicant relies on multiple courses to satisfy the Subclass 485 study requirement, each subsequent course should generally commence in the same or following academic year. 

This raised concerns for graduates who had a longer gap between their courses and whether such a gap could prevent them from satisfying the 485 study requirement. 

Following enquiries by the Migration Institute of Australia (MIA), the Department has now clarified how this policy is intended to operate. 

## The Key Clarification 

According to the Department, the requirement for subsequent courses to generally commence in the same or following academic year is guidance for visa decision-makers – it is not an absolute requirement in every case. 

This means that having a gap between two courses does not automatically prevent an applicant from combining those qualifications to satisfy the Subclass 485 Australian Study Requirement. 

Instead, applications are assessed based on their individual circumstances, including: 

* the length and circumstances of any gap between courses; 
* the applicant’s overall study pathway; 
* the qualifications being relied upon; 
* any approved deferment or break in studies; and 
* the evidence available to the decision-maker. 

For example, where a break in study was approved by an education provider due to compassionate or compelling circumstances, this may be relevant when the Department assesses the applicant’s overall study pathway. 

## Important Exception – Graduate Diplomas 

Applicants should be aware that graduate diploma qualifications are treated differently. 

The Department has confirmed that specific timing requirements apply to relevant graduate diploma qualifications under clause 485.111(b)(ii). 

Therefore, applicants relying on a graduate diploma should not assume that the broader flexibility regarding gaps between courses will necessarily apply to them. 

## What Does This Mean for 485 Applicants? 

The clarification is significant because a study gap, by itself, does not necessarily mean that multiple qualifications cannot be used to meet the Australian Study Requirement. 

However, this should not be interpreted as meaning that every combination of courses, regardless of the gap between them, will qualify. 

The Department will still assess whether the applicant satisfies the legislative requirements for the Subclass 485 visa, taking into account the particular study history and supporting evidence. 

If you have completed multiple courses in Australia, had a gap between your studies, changed courses, deferred your studies, or are unsure whether your qualifications can be combined for the Subclass 485 visa, it is important to assess your circumstances carefully before applying.
