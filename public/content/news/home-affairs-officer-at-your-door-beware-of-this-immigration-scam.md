The Australian Department of Home Affairs has recently issued a warning about a new immigration scam in which criminals are pretending to be Home Affairs officers and approaching people at their homes.

It has been discovered that these scammers use forged identification documents to conduct fake government checks and attempt to obtain personal information, biometric data, or money.

So, if you are an Australian visa applicant, temporary resident, or someone who has an ongoing visa application with the Department of Home Affairs, it is important to stay alert.

Read this full news article to understand how this scam works and what to do to protect yourself from becoming a victim of this type of Australian immigration fraud.

## **How Are Fake Home Affairs Officers Targeting People?**

According to the Department of Home Affairs, scammers are approaching people at their homes and pretending that they are government officials by presenting fake documents or identification. They convince residents that they are conducting official immigration-related checks.

**During these interactions, scammers may attempt to collect:**

- Personal information or identity documents
- Passport, visa, or other immigration-related details
- Biometric information, including fingerprints or photographs
- Money or financial details

These collected details can be misused for identity theft, financial fraud, or other illegal activities.

## **Genuine Home Affairs Officers Do Not Collect Biometrics at Your Home**

One of the most important points highlighted by the Department of Home Affairs is that genuine Home Affairs officers do not visit people’s homes to collect biometric information, such as fingerprints or photographs.

Whatever biometric information is required for Australian visa applications is collected through authorised processes and approved channels. Applicants should always verify any unexpected request before sharing sensitive information.

If someone arrives at your home claiming to represent Home Affairs and asks for your personal documents, biometric information, or payment, it should be treated as a warning sign.

## **What Should You Do If Someone Claims to Be From Home Affairs?**

**If you receive an unexpected visit from someone who claims to be a Home Affairs officer, you should take the following steps:**

- **Do Not Share Personal Information:** Avoid providing your passport, visa documents, identity details, or any other personal information.
- **Do Not Provide Money or Financial Details:** Home Affairs does not ask people to make unexpected payments during unverified home visits. Do not share your bank details, credit card information, or transfer money to unknown individuals.
- **Do Not Allow Unknown Individuals Into Your Home:** You are not required to provide access to your home to someone who is claiming to be a government representative without proper verification.
- **Report Suspicious Activity:** If you believe you have encountered a scammer or become a victim of a scam, make sure to report it to Scamwatch.

**_Here is the website to report a scam:_** **[https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam)**

## **Why Immigration Scams Target Visa Applicants and Migrants**

Immigration scams often target people because criminals take advantage of concerns around visa status, residency, citizenship applications, or future migration plans.

Scammers may create urgency by threatening visa cancellation, legal action, or immigration consequences to pressure individuals into sharing information or making payments.

## **How to Stay Safe During Your Australian Visa Journey**

Whether you are planning to migrate to Australia, applying for a visa, or already living in Australia, following these steps can help protect you:

- Communicate only through official Department of Home Affairs channels.
- Verify any unexpected calls, emails, or visits claiming to be from government authorities.
- Avoid sharing immigration documents with unknown individuals.
- Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed visa approval.
- Seek advice only from authorised migration professionals.

A genuine immigration professional will provide clear advice based on your circumstances and will never guarantee a visa outcome.
