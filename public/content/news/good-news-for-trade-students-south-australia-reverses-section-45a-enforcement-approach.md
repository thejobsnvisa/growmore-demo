Students planning to study a trade course in South Australia have received welcome news, with the South Australian Skills Commission reversing its enforcement approach towards institution-based trade training outside approved training contracts.

In official correspondence dated **27 October 2026**, the Commission confirmed that Registered Training Organisations (RTOs) delivering this training in declared trades will not face enforcement action under Section 45A of the *South Australian Skills Act 2008* in relation to that training.

The revised position applies to both new and continuing institutional trade training enrolments.

## What Has Changed in South Australia's Trade Training Rules?

The South Australian Skills Commission has revised its enforcement approach towards institution-based trade training delivered outside approved apprenticeship training contracts.

Previously, the Commission’s enforcement approach targeted colleges delivering declared trade training outside approved apprenticeship training contracts. This created a significant barrier for providers offering college-based trade courses and uncertainty for students considering those courses.

The Commission has now confirmed that it will not take Section 45A enforcement action against RTOs in relation to that training.

It has also withdrawn mandatory reporting requirements introduced specifically for Section 45A enforcement, including:

- Relevant outstanding monthly reports.
- Quarterly AVETMISS NAT files.
- Statutory declarations.

## Why Is This Positive for Students?

The reversal removes a significant enforcement barrier affecting providers offering institution-based trade training in South Australia.

For prospective students, this provides greater certainty when exploring college-based trade courses delivered outside an approved apprenticeship training contract. Students should confirm course availability and enrolment requirements directly with their chosen provider.

For students already enrolled, the announcement offers reassurance because the revised position expressly covers continuing enrolments as well.

## Stronger Support for International Students

The Commission will also strengthen awareness of and access to its independent complaint-handling, advocacy, mediation and intervention services for international VET students.

These services assist students experiencing difficulties with:

- Training providers.
- Course progression.
- Practical training arrangements.
- Enrolment agreements.
- Fees and refunds.

## Does This Change Visa or Trade Recognition Requirements?

The announcement reverses the Commission’s enforcement approach; it does not announce the repeal of Section 45A.

It also does not automatically change occupational licensing, migration skills assessment or visa requirements.

Completing a trade course does not, by itself, guarantee:

- Recognition as a qualified tradesperson.
- A positive skills assessment.
- Permanent residency in Australia.

Students should check their provider’s registration, CRICOS registration where applicable, practical training arrangements and how the course supports their intended employment and migration pathway.
