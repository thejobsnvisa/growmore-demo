Some occupations in Australia could have different classifications, titles or skill levels under the proposed 2027 update, as the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) has opened consultation on the draft 2027 update to the Occupation Standard Classification for Australia (OSCA). This gives stakeholders a final opportunity to provide feedback before the updated classification is released. 

The proposed changes may be relevant to employers, industry bodies and professionals who rely on Australia's occupation classification system. However, it is important to understand that the proposed OSCA update does not currently change Australia's skilled migration occupation lists. 

## What Is the 2027 OSCA Update? 

The 2027 update is intended to reflect changes in Australia's occupations and the way different roles are classified. 

The draft includes changes based on feedback received during two earlier consultation rounds conducted in 2025 and earlier in 2026. 

The proposed changes include: 

* new occupations being added to the classification; 
* updates to occupation titles and specialisations; 
* changes to lead statements and main tasks; 
* updates to licensing or registration requirements; and 
* changes to skill levels for some occupations. 

In cases where the scope of an occupation has changed significantly, existing occupation codes may also be retired and new codes issued. 

## Consultation Period 

The ABS consultation is open from 17 August to 18 September 2026. 

This is expected to be the final opportunity for stakeholders to provide feedback before the updated OSCA classification is officially released in March 2027. 

Businesses, industry representatives and other stakeholders who are affected by occupational classifications can review the proposed changes and provide their feedback during this consultation period. 

Stakeholders and interested parties can review the draft classification and submit feedback through the ABS Consultation Hub: 

[Review the draft 2027 OSCA update here.](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/consultation-draft-occupation-standard-classification-australia-osca/aug-2026)

## Does This Change Australia's Skilled Migration Occupation Lists? 

No, not under this consultation. 

This is an important point for skilled migrants. The OSCA classification is currently used by the ABS for statistical purposes, while Australia's skilled migration occupation lists continue to rely on ANZSCO. 

Therefore, the proposed 2027 OSCA changes should not be treated as changes to Australia's skilled migration occupation lists or visa eligibility requirements. 

Any changes to migration occupation lists would need to be separately announced by the relevant Australian Government department. 

## What Does the OSCA Update Mean for Skilled Migrants? 

For skilled migrants, an OSCA change does not automatically mean a change to their visa options. Even if an occupation is renamed, reclassified, added or removed under the updated classification, this does not automatically mean the occupation will be added to or removed from Australia's skilled migration pathways. 

If you are planning to apply for a skilled visa, what matters is the occupation list and eligibility requirements that apply to your specific visa at the time you apply. 

The updated OSCA, expected to be released in March 2027, will give a clearer picture of how occupations are classified. However, OSCA itself does not determine which occupations are eligible for skilled migration. 

## What to Expect from the OSCA Update 

The consultation remains open until 18 September 2026, giving stakeholders a final opportunity to comment on the proposed changes. 

After the consultation period, the ABS is expected to finalise the update and officially release the revised OSCA classification in March 2027.
