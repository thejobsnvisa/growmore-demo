If you are an international student in Australia and were planning to study two courses at the same time, this new rule could affect what you are able to enrol in.

The Department of Education has removed the concurrent study functionality from the Provider Registration and International Student Management System (PRISMS). In simple terms, education providers can no longer use PRISMS to create new concurrent enrolments for students who have not yet completed 12 months of their principal course.

This change is part of Australia's broader tightening of international student transfer and enrolment rules under **Standard 7 of the National Code**.

## What exactly has changed?

Previously, registered education providers could use the PRISMS concurrent study functionality to manage concurrent enrolments where the relevant requirements were met.

That functionality has now been removed.

As a result, providers will not be able to concurrently enrol students who have not completed the required 12 months of their principal course.

If you are thinking, **"Does this mean students already studying two courses have to stop?"**, the answer is no.

The Department has confirmed that the change does not affect students who already hold concurrent enrolments.

However, their providers must continue monitoring those arrangements. The student needs to be able to:

* Maintain attendance in both courses; and
* Make satisfactory progress and complete both courses within the period covered by their Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE).

So, this is mainly a change affecting **new concurrent enrolments**, not an automatic cancellation of existing ones.

## What is a principal course?

The principal course is generally the main or highest-level course in a student's packaged study program.

For example, a student might have a study package that includes ELICOS, followed by a Certificate III and then a Diploma. In that situation, the **Diploma would generally be the principal course** because it is the highest AQF-level qualification and the final course in the package.

This means you need to look at your **overall study package** rather than simply counting 12 months from the first course you started.

## Why is Australia tightening these rules?

The change needs to be understood alongside Australia's rules on student transfers between education providers.

Under **Standard 7 of the National Code**, international students face restrictions when trying to transfer from one registered provider to another during the early part of their principal course.

The purpose is not simply to stop students from changing courses. The rules are designed to make sure transfers are properly assessed and are in the student's best interests.

The **Australian Skills Quality Authority (ASQA)** has also identified concerns around onshore student transfers and concurrent enrolments that may not meet educational or industry expectations.

For providers, this means there is greater focus on whether their enrolment and transfer practices are actually complying with the National Code.

## The 12-month transfer restriction is also important

There is another major detail international students should be aware of.

From **2 October 2026**, the transfer restriction for non-school sector international students has temporarily increased from **6 months to 12 months**.

This means a registered provider generally cannot knowingly enrol a student transferring from another registered provider before the student has completed **12 months of their principal course**, unless an exception under Standard 7 applies.

This temporary 12-month restriction applies from **2 October 2026 until 30 June 2027**.

From **1 July 2027**, the transfer restriction for non-school sector students is scheduled to return to **6 months**.

So, if you are considering changing providers during this period, do not treat it as a simple change of college or university. You may need to follow the formal transfer and release process first.

## Need help understanding how this affects your visa?

The important point to consider is that Australia has **not suddenly banned students from studying more than one course**.

The change is that **new concurrent enrolments are now restricted for students who have not completed 12 months of their principal course**, while the temporary transfer restriction has also increased from **6 months to 12 months**.

So, if you are planning to enrol in another course or change providers, make sure you understand the rules before making any decision.
