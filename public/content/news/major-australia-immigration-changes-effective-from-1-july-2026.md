With the start of Australia's new financial year on 1 July 2026, several important immigration changes have also officially taken effect. From increased application and tribunal fees to revised income thresholds for employer-sponsored visas, these annual changes could impact both current and future visa applicants.

These updates affect different stages of the migration journey, from lodging a visa application to seeking a review of a decision or applying under an employer-sponsored pathway.

**Here's a detailed look at the major Australian immigration changes that have taken effect from 1 July.**

## 1. **Visa Application Charges (VAC) to Be Updated**

Australia updates its Visa Application Charges (VACs) every year in line with the Consumer Price Index (CPI), and this year, the country has also updated the fee structure that applies to applications lodged on or after 1 July 2026.

The revised charges apply to a broad range of Australian visa subclasses, including skilled migration, employer-sponsored, partner, parent, student, visitor, and temporary graduate visas.

**Learn more about these [australia visa application fees increase from 1-july-2026](https://www.growmore.one/news/australia-visa-application-fees-increase-from-1-july-2026/)**

## 2.  **Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) Fees Increase**

Applicants seeking a review of a migration decision through the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) will now pay higher application fees.

**From 1 July 2026, the new ART fees are:**

- Migration decision review (Part 5): $3,727 (previously $3,580)
- Protection visa decision review (Part 7): $2,293 (previously $2,203)

**Learn more about [ART Fee Hike From 1-july-2026](https://www.growmore.one/news/art-fee-hike-from-1-july-2026-new-migration-and-review-application-charges/)**

## 3. **Federal Circuit Court Fees Also Increase**

Applicants challenging a migration decision through the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia will also face higher filing fees.

**From 1 July 2026, the new charges are:**

- Standard application fee: $4,180 (up from $4,015)
- Reduced application fee (where eligible): $2,090 (up from $2,005)

## 4. **Fair Work High Income Threshold (FWHIT) increase**

From 1 July 2026, Australia's Fair Work High Income Threshold (FWHIT) has increased **from AUD 183,100 to AUD 190,100 per year**. This change is particularly important for applicants applying for the Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) visa under the age exemption pathway. 

Applicants relying on this exemption who meet the under-45 age requirement must now demonstrate earnings above the new AUD 190,100 threshold for the relevant qualifying period.

## 5. **Annual Indexation of the CSIT and SSIT**

Australia has increased the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT) and Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT) for the Subclass 482 Skills in Demand (SID) visa and Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) visa.

**The updated income thresholds for the 2026–27 program year are:**

- Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT): AUD 79,499 (up from AUD 76,515)
- Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT): AUD 146,717 (up from AUD 141,210)

These thresholds are automatically indexed each year based on Average Weekly Ordinary Time Earnings (AWOTE) and apply to all relevant Subclass 482 and Subclass 186 visa applications lodged on or after 1 July 2026.

**Read the full information about [Salary Thresholds Rise for Employer-Sponsored Visas](https://www.growmore.one/news/salary-thresholds-rise-for-employer-sponsored-visas-from-1-july-2026/)**

## 6. **Goldfields DAMA Transitions to the Western Australian DAMA**

The Goldfields Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) officially ended on 30 June 2026. From 1 July 2026, employers in the Goldfields region can instead access the Western Australian DAMA (WA DAMA). Businesses in the Goldfields now follow the eligibility requirements, occupations, and concessions available under the WA DAMA from 1 July 2026 onwards.

## 7.  **Pacific Engagement Visa – 2026-27 Ballot**

The 2026–27 Pacific Engagement Visa (PEV) ballot also opens on 1 July 2026 and remains open until 29 July 2026.

Applicants should note that this is a new ballot, and registrations submitted in previous years are not carried over. Anyone wishing to participate must submit a new registration during the 2026–27 ballot period.

**The participating countries for the 2026–27 PEV ballot are:**

- Federated States of Micronesia
- Fiji
- Kiribati
- Palau
- Papua New Guinea
- Republic of the Marshall Islands
- Samoa
- Solomon Islands
- Timor-Leste
- Tonga
- Tuvalu

**Read the [full information about the ballot](https://www.growmore.one/news/australia-announces-new-visa-ballot-for-pacific-region/)**

## 8.  **2026–27 Migration Program Planning Levels**

In the new program year, the Australian Government confirms the 2026–27 permanent Migration Program at 185,000 places.

Of the total places, 129,590 are allocated to onshore applicants already living in Australia, while the remaining 55,110 places are reserved for offshore skilled migrants.
