Victoria is preparing to open its 2026–27 Skilled Migration Program, but there is one change applicants should be aware of before applying: submitting a Registration of Interest (ROI) will now come with a fee. This is a change from the 2025–26 program, when applicants could submit an ROI without paying a fee to the Victorian Government.

Victoria has also announced fees for nomination applications under the subclass 190 and subclass 491 visa pathways. So, if you are planning to apply for state nomination in the upcoming program year, here is a breakdown of the fees.

## Victoria Skilled Migration 2026–27: ROI and Nomination Fees

The Victorian Government has announced the following fees for the 2026–27 program year:

| Application type | Fee excluding GST | Fee including GST |
|---|---:|---:|
| Registration of Interest (ROI) | $60 | $66 |
| Subclass 190 nomination application | $180 | $198 |
| Subclass 491 nomination application | $180 | $198 |

A 10% Goods and Services Tax (GST) applies if the applicant is in Australia at the time of payment. The applicable GST is calculated and added when the application form is completed.

## When Do You Need to Pay the Fees?

Not every fee is payable at the same stage of the process. Applicants should understand the following conditions before submitting their applications:

- **ROI fee:** The $60 fee, excluding GST, is charged once per program year when submitting a Registration of Interest.
- **Submitting a new ROI:** If you withdraw your ROI and submit another one within the same program year, you will not be charged the ROI fee again.
- **Subclass 190 nomination fee:** The $180 fee, excluding GST, is payable only if you receive an invitation to apply for nomination and submit the nomination application.
- **Subclass 491 nomination fee:** The same $180 fee, excluding GST, applies when an invited applicant submits a subclass 491 nomination application.

These are fees for Victoria's state nomination process. They are separate from the visa application charges payable to the Australian Department of Home Affairs.

## How Does Victoria Select Applicants for Nomination?

Alongside the fee announcement, Victoria has published more detailed information explaining how Registrations of Interest are selected for invitations to apply for state nomination.

The selection process considers factors outlined in the Victorian Points Test (VPT), including the weighting assigned to different selection factors. Victoria has also provided information about priority industries and any additional industry factors considered when selecting applicants for nomination invitations.

## Do Existing ROIs Remain Valid for 2026–27?

If you submitted a Registration of Interest during a previous program year and want to be considered for Victorian nomination in 2026–27, you must log in to the Live in Melbourne portal and submit a new ROI when the program opens.

An ROI from a previous program year will not automatically carry forward for consideration under the 2026–27 program.

## When Will Victoria's 2026–27 Skilled Migration Program Open?

Victoria has stated that further information about the 2026–27 program will be published on the Live in Melbourne website when available. So, applicants should check the official website for updates on the program opening, nomination arrangements and application requirements.
