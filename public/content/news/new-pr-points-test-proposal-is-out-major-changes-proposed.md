Australia’s skilled migration points test continues to attract calls for major reform.

Following earlier proposals from the **Grattan Institute**, another detailed model for redesigning Australia’s points-tested skilled migration system has now been released — this time by researchers from the **Australian National University (ANU)**.

The March 2026 working paper, *Designing points-tested visas for Australia using Mincer regression models*, was prepared by **Peter Varela and Robert Breunig** from the Tax and Transfer Policy Institute at ANU’s Crawford School of Public Policy.

Importantly, **this is not a new Australian Government points test and no migration law has changed**.

However, the paper is particularly interesting because the authors state that they benefited substantially from comments from the **Australian Treasury and Department of Home Affairs** while developing the research.

## What Is Being Proposed?

The researchers argue that Australia’s points test should focus more strongly on selecting **younger migrants with stronger long-term earning potential and proven Australian employment outcomes**.

Their benchmark model produces some major differences from the current points test:

| Factor | Current Points | Proposed Benchmark |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Age 18–24** | 25 | **65** |
| **Age 25–32** | 30 | **65** |
| **Age 33–39** | 25 | **45** |
| **Age 40–45** | 15 | **15** |
| **Superior English** | 20 | **20** |
| **Proficient English** | 10 | **10** |
| **Competent English** | 0 | **0** |
| **PhD** | 25 | **35** |
| **Master’s** | 20 | **15** |
| **Bachelor’s** | 20 | **15** |
| **Diploma/VET** | 15 | **0** |
| **Australian experience – 0–1 year** | 0 | **0** |
| **Australian experience – over 1 year** | 5–20 | **15** |
| **International work experience** | Up to 15 | **0** |
| **Regional study** | 5 | **0** |
| **Professional Year** | 5 | **0** |
| **Community language / NAATI** | 5 | **0** |
| **Partner language** | 10 | **0** |
| **Specialist education** | 5 | **0** |

The proposed benchmark therefore places **far greater weight on younger applicants and PhD qualifications**, while removing points for several categories commonly used by skilled migrants to increase their points.

The researchers argue that **community language, regional study, Professional Year and specialist qualifications** are not sufficiently predictive of stronger migrant economic outcomes.

Australian work experience, on the other hand, receives greater importance. The research found that migrants with previous Australian work experience earned around **15% more**, all else being equal, and were more likely to work in their nominated occupation.

## Could Your Australian Salary Become Part of the PR Points Test?

Perhaps the most significant idea in the paper is to consider an applicant’s **actual income earned in Australia while holding a temporary visa** when selecting migrants for permanent residence.

One illustrative income model contained in the paper proposes:

| Australian Income | Possible Points |
|---|---:|
| Less than $80,000 | **0** |
| $80,000–$100,000 | **20** |
| $100,000–$125,000 | **40** |
| Above $125,000 | **60** |

The idea is that instead of relying only on qualifications, English, experience and other characteristics to predict whether someone will succeed economically after receiving PR, Australia could consider **how that person is already performing in the Australian labour market**.

This could also benefit some high-earning skilled workers who may not necessarily hold university qualifications.

## Three Possible Models for Australia’s Future Points Test

The researchers ultimately consider three broad options:

**Option 1:** A redesigned points test focused primarily on human-capital characteristics such as age, education, English and experience.

**Option 2:** A similar points test, but with additional points based on the applicant’s **nominated occupation**.

**Option 3:** A broader system where temporary migrants could qualify for permanent migration based on their **actual Australian income**, alongside a human-capital pathway.

The researchers identify the third approach as their **preferred model** because they believe it would select migrants with stronger economic outcomes.

## Is Australia Changing the Points Test?

**Not yet.**

This is an **ANU research working paper**, not legislation, a Ministerial announcement or a Department of Home Affairs policy change.

However, following the Grattan Institute’s earlier proposal, we are now seeing **another substantial proposal calling for Australia’s skilled migration points test to be redesigned**.

The direction is particularly important for prospective migrants.

If future reforms follow this type of model, **age, Australian employment, occupation and actual salary could become increasingly important**, while familiar ways of increasing migration points — including **NAATI, Professional Year, regional study, overseas work experience and some partner-related points — could lose importance or potentially disappear.**

For now, **Australia’s existing points test continues to apply**.
