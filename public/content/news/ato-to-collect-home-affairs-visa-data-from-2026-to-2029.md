The ATO is set to receive selected visa and migration information from the Department of Home Affairs under a new data-matching program. Published through a Gazette notice on 21 September 2026, the program will run for three financial years from 2026–27 to 2028–29 and forms part of the ATO’s broader tax and superannuation compliance activities.

The ATO estimates that records relating to approximately 9 million individuals will be obtained each financial year. Here is what the ATO’s visa data-matching program means for visa holders, sponsors and migration agents.

## What Is the ATO’s Visa Data-Matching Program?

The ATO is introducing a data-matching program under which it will obtain visa and migration-related information from the Department of Home Affairs.

The purpose is to compare relevant information held by Home Affairs with taxation and superannuation information available to the ATO. This can help the ATO identify potential discrepancies, understand compliance risks and determine where further educational or compliance action may be appropriate.

The program will operate across the 2026–27 to 2028–29 financial years.

## What Visa Information Will the ATO Receive?

The data covered by the program is broader than basic visa status information.

According to the Gazette notice, the information may include:

- Address history of visa applicants and sponsors
- Contact history of visa applicants and sponsors
- Address history of migration agents
- Contact history of migration agents
- History of visas granted
- A person's visa status at a particular point in time
- Information identifying migration agents who prepared or assisted with visa applications
- International travel movements, including arrivals and departures
- Sponsor details relating to Subclass 457 visas
- Visa subclass information
- Education provider information relating to student visa holders

## Why Is the ATO Collecting Visa Data?

The ATO has outlined several objectives for the program, with a major focus on tax and superannuation compliance.

The data-matching program is intended to help the ATO:

- Check whether individuals and businesses are meeting their tax and superannuation reporting obligations
- Improve understanding of tax and superannuation risks involving visa holders, sponsors and migration agents
- Encourage voluntary compliance
- Develop appropriate compliance and educational strategies
- Identify potential fraud and emerging methods of exploiting the visa framework
- Test and improve risk detection models
- Identify potentially ineligible ABN holders and support ABN cancellation
- Support compliance activities under Australia's foreign investment rules

The ATO will therefore use the information as part of its broader compliance and risk-management activities.

## How Does the Program Address Privacy?

The Gazette notice states that the program follows the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner's Guidelines on Data Matching in Australian Government Administration.

These guidelines provide standards for using data matching as an administrative tool in a way that:

- Complies with the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) and the Privacy Act 1988
- Is consistent with good privacy practice

The ATO also provides information about its privacy practices and data-matching programs.

## What Does This Mean for Visa Holders, Sponsors and Migration Agents?

The program does not introduce a new visa subclass or change the eligibility criteria for Australian visas. Instead, it allows the ATO to use selected Home Affairs information as part of its tax, superannuation, fraud detection and broader compliance activities.

For visa holders, sponsors and migration agents, this makes it important to ensure that information provided to government agencies is accurate and that relevant tax, superannuation and reporting obligations are properly maintained.
