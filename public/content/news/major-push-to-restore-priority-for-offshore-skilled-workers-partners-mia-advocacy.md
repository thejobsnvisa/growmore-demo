Australia’s latest visa priority changes are raising concerns among skilled workers waiting offshore and Australians hoping to reunite with their partners overseas. The changes have deprioritised highly skilled offshore workers and offshore partners of Australians, according to the Migration Institute of Australia (MIA).

The MIA is now calling on the Australian Government to restore greater priority for highly skilled offshore workers and partners of Australians. The call comes after changes introduced through **Ministerial Directions 117 and 119 in July 2026**, which changed the processing priorities for certain skilled and family visa applicants.

## Why Are Offshore Applicants Facing Concern?

The issue comes down to **visa processing priority**.

Ministerial Directions determine which visa applications are processed ahead of others when the Department of Home Affairs has a large number of applications to assess. They do not change the basic eligibility requirements for a visa, but they can significantly affect how quickly an eligible application may be processed.

Under the current arrangements, certain offshore applicants have been placed at a lower processing priority.

For Australian employers, particularly businesses operating in regional areas, this has raised concerns about accessing skilled workers from overseas when suitable Australian workers cannot be found.

The MIA says businesses have already invested money and resources into overseas recruitment and sponsorship, only to face greater uncertainty over when those workers may be able to move to Australia.

## Partner Visa Applicants Also Caught in the Priority Shift

The concern is not limited to employers.

Australians with partners living overseas could also face longer waits under the current family visa processing priorities.

For offshore Partner visa applicants, including applications under the **Partner (subclass 309/100)** pathway, lower processing priority can mean a longer period of separation while the application is waiting to be assessed.

The MIA has warned that the existing backlog could make the wait particularly difficult for Australian citizens and eligible sponsors who are trying to reunite with their partners.

For families, this is more than a processing-time issue. It can mean months or potentially years of uncertainty about when they can live together in Australia.

## What Is the MIA Asking the Government to Do?

The MIA is calling on the Government to amend **Ministerial Directions 117 and 119** and restore priority for:

- Highly skilled offshore workers needed by Australian businesses
- Offshore partners of Australians

The organisation argues that Australia can continue managing migration levels through other measures without significantly disadvantaging businesses that rely on skilled overseas workers or families separated by borders.

## Will the Priority Settings Change?

At this stage, there is **no confirmed government decision to reverse the current arrangements**.

The latest development is the renewed industry call for change. Unless the Government amends or replaces the relevant Ministerial Directions, the existing processing priorities continue to apply.

This means offshore applicants should not assume that their visa application is no longer viable. Instead, they need to understand how the current priority framework applies to their specific visa subclass, location and circumstances.

## What Should Offshore Applicants Do Now?

If you are an offshore skilled worker or applying for a Partner visa, the current priority settings are an important factor to consider when planning your Australian immigration pathway.

However, **priority is not the same as eligibility**. A lower processing priority does not automatically mean a visa will be refused. It primarily affects where an application sits in the Department’s processing queue.

Applicants should therefore focus on submitting a properly prepared application and ensuring that all relevant eligibility and supporting evidence requirements are met.
