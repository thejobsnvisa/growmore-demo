If you are planning to study Southern Cross University’s Graduate Diploma of Teaching (GDoT) and your goal is to become a registered teacher in Western Australia, there’s an important deadline you need to know about: **30 June 2027**.

The Teacher Registration Board of Western Australia (TRBWA) has confirmed that students who enrol in the GDoT by **30 June 2027** will be recognised for the **Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher)** pathway in WA after meeting the registration requirements. However, students who enrol after 30 June 2027 will not receive the same recognition.

So, if WA teacher registration is part of your study or future work plan, here is what this deadline means for you and what registration pathways may still be available.

## What Has Changed for SCU Graduate Diploma of Teaching Students?

The TRBWA currently lists Southern Cross University’s Graduate Diploma of Teaching among programs that are recognised for the qualification requirements for Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher).

The Board also states that an applicant can qualify where the program was accredited, or taken to be accredited, at any time during their enrolment. It does not have to remain accredited for the entire period of study.

For SCU’s Graduate Diploma of Teaching (GDoT), the current arrangement means:

* **Enrol by 30 June 2027:** You can be recognised for the WA Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher) pathway, subject to meeting registration requirements.
* **Enrol after 30 June 2027:** You will not receive that same recognition for Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher) in WA.
* **Already enrolled:** Current GDoT students are covered under the recognised arrangement, subject to meeting the applicable requirements.

## Why Is 30 June 2027 Important?

The deadline is particularly important if your plan to work in Australia looks something like this:

**Study at SCU → qualify as a teacher → obtain WA teacher registration → work as a teacher in Western Australia.**

If that is the pathway you are planning to take, enrolling before the deadline can help ensure you remain eligible for Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher).

But there is an important distinction: being recognised for the pathway does **not** mean registration is automatic. You still need to satisfy the TRBWA’s registration requirements, including the requirements applicable to your application and circumstances.

## What If You Enrol After 30 June 2027?

This does not necessarily mean that every GDoT graduate will have no way to seek teacher registration in Western Australia.

According to the information provided by SCU, graduates who enrol after the deadline may be eligible for **Limited Registration**.

Some graduates with previous teaching experience may also potentially qualify for **Provisional Registration (Returning Teacher)**.

However, these are different registration categories from Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher), and eligibility depends on the individual applicant and the requirements of the TRBWA.

## What Is Limited Registration in WA?

Limited Registration is for a specific teaching role where an employer or educational institution nominates a suitably qualified person.

You generally need:

* A job offer
* Relevant skills or qualifications
* English proficiency
* Fit-and-proper approval

The exact requirements depend on the circumstances of the applicant and the registration application.

## What Is Provisional Registration (Returning Teacher)?

This pathway is generally for applicants who are not recent graduates of an accredited Australian teaching program but have an equivalent teaching qualification or sufficient teaching experience.

Applicants generally need **four years of higher education study at Bachelor degree level or higher**, along with meeting the Board’s other registration requirements.

Eligibility depends on the individual applicant and the requirements in force at the time of application.

## Is SCU’s Graduate Diploma of Teaching Recognised in Every Australian State?

**No.**

This is one of the most important points students need to understand before choosing a teaching qualification in Australia.

Teacher registration is regulated separately by each Australian state and territory. A qualification recognised for teacher registration in Western Australia is not automatically treated the same way in every other jurisdiction.

According to the information provided by SCU, the Graduate Diploma of Teaching is currently **not recognised for teacher registration and/or employment in New South Wales, the Australian Capital Territory, Victoria or South Australia**.

In those jurisdictions, students need to complete a **Master of Teaching** for teacher employment.

On the other hand, **Queensland, the Northern Territory and Tasmania** may recognise the Graduate Diploma of Teaching through mutual recognition arrangements.

## What If You Want to Work in NSW, Victoria, SA or the ACT?

If your long-term goal is to teach in **New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia or the Australian Capital Territory**, relying on the SCU GDoT alone may not give you the registration pathway you expect.

If your objective is broader teacher registration and employment across Australia, the **Master of Teaching** may be the more appropriate qualification to consider.

## What About SCU’s Master of Teaching?

The Master of Teaching provides another pathway for students who want to become teachers in Australia.

SCU states that students who subsequently complete its Master of Teaching can apply for **Provisional Registration (Graduate Teacher)** because the Master of Teaching is accredited by NESA.

This can provide a clearer pathway for students who want to keep their options open beyond Western Australia.
