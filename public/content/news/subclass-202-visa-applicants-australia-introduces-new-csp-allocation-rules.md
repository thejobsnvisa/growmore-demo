Australia has introduced new allocation rules for the Subclass 202 (Global Special Humanitarian) Visa under the Community Support Program (CSP). This provides clear guidelines on how humanitarian visa places will be managed for the 2026–27 financial year.

To better manage the Subclass 202 (Global Special Humanitarian) visa program, the Australian Government has introduced new applicant limits through a legislative instrument (LIN 26/035). Under these rules, each Approved Proposing Organisation (APO) will now have a set annual limit on the number of applicants it can propose for this visa pathway.

These changes are important for Subclass 202 visa applicants, as the new APO limits may impact when they can submit their proposals.

## What is the Subclass 202 Global Special Humanitarian Visa?

The Subclass 202 Global Special Humanitarian Visa is a permanent Australian Visa for eligible people who are living outside of Australia and outside of their home country, where they may be affected by serious human rights issues or substantial discrimination.

Under the Community Support Program (CSP), applicants must have a proposal from an Approved Proposing Organisation (APO) at the time of submitting their Subclass 202 visa application.

The CSP is designed to support the resettlement of applicants who are:

- Aged between 18 and 50 years
- Able to demonstrate adequate English language skills
- Likely to become financially self-sufficient within 12 months of arriving in Australia

## New Subclass 202 CSP Applicant Limits for 2026–27

The Australian Government has specified annual applicant limits for each APO responsible for managing CSP proposals.

For the 2026–27 financial year, the approved allocation limits are:

| Approved Proposing Organisation (APO) | Applicant Limit |
|----------------------------------------|----------------:|
| Assyrian Australian Association | 50 |
| Australian Migrant Resource Centre | 50 |
| Australian Refugee Association Inc | 50 |
| Eastern Christian Welfare Australia Ltd | 0 |
| Illawarra Multicultural Services | 50 |
| International Organization for Migration | 75 |
| Multicultural Australia Ltd | 50 |
| Multicultural Community Services Geelong Inc trading as Cultura | 75 |
| Settlement Services International Ltd | 0 |
| Spectrum Migrant Resource Centre | 50 |
| AMES Australia | 75 |

These limits decide how many applicants each APO can support and submit for the Subclass 202 visa in the financial year starting from 1 July 2026.

## How Are Subclass 202 Applicants Counted Under CSP Limits?

The updated rules explain how applicants will be counted under an APO’s annual allocation.

- Applicants will be counted in the financial year they apply for the Subclass 202 visa.
- Both primary and secondary applicants included in the application will count towards the APO’s limit.
- If an APO supports an applicant after the visa application has already been submitted, the applicant will not count towards that APO’s limit.

## Australia Introduces New Priority Criteria for Subclass 202 Visa Applicants

Along with CSP allocation limits, Australia has also introduced the Migration (Specification of Commonwealth Priorities for Subclass 202 (Global Special Humanitarian) Visas) Instrument 2026 (LIN 26/036).

This instrument outlines the Commonwealth priorities for applicants seeking to meet the primary criteria for a Subclass 202 visa.

The purpose of these priorities is to support the CSP as a job-ready humanitarian resettlement program, focusing on applicants who have strong settlement prospects in Australia.

## What Do These Changes Mean for Subclass 202 Visa Applicants?

The latest CSP updates show that Australia continues to focus on a structured and sustainable humanitarian settlement program.

While humanitarian circumstances remain central to Subclass 202 visa applications, applicants applying through the Community Support Program should also meet the following requirements:

- Strong settlement potential
- English communication ability
- Relevant skills and work experience
- A pathway towards employment and financial independence

Applicants should also be aware that available CSP places are limited and managed through approved organisations.
