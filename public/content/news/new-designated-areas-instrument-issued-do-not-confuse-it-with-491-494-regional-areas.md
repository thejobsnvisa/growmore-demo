The Australian Government has released a new **Migration (Designated Areas) Instrument 2026 (LIN 26/004)**. This document identifies particular areas as **“designated areas”** for migration purposes.

The instrument was made on **18 September 2026**, registered on **21 September 2026** and commenced on **22 September 2026**. It replaces the previous **Designated Areas 2016/044** instrument.

As the new instrument comes into effect, some misconceptions have appeared among people who are thinking of applying for **Subclass 491** and **Subclass 494** visas.

So, let us understand what has changed, what **“designated area”** means under LIN 26/004, and why applicants should not confuse it with the separate **designated regional area** requirements for Subclass 491 and Subclass 494.

## What Is the New Designated Areas Instrument?

The **Migration (Designated Areas) Instrument 2026** is made under the definition of **“designated area”** in regulation 1.03 of the *Migration Regulations 1994*.

Under the new instrument, certain postcodes, states and territories are specified as designated areas. The list includes:

| **Australian Capital Territory**                                   | Entire Territory                                                             |

## Why Was a New Instrument Introduced and Where Is It Relevant?

The old **Designated Areas 2016/044** instrument was going to expire on **1 October 2026**. The Government therefore introduced **LIN 26/004** to replace it before expiry and repeal the previous instrument.

The designated area rules mostly apply to people who have a visa that comes with **condition 8549**.

This includes people who have the **Subclass 489 Skilled Regional (Provisional)** visa, and it can also be important for eligible **Subclass 887 Skilled Regional** visa holders.

## Do Not Confuse “Designated Area” With “Designated Regional Area”

This is the key distinction applicants need to understand.

The term **“designated area”** used in the *Migration (Designated Areas) Instrument 2026* is **not the same as** the **“designated regional area”** concept used for current regional visas such as:

* **Subclass 491 – Skilled Work Regional (Provisional)**
* **Subclass 494 – Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional)**

This means people applying for these visas should **not assume that a designated area listed under LIN 26/004 automatically qualifies for Subclass 491 or Subclass 494**.

### Example

The **whole state of Victoria** is listed as a designated area under LIN 26/004.

This does **not** mean that every location in Victoria is eligible for Subclass 491 or Subclass 494.

If you are planning to apply for **Subclass 491 or Subclass 494** as part of your Australia PR pathway, you should refer to the **designated regional area postcodes** that are applicable to Subclass 491 and Subclass 494.

[Check the official regional postcodes for Subclass 491 and Subclass 494](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/working-in-australia/skill-occupation-list/regional-postcodes)

## Which Area Requirements Apply to Your Migration Pathway?

The correct location requirements depend on your **visa subclass and individual circumstances**.

Different skilled and employer-sponsored visa pathways can have different location requirements, so applicants should **not rely on LIN 26/004 alone** when assessing a potential Australia PR pathway.
