From 30 November 2026, applicants with pending employer-sponsored visa applications may receive shorter travel periods under their Bridging Visa B (BVB). The Department of Home Affairs will determine the travel facility based on the applicant's stated purpose, anticipated travel dates and individual circumstances. 

This update affects applicants with pending Subclass 482 (Skills in Demand), 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme) and 494 (Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional) visa applications. 

Here’s what employer-sponsored visa applicants need to know about the updated BVB travel requirements and how to prepare for their upcoming travel plans. 

## When Is a Bridging Visa B (BVB) Used? 

A Bridging Visa B (BVB) is used when someone has lodged a substantive visa application in Australia but needs to travel overseas and return before receiving a visa decision.  

For example, an applicant waiting for a Subclass 482, 186 or 494 visa decision may need to travel to their home country to visit family, attend an important event or manage personal matters. 

If the applicant holds a Bridging Visa A (BVA), they generally need to obtain a BVB before leaving Australia, as a BVA does not provide travel rights. A BVB allows eligible applicants to leave and re-enter Australia within the approved travel facility period while their substantive visa application is being processed. 

## What Is Changing in BVB Applications from 30 November 2026? 

From 30 November 2026, the Department will take a stricter approach when assessing newly lodged BVB applications associated with employer-sponsored visa applications. 

The key updates include: 

* **Shorter travel periods:** Applicants should not expect lengthy travel facilities to be granted as a matter of course. The approved period will generally reflect their actual travel requirements. 

* **Closer assessment of travel reasons:** Applicants will need to demonstrate substantial reasons for leaving and returning to Australia. 

* **Clear travel details:** Applicants should specify the purpose of travel, intended departure and return dates, and expected duration overseas. 

* **Supporting evidence:** Relevant documents should be provided to support the stated reasons for travel. 

Importantly, the existing legal requirements for a BVB are not changing. The Department will apply the current requirements more closely when assessing applications. 

## Why Will Applicants Need to Provide Substantial Reasons for Travel? 

Under clause 020.212(2)(d) of the Migration Regulations 1994, applicants must satisfy the decision-maker that they have substantial reasons for wishing to leave and return to Australia while their substantive visa application is being processed. 

Although this requirement already exists, the Department intends to examine applicants' reasons more closely from 30 November 2026. 

A general statement that an applicant wants to take a holiday or visit family may not be sufficient on its own. Applicants should explain their circumstances clearly and provide relevant supporting documents wherever possible. 

Examples of travel reasons and supporting evidence include:

| Reason for Travel | Supporting Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Visiting a seriously ill family member | Medical reports, hospital letters and proof of relationship |
| Attending a wedding or funeral | Wedding invitation, death notice or relevant event documents |
| Work-related travel | Employer letter explaining the business requirement |
| Managing overseas property or legal matters | Property documents, court notices or legal correspondence |
| Visiting family after a long period away | Explanation of family circumstances and time since the last visit |

How Will the Department Determine the BVB Travel Period? 

Under the updated assessment approach, the Department will consider the applicant's actual travel requirements before deciding the length of the travel facility. 

The assessment will generally take into account: 

* The reason for overseas travel. 
* The proposed departure date. 
* The expected return date. 
* The total duration of the trip. 
* The applicant's individual circumstances. 

For example, an applicant travelling overseas to attend a family emergency may have different travel requirements from someone planning an extended personal trip. 

## What Happens to BVB Applications Lodged Before 30 November 2026? 

BVB applications lodged before 30 November 2026 will continue to be processed under the Department’s current approach. 

Applicants with applications already under processing generally do not need to provide additional documents unless specifically requested by the Department. 

## What Should Employer-Sponsored Visa Applicants Do Before Travelling? 

If you have a pending Subclass 482, 186 or 494 visa application and are planning to travel overseas, it is important to review your visa status and travel requirements before making arrangements. 

Consider taking the following steps: 

* **Check your current visa status:** Confirm whether you hold a BVA or BVB and understand the travel conditions attached to your visa. 

* **Explain your travel purpose:** Clearly describe why you need to travel and why the trip is important in your circumstances. 

* **Confirm your travel dates:** Provide your intended departure date, return date and expected duration overseas. 

* **Prepare supporting documents:** Gather relevant medical reports, family event invitations, employer letters or legal documents, depending on your reason for travel. 

* **Apply within the recommended timeframe:** Lodge your BVB application between two weeks and three months before your planned departure. 

Wait for the BVB grant: Do not leave Australia while relying on a pending BVB application.
