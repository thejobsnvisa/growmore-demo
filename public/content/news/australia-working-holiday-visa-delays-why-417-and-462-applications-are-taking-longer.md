The excitement of planning a Working Holiday Visa in Australia is starting to be replaced by frustration among many applicants as Subclass 417 and Subclass 462 visa applications are taking longer to process than usual. The Department of Home Affairs has confirmed that a high volume of applications is slowing down processing, while no revised processing timeframe has been announced. 

For applicants waiting to start their life in Australia, the delay can create uncertainty around travel, work and accommodation plans. But what is causing the slowdown, and what should applicants know before lodging or waiting for their Working Holiday Maker visa? Let’s understand. 

## Why Are Working Holiday Visa Applications Taking Longer? 

The Department of Home Affairs has advised that it is currently receiving a high volume of Working Holiday Maker visa applications. 

Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke also confirmed on 16 August 2026 that Working Holiday Maker applications are still being processed, but more slowly than previously. 

This means Australia has not stopped processing Working Holiday Maker visas. Instead, because of the increased number of applications, the visa processing time is taking longer than usual. 

At present, the Department has not announced a revised processing timeframe for either Subclass 417 or Subclass 462 applications. 

## What Is Happening With Subclass 462 Country Caps? 

According to the Department's country-cap information updated on 19 August 2026, most partner countries subject to an annual Subclass 462 cap were listed as “paused.” 

Currently, only San Marino and Türkiye were listed as “open”. 

However, applicants from some countries may face different arrangements. China, India and Vietnam are managed through a ballot pre-application process, while Indonesia is moving to a ballot system, and registration for this country is expected to open later in 2026. 

Therefore, applicants should not assume that a delayed visa decision and a paused country cap are the same issue. 

## What Does a “Paused” Country Cap Mean? 

If your country is listed as **“paused”**, it does not necessarily mean that all places have been permanently exhausted. 

The Department can temporarily pause lodgements to manage application numbers, spread applications throughout the program year or because the country is approaching its annual cap. 

A paused cap may reopen later during the same program year. 

This differs from a **“closed”** cap.  

A closed cap means the available places for that program year have been filled, and the cap will not reopen until the next program year starts on 2 July. 

## What Should Applicants Do While Waiting? 

If you have already lodged a Working Holiday Maker visa application, the slowdown does not mean your application has stopped. However, you should allow extra time for processing. 

If you are preparing to lodge a Subclass 462 application, check your country’s cap status immediately before lodging, as it can change during the program year. The Department also notes that updates may take up to 48 hours to appear. 

If the current Working Holiday Maker pathway is not available to you, you can also explore other Australian visa options that may suit your circumstances. 

## What Does This Mean for Working Holiday Visa Applicants? 

The latest update does not mean that Australia has suspended the Working Holiday Maker program. 

The main change is that Subclass 417 and Subclass 462 applications are taking longer than usual to finalise because of high application volumes. The safest approach is to check the latest Department of Home Affairs information before lodging and allow additional time when planning your move to Australia.
