If you were waiting for the right time to apply for a Northern Territory nomination, this update may not be the news you were hoping for. For applicants who were planning their pathway to Australia PR through NT subclass 190 or 491, the window of opportunity has now become much narrower.

The Northern Territory Government has announced a major update to its General Skilled Migration (GSM) nomination program for 2026–27, confirming that it has now received sufficient nomination applications for the program year. However, applications are not completely closed because there are certain applicants who fall under specific criteria and may still be able to submit a new nomination application.

So, let’s find out who may still be able to submit a nomination application and what the latest NT nomination update means for applicants who were planning to apply in 2026–27.

## Northern Territory Has Received Sufficient Applications for 2026–27

MigrationNT has now confirmed that sufficient applications have been received for the 2026–27 GSM program, meaning new nomination applications will only be accepted in limited circumstances.

As a result, new NT nomination applications will only be accepted by exception for certain eligible onshore applicants.

This is an important change for skilled migrants who were preparing to seek NT nomination for the Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190) or Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 491).

The NT received a total allocation of 1,850 nomination places for 2026–27, which includes 850 places for subclass 190 and 1,000 places for subclass 491.

## Who Can Still Apply for NT Nomination?

The exception is not available to everyone.

New nomination applications will only be accepted for onshore applicants who meet the NT Graduate or NT Resident eligibility criteria and have one of the specified deadlines falling on or before 30 September 2027.

There are three circumstances under which an applicant may still be able to submit a new application.

### 1. Your Substantive Visa Expires on or Before 30 September 2027

The first exception applies to applicants whose substantive visa expires on or before 30 September 2027 and who are not eligible for a Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485).

### 2. You Will Turn 45 on or Before 30 September 2027

The second exception relates to age.

Applicants who become ineligible for the GSM program because they will turn 45 on or before 30 September 2027 may still be able to submit a nomination application if they meet the other relevant NT requirements.

### 3. Your Subclass 491 Points Could Fall Below 65

The third exception is specifically relevant to applicants targeting subclass 491.

If an applicant's birthday falls on or before 30 September 2027 and this results in their skilled migration points falling below 65 points, they may be eligible to submit a nomination application under the exception.

## NT Cannot Guarantee Nomination Before Your Visa Expires

The NT Government has also issued an important warning for onshore applicants.

MigrationNT has stated that it cannot guarantee nominations before an applicant's visa deadline.

This is particularly important for applicants who are relying on an NT nomination because their current Australian visa is approaching expiry.

Applying for a nomination does not automatically allow you to stay in Australia indefinitely. If you are in Australia, it is important to keep track of your visa expiry date, understand your current visa conditions and consider what visa options may be available to you next.

So, applicants are advised to seek guidance from a Registered Migration Agent in Australia or a legal practitioner to make sure they remain lawful in Australia.

## What If You Do Not Meet the NT Exception Criteria?

If you do not meet the exception criteria, this does not necessarily mean that your Australian migration plans have to stop. Depending on your circumstances, you can explore other visa pathways that could be available to you.

These may include:

- Other state or territory nomination pathways, such as subclass 190 or 491, if you meet the relevant state or territory requirements.
- Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189), if you meet the eligibility requirements and receive an invitation.
- Employer-sponsored pathways, such as the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) or Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186), where eligible employer and occupation requirements are met.
- Labour Agreement (LA) pathways, where an Australian employer has an approved Labour Agreement and the applicant meets the relevant visa and nomination requirements.

The right option will depend on factors such as your age, occupation, skills assessment, points, English proficiency, work experience, current visa status and whether you have an eligible employer.
