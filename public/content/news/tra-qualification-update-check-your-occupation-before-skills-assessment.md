Trades Recognition Australia (TRA) has published its current **Australian Qualifications by Occupation** reference, giving skilled and trade applicants an important resource to check before starting a TRA skills assessment.

The document lists occupations assessed by TRA alongside the relevant Australian qualification and qualification code. It covers a wide range of trade and technical occupations, including electricians, plumbers, carpenters, mechanics, chefs, welders, telecommunications technicians and many others.

So, if you are planning to do a TRA skills assessment, it is important to first check your nominated occupation and the qualification listed against it.

## Why Should You Check Your Occupation Before Applying?

A TRA skills assessment is not simply a check that you possess a qualification.

TRA assesses whether your qualifications, training and employment evidence demonstrate the required skills and competence for your nominated occupation. Its assessment standards state that the assessment considers the level and relevance of an applicant's evidence, including training, qualifications and employment.

This means applicants should establish the connection between:

**Your qualification → Your training → Your occupation → Your employment experience**

If these elements do not properly align, having work experience alone may not be enough to obtain a positive skills assessment.

For example, someone working in an electrical occupation should not assume that any electrical qualification will automatically meet the TRA requirements for **Electrician (General)**. The relevant qualification, training content and assessment pathway all need to be considered.

## Does Your Qualification Have to Be Exactly the Same?

No. This is an important distinction.

TRA specifically states that the Australian Qualifications by Occupation list is **not exclusive**. Where a TRA skills assessment requires an Australian qualification, alternative qualifications or older versions may be accepted if they are relevant to the nominated occupation and the applicant meets the applicable assessment standard.

Therefore, applicants should not interpret the list as saying:

> "If my qualification is not listed, my application will automatically fail."

At the same time, applicants should not assume that any qualification will be accepted simply because it has a similar title. TRA's assessment criteria still apply, and the qualification must be relevant to the nominated occupation.

## Some Occupations Have Additional Licensing Considerations

A positive TRA skills assessment should also not be confused with an Australian occupational licence.

TRA states that it does not issue occupational registrations or licences. Applicants in licensed occupations may need to satisfy separate licensing requirements with the relevant Australian state or territory authority.

This is particularly relevant for occupations such as:

* Electrician (General)
* Electrician (Special Class)
* Plumber (General)
* Airconditioning and Refrigeration Mechanic

## What This Means for Australian Migration Applicants

The latest TRA qualification reference is a useful reminder that skills assessment starts before you submit the application.

Choosing the wrong occupation, relying on an unrelated qualification or applying through the wrong TRA pathway can create unnecessary delays and potentially affect your migration plans.

The safest approach is to assess your occupation, qualification, training, employment history and intended visa pathway together, rather than treating the qualification list as a standalone checklist.
