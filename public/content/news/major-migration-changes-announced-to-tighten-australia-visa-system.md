The wait is finally over, with the Australian Government announcing its long-awaited migration changes. The announcement provides greater clarity for international students, skilled migrants, Working Holiday Makers and other temporary visa holders on the government’s plans to tighten the visa system and manage migration levels.

Immigration Minister Tony Burke announced a series of measures at the National Press Club on 17 September 2026, which may affect family members of students, visa hopping, visitor visas, skilled migration priorities, Working Holiday visas and visa compliance.

So, let’s look at the key changes and what they could mean for people planning their Australian visa pathway.

## 1. New Restrictions Proposed for Student Visa Dependants

One of the biggest changes announced concerns international students and their family members.

Under the announced changes, most new international students and Temporary Graduate visa holders will no longer be able to have secondary visa applicants, with exceptions including students from Pacific and ASEAN nations and postgraduate students undertaking a PhD.

This could significantly affect students who are planning to study in Australia with their partner or children, and people who are already in Australia with secondary applicants will not be affected by this change.

The government is also targeting visa hopping, particularly where people move between temporary visas and use repeated applications or appeals to extend their stay beyond the original purpose of their visa.

However, the government has distinguished this from legitimate progression through the migration system, such as progressing from a student visa to a graduate visa, then a skilled visa and eventually citizenship.

Students will generally be able to apply for a new visa where they are genuinely progressing to a higher-level qualification, such as moving from a bachelor's degree to a master's qualification.

Transfers to lower-tier education providers will require a new visa application, consistent with existing policy and visa condition 8202.

## 2. Working Holiday Visa Changes to Introduce a Ballot

Working Holiday Makers are another major focus of the announcement.

The government will introduce a ballot for second- and third-year Working Holiday Maker visas. Applicants will still need to meet the existing specified work requirements, including 88 days of regional work for a second-year visa and six months of specified work for a third-year visa.

The proposed annual numbers are:

| Working Holiday Visa | Proposed Places |
| -------------------- | --------------: |
| Second year          |          45,000 |
| Third year           |           5,000 |

This represents a reduction from the previous numbers of approximately 57,000 second-year and 31,000 third-year Working Holiday Makers.

Processing for Working Holiday Maker applications is also expected to return to a normal pace for applicants who have already completed the relevant regional work requirements. However, processing for UK applicants is expected to remain slower because UK citizens are not required to undertake regional work under the relevant Free Trade Agreement and the UK cohort has grown significantly.

For people using the Working Holiday Maker program to gain Australian work experience, these changes could affect their plans for extending their stay beyond the first year.

## 3. Visitor Visa Holders Could Face a No Further Stay Condition

The government also plans to introduce a No Further Stay condition, **condition 8503**, on all future visitor visa grants.

This condition will generally prevent visitor visa holders from making further onshore applications for most substantive visas, with protection visa applications remaining an exception.

Visitor visas that have already been granted, and people already in Australia holding a visitor visa, will not be affected by this new measure.

The measure is intended to reduce visa hopping and the use of visitor visas as a pathway to lodge other visa applications onshore.

The government has also indicated that offshore partners may be able to more easily obtain visitor visas for extended periods to spend time with their partners in Australia under this approach.

## 4. Skilled Migration to Prioritise Key Industries

The government also announced changes aimed at better targeting skilled migration towards areas considered important to Australia's workforce needs.

Under changes to processing priorities, agriculture and resources/fisheries are being added to the priority-processing arrangements. Construction, teaching and healthcare are already included in the relevant priority settings.

The government will announce a new points test that will give greater weight to housing and construction trade qualifications. Trades in high-demand areas are expected to receive points broadly comparable to those available for a university qualification.

This means skilled migrants in construction and other high-demand trade occupations could be affected by the design of the new points test once further details are released.

## 5. Stronger Action Against Visa Overstayers

Visa compliance is another major part of the government's announcement.

The government has stated that there are approximately 77,000 visa overstayers in Australia and plans to strengthen measures to ensure people without a valid visa leave Australia.

The government plans to return to a process involving detention followed by voluntary departure within weeks for visa overstayers, while stating that the approach will not involve raid-style enforcement.

For current visa holders, this reinforces the importance of maintaining lawful status in Australia and complying with visa conditions.

## 6. Migration Levels Remain a Major Focus

The latest changes are linked to the government's target of reducing net overseas migration (NOM).

According to Australian Bureau of Statistics data released on 17 September 2026, net overseas migration was **292,100** for the year to March 2026, down from **309,500** in the previous year.

The government is targeting NOM of **245,000 in 2026–27** and **225,000 per year from 2027–28**. The government has also indicated that NOM is currently tracking below its forecast for this year.

## 7. Stronger Regulation of Migration Agents

The government has also indicated further action against migration agents who knowingly support visa applications without merit, with additional sanctions expected.

Ministerial Direction 110 will also be updated to strengthen protections for child crime victims and family violence victims and to reflect the government's approach to addressing hatred.
