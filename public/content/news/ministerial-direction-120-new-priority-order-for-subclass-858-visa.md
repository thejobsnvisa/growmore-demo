Australia has introduced a new priority framework for high-achieving professionals, researchers, innovators, and global talents who are applying for the **Subclass 858 visa**.

So, if you are aiming to secure your Australia PR through the National Innovation visa, Global Talent visa, or Distinguished Talent visa pathways, understanding these changes is important. Because the priority of your visa process will now depend on your achievements, nominations, and contribution to Australia’s key sectors. 

Here is everything you need to know about the latest Subclass 858 visa priority changes under Ministerial Direction 120. 

## What Is Ministerial Direction 120?

Ministerial Direction 120 provides instructions to Australian immigration decision-makers on how to order the processing and assessment of **Class BX (Subclass 858)** visa applications.

### Covered Visas
* **National Innovation visa (Subclass 858)**
* **Global Talent visa (Subclass 858)**
* **Distinguished Talent visa (Subclass 858)**

The National Innovation visa is the current form of the Subclass 858 visa, while the Global Talent and Distinguished Talent visas were earlier versions of this visa category. 

This Direction applies to National Innovation visa applications which are lodged on or after 25 July 2026 and National Innovation visa applications which are lodged before 25 July 2026 but not yet finalised. 

## New Subclass 858 Visa Priority Order Under MD 120

Ministerial Direction 120 introduces a **five-level priority structure**. Applications in **Priority 1** receive the highest consideration, while **Priority 5** represents all other applications.

**Priority 1: Global Experts and International Award Recipients**

The highest priority is given to applicants who: 

* Are recognised as global experts in their field  
* Have received top-level international awards  

Examples of recognised awards may include internationally significant achievements such as Nobel Prizes, Fields Medal, Olympic Gold Medals, and other equivalent global awards. 

**Priority 2: Government-Nominated Applicants**

Applicants nominated by an approved Australian Government agency or a State or Territory Government agency through the approved Form 1000 nomination process receive the second priority. 

**Priority 3: Applicants in Tier 1 Priority Sectors**

Applicants demonstrating exceptional and outstanding achievements in Australia’s critical sectors may receive priority consideration. 

Tier 1 sectors include: 
* Critical technologies  
* Renewable and low-emission technologies  
* Health industries

**Priority 5: All Other Applications**

Applications that do not fall under the above categories will be considered under the fifth priority level. 

## What Has Changed From Ministerial Direction 112?

The priority order and assessment approach under MD 120 remain largely similar to the previous Ministerial Direction 112. 

The major change is that MD 120 expands the scope of the priority system to cover the entire Class BX Subclass 858 visa category. 

This ensures that updated priority rules clearly apply across:
1. Current **National Innovation visa** applications.
2. Existing **Global Talent visa** applications awaiting decisions.
3. Existing **Distinguished Talent visa** applications awaiting decisions.

## What This Means for Subclass 858 Visa Applicants
The introduction of MD 120 highlights Australia’s focus on attracting individuals who can contribute significantly to the country’s innovation, economic growth, research, and priority industries. 

Applicants with strong international recognition, government nominations, or achievements aligned with Australia’s priority sectors may receive earlier consideration compared with other applications. 

However, meeting priority criteria does not guarantee visa approval. Applicants must still satisfy all Subclass 858 visa requirements, including demonstrating exceptional and outstanding achievement.
