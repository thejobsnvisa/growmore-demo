Tasmania has continued its regular weekly skilled migration invitation activity and invited **59 Registrations of Interest (ROIs)** for state nomination on **3 September 2026**.

This is the state’s **third weekly invitation round**, as Tasmania continues to use its **2,050 nomination allocations** for the 2026–27 program year.

For skilled migrants who are waiting for a Tasmania state nomination invitation, the latest round gives another opportunity to progress towards nomination under **Subclass 190 or Subclass 491** by meeting Tasmania’s eligibility and nomination requirements.

Here is a complete update on the latest invitation round and what it means for applicants

## Tasmania’s Third Weekly Invitation Round

The **3 September 2026 Tasmania invitation round** included both **Gold and other priority invitations** across both skilled visa subclasses.

| Visa Subclass    | Total Invitations | Gold Invitations | Other Invitations |
| ---------------- | ----------------: | ---------------: | ----------------: |
| **Subclass 190** |                34 |               21 |                13 |
| **Subclass 491** |                25 |                3 |                23 |
| **Total**        |            **59** |           **24** |            **36** |

The lowest scores invited in this round were:

* **Subclass 190:** 519 points
* **Subclass 491:** 82 points

These are the lowest scores among ROIs invited in this particular round, but they should **not be treated as fixed point cut-offs** for future Tasmania invitation rounds.

## More Subclass 190 Invitations Expected

One of the key developments in this week's update is Tasmania’s expectation that it can continue issuing more **non-Gold Subclass 190 invitations**.

Over the past three weeks, the number of **Gold invitations for Subclass 190** has been declining, as anticipated by Migration Tasmania. This has created scope for more invitations to other eligible ROIs.

At the same time, new ROI submissions fell to around **160 this week**, which Tasmania says is in line with its long-term average. The state expects the number of new ROIs to stabilise at this level.

If that occurs, Tasmania anticipates being able to issue more **non-Gold invitations for Subclass 190 ROIs** in the coming weeks.

For applicants without a **Gold priority attribute**, this could be an important development. However, it does not mean that simply having **519 points** or any particular score will guarantee an invitation.

## Current Tasmania Points Benchmark

Applicants should know the difference between the score of the **lowest ROI invited in this round** and Tasmania’s **rolling three-month invitation benchmark**.

For the **3 September 2026** round, the lowest invited scores were:

* **Subclass 190:** 519 points
* **Subclass 491:** 82 points

Tasmania also reports the lowest point score required to gain nomination at assessment, based on a **rolling three-month average**:

* **Subclass 190:** 547 points
* **Subclass 491:** 90 points

The first figures represent the **lowest scores invited in this particular round**, while the second figures are based on a **rolling three-month average of the lowest point scores resulting in an invitation**.

## What Does This Mean for Applicants?

The third weekly invitation round provides a clearer indication of how Tasmania is managing its skilled migration invitations.

The lower number of **Gold Subclass 190 invitations**, together with ROI submissions returning to the long-term average of around **160 per week**, is a positive sign. This may allow Tasmania to invite more **non-Gold candidates** in upcoming rounds.

However, applicants should not assume that the latest invitation score is a new permanent threshold. Tasmania’s invitation results can change from one round to another based on the number and characteristics of ROIs available at the time.

For anyone preparing an ROI, getting the details right is important. Claims regarding **points, employment, skills assessment, English proficiency, and priority attributes** must be supported by authentic and verifiable evidence.
