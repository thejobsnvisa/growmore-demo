Tasmania has released the results of its latest skilled migration invitation round held on 14 May 2026. In this round, the state issued a total of 56 invitations across the Skilled Nominated visa (Subclass 190) and Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa (Subclass 491) pathways.

This latest round shows increasing competition, particularly for Subclass 190 applicants, where the minimum invited score increased significantly compared to the previous round. Here are all the updated figures, which give you a clearer picture of current invitation trends, remaining nomination places, and candidate demand in Tasmania’s migration program.

## Tasmania Invitation Round Results

| Visa Subclass | 07 May 2026 | 14 May 2026 |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Subclass 190 Invitations | 31 | 32 |
| Subclass 190 Lowest Score | 330 | 356 |
| Subclass 190 Pass Category | Green Pass | Green Pass |
| Subclass 491 Invitations | 31 | 24 |
| Subclass 491 Lowest Score | 54 points | 56 points |
| Subclass 491 Pass Category | Orange-Plus Pass | Orange-Plus Pass |
| Total Invitations | 62 | 56 |

Tasmania issued a slightly higher number of invitations under the Subclass 190 visa this week. However, the number of invitations for the Subclass 491 visa saw a small drop compared to the previous round.

One of the most noticeable changes in this round was the increase in the Subclass 190 minimum invited score, which rose from 330 points to 356 points within just one week. This suggests that competition for Tasmania nomination under the Green Pass category is becoming increasingly competitive.

The updated nomination data shows that Tasmania continues to manage a large number of pending ROIs across both visa subclasses. At the same time, the number of remaining nomination places is gradually declining as weekly invitation rounds continue, and the program moves into its final quarter of the 2025–26 program year.

## What This Means for Skilled Migration Applicants

The latest invitation round highlights that competition for Tasmania state nomination remains strong, particularly under the Subclass 190 stream.

Applicants with lower or borderline scores may face difficulty securing invitations in upcoming rounds unless they improve their overall profile competitiveness. To improve this, you may consider:

* Higher English language test scores  
* Additional skilled employment experience  
* Partner skills points  
* NAATI credentialing  

With Tasmania’s invitation scores changing quickly and nomination places reducing each week, many skilled migration applicants are now finding that simply meeting the minimum points requirement is no longer enough for an Australia PR Visa pathway. A strong EOI strategy, correct points claims, occupation planning, and choosing the right state nomination pathway can make a major difference in receiving an invitation.

If you are confused about whether you should continue waiting for a Tasmania nomination or explore another state where your chances may be stronger, speaking with a Registered Migration Agent (MARA No. 2217941) can help you better understand the most suitable pathway for your Australia PR Visa goals.
