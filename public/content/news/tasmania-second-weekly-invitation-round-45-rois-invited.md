While many Australian states are yet to open their 2026–27 skilled migration invitation rounds, Tasmania is already moving ahead. The state began inviting ROIs last week and has now come back with its second weekly invitation round, giving skilled migrants another opportunity to receive a state nomination invitation.

In the latest round held on **27 August 2026**, Tasmania invited a total of **45 ROIs** across Subclass 190 and Subclass 491. Here is a closer look at the latest invitation numbers and Tasmania’s current nomination position.

## Tasmania Invites 45 ROIs in Latest Round

In this second weekly round, Tasmania invited **20 candidates for Subclass 190** and **25 candidates for Subclass 491**.

| Visa subclass    | Invitations issued | Gold invitations | Other invitations | Lowest score invited |
| ---------------- | -----------------: | ---------------: | ----------------: | -------------------: |
| **Subclass 190** |                 20 |               17 |           3 Green |           544 points |
| **Subclass 491** |                 25 |                1 |                24 |            91 points |
| **Total**        |             **45** |           **18** |            **27** |                    — |

For Subclass 190, Tasmania issued 20 invitations, including 17 Gold invitations and 3 Green invitations, while for Subclass 491, the state invited 25 ROIs, including 1 Gold invitation and 24 further invitations.

Tasmania said that due to the high number of Gold ROIs received, only a limited number of additional ROIs were selected for Subclass 190. The state also noted that ROI invitations had been oversubscribed over the past two weeks, so only three non-Gold ROIs were invited in this round.

Tasmania expects the number of Gold Pass ROIs to decrease significantly over the coming weeks, which may allow the state to issue more invitations in future weekly rounds.

## Lowest Score Invited vs Lowest Score Required for Nomination

Applicants should understand that the lowest score invited in this round is different from Tasmania’s lowest point score required to gain nomination at assessment.

The latest invitation round recorded the lowest invited scores as:

* **Subclass 190:** 544 points
* **Subclass 491:** 91 points

However, Tasmania's reported lowest point score required to gain nomination at assessment is currently:

* **Subclass 190:** 562 points
* **Subclass 491:** 94 points

These figures represent a **rolling three-month average of the lowest point score resulting in an invitation**, rather than a fixed minimum score for every candidate.

This distinction matters because receiving an invitation does not automatically guarantee nomination. Tasmania verifies the priority attributes and information claimed in the ROI during the nomination assessment.

Where an ROI is invited and all claimed priority attributes are verified, the nomination will be approved. However, if one or more claimed priority attributes cannot be verified and the applicant's score falls below the applicable lowest-score threshold, the nomination application will be declined.

Tasmania has also warned that applications will be declined where candidates are found to have intentionally claimed priority attributes to artificially increase their points score.

For Gold Pass holders, the requirement is even stricter. A Gold Pass holder who does not meet the Gold priority attribute will be declined regardless of their total points score.

## What This Tasmania Invitation Round Means for Applicants

Tasmania is actively inviting skilled migrants early in the 2026–27 program year, but competition remains strong, particularly for Subclass 190.

Tasmania issued **45 invitations this round**, down from **93 invitations in the first round**. Subclass 190 invitations fell from 67 to 20, while Subclass 491 stayed almost steady at 25 compared with 26 previously. The lowest invited scores also dropped to **544 for Subclass 190** and **91 for Subclass 491**.

This round also shows a strong focus on Gold Pass holders, who received 17 of the 20 Subclass 190 invitations. However, Tasmania expects Gold Pass ROIs to decline in the coming weeks, potentially creating more opportunities for other eligible applicants.

Applicants should also remember that points alone do not guarantee an invitation. Tasmania assesses the information and priority attributes claimed in the ROI, which must be accurate and verifiable.
