A recent Aged Parent visa refusal case has raised concerns about Australia's parent visa program and how migration laws apply when the primary applicant passes away during the visa processing period. The case has also sparked calls for greater clarity, consistency and compassion for affected families.

## What Happened in This Aged Parent Visa Case?

The case involved a family that lodged an Aged Parent (Subclass 804) visa application in January 2021. The father was listed as the primary applicant because he met the age requirement at the time of application, while his wife was included as the secondary applicant.

Sadly, the primary applicant passed away in October 2023 while the visa application was still waiting to be processed. The family continued to wait, expecting the application to proceed for the surviving applicant. Instead, they received a visa refusal in May 2026.

The refusal came as a surprise because the surviving spouse had since reached the qualifying age for an Aged Parent visa.

However, under Australia's migration legislation, meeting the age requirement later does not necessarily make an applicant eligible if they did not satisfy the relevant criteria when the original application was lodged.

## Understanding the Aged Parent Visa Requirement

The Aged Parent (Subclass 804) visa is designed for eligible parents of Australian citizens, permanent residents or eligible New Zealand citizens who are old enough to receive the Australian Age Pension.

In joint applications:

- At least one applicant must satisfy the aged parent requirement when the application is submitted.
- If the primary applicant dies before a decision is made, the remaining applicant must independently meet the primary applicant requirements.
- Simply reaching pension age later does not automatically make the surviving applicant eligible under the existing application.

This requirement is especially important because parent visa applications can take several years to process.

## Here Is Where the Concerns Lie

This case has highlighted the challenges families can face when personal circumstances change during the long parent visa processing period.

In some similar situations, applicants were reportedly allowed to withdraw and re-lodge their applications without losing their place in the processing queue.

However, this option has not always been available or clearly communicated.

As a result, questions remain about:

- Whether applicants can retain their queue date if they re-lodge their application.
- What options are available if a parent visa application is refused.
- How similar cases will be assessed in the future.

## What Does the Department of Home Affairs Say?

The Department of Home Affairs has stated that all visa decisions are made in accordance with Australia's migration laws and regulations.

Under current legislation, applicants must satisfy the relevant visa requirements both when the application is lodged and when a decision is made.

Every application is assessed on its individual circumstances.

The department has also indicated that when it becomes aware that a primary applicant has died before a visa decision is made, officers may contact the remaining applicant to discuss any available options. However, the legislative requirements for the visa must still be met.

## Long Processing Times Continue to Create Challenges

Parent visas are among Australia's longest-processing visa categories. During these lengthy processing periods, significant life events such as illness, death or changes in family circumstances can affect an applicant's eligibility.

No doubt, Australia's migration program has to balance different visa priorities, but long processing times can create difficulties when an applicant's circumstances change while they are waiting for a decision.

## What This Means for Aged Parent Visa Applicants

Changes in personal circumstances during long processing periods can significantly affect the outcome of a visa application.
