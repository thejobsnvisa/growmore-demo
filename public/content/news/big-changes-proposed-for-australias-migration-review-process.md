Australia's migration review system could soon see major reforms after the Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Legislation Committee recommended passing the Administrative and Judicial Review Legislation Amendment Bill 2026. The proposed legislation is designed to improve the efficiency of Australia's migration review framework, which will help the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) and the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (FCFCOA) manage an increasing number of migration cases.

Although the Bill has not become law yet, it is an important step towards improving Australia's migration review system. If it is approved by Parliament, it will introduce several changes to how visa reviews will take place. 

## Why Is Australia Reforming the Migration Review Process? 

Australia has experienced a growing number of migration review applications in recent years that place additional pressure on the Administrative Review Tribunal and the courts. 

To improve efficiency while also maintaining access to justice, the proposed Administrative and Judicial Review Legislation Amendment Bill 2026 seeks to amend both the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 and the Migration Act 1958. The reforms are intended to make review procedures easier, reduce administrative delays, and ensure the system can properly manage increasing caseloads. 

**Key Changes Proposed Under the Bill**

If the legislation is passed, several important changes will be introduced to Australia's migration review process. 

**1. Tribunal May Deliver Oral Reasons for Decisions**

One of the most notable reforms is the ability for the Administrative Review Tribunal to provide oral reasons for certain decisions instead of automatically issuing written reasons. 

To protect applicants, the Senate Committee recommended that individuals receive clear written advice explaining: 

* How to request written reasons for a decision. 
* The applicable deadlines for seeking judicial review. 

**2. Introduction of an Approved Application Form** 

The Bill proposes that certain migration review applications must be lodged using an approved application form. 

This measure is intended to simplify application processing, improve consistency, and reduce administrative errors during the review process. 

**3. New Identity Verification Requirements** 

Applicants may also be required to satisfy new identity verification requirements at the time of lodging review applications. 

**4. Expanded Powers for Tribunal Registrars** 

The proposed legislation would also expand the powers of Administrative Review Tribunal registrars. 

Under the reforms, registrars would be able to dismiss certain applications in specific circumstances without requiring every matter to be considered by a Tribunal member. 

## Senate Committee Recommends the Bill Be Passed 

The Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Legislation Committee conducted an inquiry into the proposed legislation after receiving 16 submissions from key stakeholders, including: 

* Attorney-General's Department  
* Administrative Review Tribunal  
* Law Council of Australia  
* Migration Institute of Australia  
* Refugee and community legal organisations  

The Committee also held a public hearing on 27 July 2026 before delivering its report on 7 August 2026. After reviewing the Bill, the Committee recommended that it be passed without amendments. 

## What Does This Mean for Australian Visa Applicants? 

For now, nothing changes immediately. 

The Bill has only been recommended by the Senate Committee and must still complete the remaining stages of the parliamentary process before it can become law. 

However, if it is passed, visa applicants seeking a review before the Administrative Review Tribunal should expect changes to application procedures, identity verification requirements, and how Tribunal decisions may be communicated. 

Anyone currently involved in an ART review or considering lodging a review application should continue following the existing legal requirements until any new legislation officially commences.
