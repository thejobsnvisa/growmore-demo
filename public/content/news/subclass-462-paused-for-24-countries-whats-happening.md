Many people hoping to apply for Australia’s Work and Holiday (subclass 462) visa may now have to wait because the Department of Home Affairs has paused applications for almost all countries covered by annual caps. As a result, only a few countries are showing an open application status at the moment.

If your country is marked “paused,” you may still get a chance to apply later. The Department can reopen applications during the same program year, but there is no guarantee when the pause cap will reopen.

So, let’s look at why this is happening and what subclass 462 visa applicants should do next.

## Why Have 24 Countries Been Paused?

The Department of Home Affairs says there is currently strong demand for the Work and Holiday Australia program, and applications from some countries may exceed the number of places available. When this happens, the Department temporarily pauses applications under the country-specific subclass 462 visa cap.

A country cap can be paused for several reasons, including:

- Managing the number of applications being lodged at a particular time
- Spreading visa lodgements throughout the program year
- Closely monitoring applications when a country is approaching its annual grant cap
- Supporting efficient visa processing and the Government’s migration settings

So, “paused” does not necessarily mean that all available places have already been used. It means applications are temporarily not being accepted while the Department manages the cap.

## Paused vs Closed: What is the Difference?

This is the most important point for applicants.

A paused subclass 462 country cap is temporary. The Department may reopen applications later in the same program year.

However, a closed country cap is different. It means the places available for that program year have already been filled, and the cap will not reopen until the next program year.

## What Should Applicants Do Now?

If you are planning to apply for the Australian Work and Holiday visa subclass 462, the current pause doesn’t mean you should simply sit back and wait for the next update. A smarter approach is to start preparing now, which makes you ready for whatever comes next.

So, do it by:

- Checking your country’s current cap status regularly.
- Keeping all required documents ready.
- Reviewing the subclass 462 visa eligibility requirements.
- Exploring migration pathways that may suit your profile based on your skills and experience.
- Looking for suitable employers if you may qualify for an employer-sponsored visa in Australia.
- Keeping an eye on Australia’s broader migration opportunities and preparing to act when the right pathway becomes available.
