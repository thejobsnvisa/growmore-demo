Australia’s immigration system is seeing several changes following Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke’s recent speech. One of the key changes is the introduction of two new Ministerial Directions, MD 121 and MD 122, which replace the previous processing arrangements under MD 119.

Ministerial Direction 122 (MD 122) came into effect on 19 September 2026 and introduces a new processing order for some provisional and permanent skilled visa applications. This is the full update on the new priority for skilled visa processing.

## Which Visas Are Covered by MD 122?

MD 122 applies to visa applications for several skilled, regional, permanent and business visa subclasses, including:

* Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme
* Subclass 187 Regional Employer Nomination Scheme
* Subclass 189 Skilled Independent
* Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated
* Subclass 191 Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional)
* Subclass 489 Skilled-Regional Sponsored
* Subclass 491 Skilled Work Regional
* Subclass 494 Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional
* Subclass 887 Skilled (Residence)
* Subclass 888 Business Innovation and Investment

Subclass 482 is not covered by MD 122. Its processing order is allocated separately under [Ministerial Direction 121 (MD 121)](https://www.growmore.one/news/new-subclass-482-visa-processing-priorities-what-applicants-need-to-know/).

Another important distinction is that MD 122 applies to visa applications only, rather than nominations.

## Four New Priority Groups for Skilled Visa Processing

Under MD 122, the Department has established four processing priority groups.

### Priority 1: Key Occupations and Sectors

The highest priority applies to applications where the primary applicant is in an occupation in:

* Construction
* Healthcare
* Teaching
* Agriculture
* Aquaculture
* Fishing
* Resources

It also covers occupations related to Australia’s law enforcement or defence interests, as defined by the Direction.

A key change is that applicants can receive this highest processing priority whether they applied while they were in Australia or while they were overseas.

### Priority 2: Other Onshore Applications

The second priority group covers other applications where the primary applicant was in Australia when the visa application was made.

If the applicant does not fall within the first priority group but was onshore when applying, their application generally falls into this category.

### Priority 3: Other Offshore Applications

The third group covers other applications where the primary applicant was outside Australia when the application was made.

### Priority 4: Remaining Applications

The fourth group covers the remaining applications that do not fall within the first three categories.

This does not mean these applications will not be processed. Rather, they sit lower in the priority order established by MD 122.

## Existing Skilled Visa Applications Can Also Be Reordered

One of the most important points for applicants is that MD 122 does not only apply to new visa applications.

The Direction applies to applications made on or after 19 September 2026, as well as applications made before that date that had not been finally determined when MD 122 commenced.

Therefore, an applicant who has already lodged a covered skilled visa may have their application considered under the new processing priority structure.

## Which Applications Are Not Covered?

MD 122 does not apply to every application within the listed visa subclasses.

The Direction does not apply to certain matters, including:

* Visa applications remitted by the AAT or ART for reconsideration
* Applications where the AAT or ART has set aside a decision and substituted a new decision
* Applications where it is readily apparent that the visa criteria would not be satisfied
* Certain applications made separately by a family unit member where the primary visa holder was in Australia when they applied
* The Administrative Review Tribunal itself

## MD 122 vs Previous MD 119: What Has Changed?

There are several important differences between the two Directions:

* MD 122 focuses on visa applications, while the previous arrangements under MD 119 also dealt with nominations.
* The Subclass 482 visa has also been separated from MD 122 and is now dealt with under MD 121.
* The highest priority category under MD 122 is also broader. Along with construction, healthcare and teaching, it now includes agriculture, aquaculture, fishing and resources, as well as specified law enforcement and defence-related occupations.
* Another major change is that the highest priority group does not distinguish between applicants who are onshore and offshore.

## What Does MD 122 Mean for Skilled Visa Applicants?

MD 122 makes processing priority an important consideration for applicants with covered skilled visa applications. However, priority does not mean guaranteed approval or a fixed processing timeframe.

The Department must still assess the relevant visa criteria, nomination requirements where applicable, health and character requirements, and other legislative requirements.
