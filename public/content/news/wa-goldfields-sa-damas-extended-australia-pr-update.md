The Australian Capital Territory (ACT) has officially wrapped up its 2025–26 Skilled Migration Program by reaching its full allocation of 1,600 nominations.

While ACT is still waiting for the 2026–27 nomination allocation from the Department of Home Affairs, it has announced several important updates that will affect applicants planning to apply for Australian permanent residency through the Subclass 190 and Subclass 491 visa pathways.

The ACT has introduced a new online portal, updated Canberra Matrix settings, and revised fees for the new financial year, which will reshape how ACT skilled migration applications are managed during the new financial year.

### **Here's a summary of the key changes announced by ACT Migration for the upcoming program year.**

## ACT Reached Its Full Nomination Allocation for 2025–26

In a significant Australia PR update, ACT Migration confirmed it has successfully filled all 1,600 nomination places allocated for the 2025–26 financial year.

### **here Key Statistics**

| **Category** | **Details** |
|----------|---------|
| Total Approvals | 1,600 approvals |
| 190 Nominations | 800 approvals |
| 491 Nominations | 800 approvals |
| Total Refusals | 229 applications |
| 190 Nominations | 111 refusals (12.2%) |
| 491 Nominations | 128 refusals (13.8%) |
| ACT Resident | 1,417 approvals (89%) |
| Overseas Resident | 183 approvals (11%) |
| Average Processing Time | 18 business days |

## Which Migration Pathways Received the Most Nominations?

The majority of nominations were issued through the standard ACT Occupation List. **However, here is the nomination breakdown by pathway:**

- ACT Occupation List: 1,244 nominations
- PhD Streamlined Pathway: 133 nominations
- Small Business Owner Pathway: 124 nominations
- Subclass 482 Streamlined Pathway: 109 nominations

These figures show that the ACT Occupation List remains the primary pathway for skilled workers seeking ACT nomination.

## ACT Occupation List to Be Updated

ACT Migration has confirmed that it is currently reviewing the [ACT Occupation List ahead of the 2026–27 program year](https://www.growmore.one/news/act-state-nomination-2025-2026-latest-round-allocation-update/). Some changes are accepted in this occupation list, but it will remain broadly aligned with Canberra's labour market needs.

Applicants planning to submit a Canberra Matrix for ACT Skilled Migration should monitor future occupation list updates carefully, as occupation eligibility may change.

## ACT Nomination Guidelines Will Also Change

Alongside the occupation list review, ACT will introduce minor updates to its nomination guidelines.

**The revised guidelines are expected to:**

- clarify existing policy requirements,
- improve operational processes,
- introduce a clearer framework for requesting priority processing, and
- reflect changes associated with the new online application system.

Updated guidelines are expected to be released in late July 2026, although the timeline may change depending on operational requirements.

## New ACT Nomination Portal Launching in July 2026

One of the biggest changes announced is the launch of a completely new ACT Nomination Application Portal, which is expected to go live on 27 July 2026 (subject to change). To prepare for the transition, the existing portal is expected to be unavailable from the week beginning 13 July 2026.

The new portal aims to provide a smoother and more secure application experience.

**here is Key improvements include:**

- Multi-factor authentication for enhanced security
- Live application status tracking
- Ability to edit or withdraw Canberra Matrix submissions
- Better mobile compatibility
- Improved user experience for applicants and migration agents
- Measures to reduce non-genuine Expressions of Interest affecting invitation rounds

These upgrades are designed to make the ACT nomination process more transparent and efficient.

## ACT Migration Fees Will Increase for the First Time in 14 Years

With the launch of the new portal, ACT Migration will introduce updated fees for the first time in 14 years.

**From the 2026–27 program year:**

| **Application** | **New Fee (excluding GST)** |
|-------------|------------------------:|
| Canberra Matrix submission | $25 |
| ACT Nomination Application (Subclass 190 or 491) | $325 |
| Regional Certifying Body (RCB) Support Application (SESR Visa) | $325 |

For most skilled migration applicants, the total ACT nomination cost will now be $350 (excluding GST). The new fee structure separates the Canberra Matrix submission from the nomination application, creating a more transparent payment process.

## Canberra Matrix Rules Are Changing

ACT has also introduced several important changes to the Canberra Matrix system.

### **1. Matrix validity extended**

A Canberra Matrix will continue to remain valid for six months. However, applicants who update their Matrix before it expires can now extend its validity by another six months, allowing a maximum validity period of 12 months.

### **2. Updated Matrix is treated as a new submission**

Every time an applicant edits their Canberra Matrix, it will be treated as a new submission.

**This means:**

- all eligibility claims must be valid on the latest update date,
- employment claims must meet eligibility requirements at the time of update, and
- invitation rankings will be based on the last updated date, not the original submission date.

Applicants can update their Matrix without paying another submission fee.

## Existing Canberra Matrix Applications Will Be Transferred

Applicants who already have an active Canberra Matrix do not need to lodge a new submission. Existing Matrix applications will automatically transfer to the new portal if applicants register using the same email address linked to their current account.

## When Will ACT Resume Invitation Rounds?

Many skilled visa applicants are waiting for the first ACT invitation round for the new financial year.

However, ACT Migration has confirmed that:

- No invitation round has yet been scheduled,
- Invitations are unlikely before one month after the new portal launches, and
- Invitation rounds also depend on the Department of Home Affairs announcing the ACT's nomination allocation for the 2026–27 Migration Program.

## What This Means for Skilled Migration Applicants

Although ACT Migration has not yet received its 2026–27 nomination allocation, the latest announcement gives applicants a clear indication of what to expect in the new financial year.
