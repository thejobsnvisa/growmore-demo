Ignoring Australia's employer sponsorship rules can come at a high cost. That is exactly what happened to a New South Wales’ aged care provider. The provider has been ordered to repay nearly $120,000 after the Australian Border Force (ABF) found it had unlawfully passed sponsorship-related costs onto workers who are holding Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) visas. 

The employer has also been barred from lodging new sponsor nomination applications for six months, which highlights the serious consequences of failing to meet sponsorship obligations under Australian migration law. 

## ABF Investigation Reveals Sponsorship Breach 

According to the Australian Border Force, the investigation began after information was submitted through the Government's Border Watch reporting system. 

ABF officers found that the NSW aged care provider had sponsored 19 overseas workers under the Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) visa. During the investigation, it was discovered that the business had recovered sponsorship-related costs from 13 of those workers, which makes a total of $118,197. 

But if we look at Australia's actual migration laws, then employers are prohibited from recovering certain sponsorship-related costs from sponsored workers. These costs must be paid by the sponsoring employer and cannot be passed on to employees either directly or indirectly. 

Following the investigation, the company repaid all affected workers in full. In addition to the repayment, it is now prohibited from submitting new sponsor nomination applications for six months.

## Why Employers Cannot Pass Sponsorship Costs to Workers 

Australia's employer-sponsored visa program places strict legal obligations on approved sponsors. Employers sponsoring overseas workers under the Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) visa are responsible for meeting various sponsorship requirements, including paying specific sponsorship-related costs. 

Employers cannot ask workers to reimburse these expenses, deduct them from wages, or enter into arrangements that effectively transfer these costs to sponsored employees. Doing so may result in compliance action by the Australian Border Force, sponsorship sanctions, financial penalties, and restrictions on sponsoring overseas workers in the future. 

This case is a reminder that employer sponsorship is not only about filling skill shortages but also about complying with Australia's migration framework designed to protect migrant workers. 

## Border Watch Report Triggered the Investigation 

The investigation was launched after a report was lodged through Border Watch, the Australian Government's confidential online reporting platform. 

Border Watch allows members of the public to report suspected breaches of Australia's border, customs, and immigration laws, including: 

* Exploitation of visa holders 
* Misuse of employer sponsorship programs 
* Illegal work practices 
* Other suspected immigration offences 

Reports can be submitted anonymously, and where appropriate, the ABF investigates the information received. 

## What This Means for Sponsored Workers 

This enforcement action reinforces an important message for overseas workers in Australia: your employer cannot require you to pay costs that Australian migration law says they must bear. 

Sponsored workers who believe they are being asked to pay prohibited sponsorship-related costs or who suspect other sponsorship breaches can report their concerns through the Border Watch reporting system. The Australian Government treats these reports confidentially and may investigate where there is evidence of non-compliance. 

Understanding these protections is particularly important for workers holding employer-sponsored visas such as the Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) visa.
