For skilled migrants who look forward to Tasmania’s invitation updates every Thursday, the latest round brings encouraging news. At the end of October, Tasmania issued **90 invitations to apply for state nomination**, marking a higher number than in the previous rounds this month.

The latest round includes invitations for both the **Skilled Nominated visa (Subclass 190)**, a permanent residency pathway, and the **Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa (Subclass 491)**, which allows eligible skilled migrants to live and work in designated regional areas of Australia.

## Key Takeaways from Tasmania’s Latest Round

Tasmania issued **90 invitations** in its 29 October round, with 64 invitations for Subclass 190 and 26 for Subclass 491. This is a higher number than in the previous rounds this month, making it an important update for skilled migrants monitoring Tasmania’s weekly invitation activity.

Another key change is the use of a **six-week rolling average invitation point score** instead of the previous three-month average. The latest six-week averages are 326 points for Subclass 190 and 78 points for Subclass 491. These are lower than the three-month rolling averages reported in the previous round, indicating a lower recent assessment benchmark.

However, this does not guarantee invitations for candidates with lower scores. Tasmania continues to verify claimed priority attributes, and applications may be declined if applicants fail to meet the relevant requirements. Gold pass holders who do not satisfy the required Gold priority attribute will be declined regardless of their total points score.

## What Should Tasmania Applicants Do Next?

For candidates planning to apply for Tasmania state nomination, the latest round is a reminder to focus on eligibility and evidence rather than points alone.

- Review your eligibility for Subclass 190 or Subclass 491.
- Ensure that your ROI accurately reflects your circumstances.
- Verify that you meet the requirements for every priority attribute claimed.
- Prepare supporting documents to substantiate your claims.
- Monitor Tasmania's official updates rather than relying on previous invitation scores as a guarantee of future selection.
