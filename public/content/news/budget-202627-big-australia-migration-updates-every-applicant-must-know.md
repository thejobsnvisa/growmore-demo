The Australian Federal Budget 2026–27 was announced yesterday, and it has included many important updates for the country’s migration system. Starting from permanent migration planning levels to points test changes and major investments in skills recognition, this Budget sets a clear direction for Australia’s migration strategy ahead.

For skilled migrants, students transitioning to PR pathways, and applicants already in Australia, these updates will directly influence your eligibility, competition levels, and future migration opportunities.

Below is a complete breakdown of all key updates you need to know.

## 1. Permanent Migration Program Remains at 185,000 Places

The Government has confirmed that the 2026–27 permanent Migration Program will stay at 185,000 places, which splits into around 70% Skilled visa and 30% Family visa. 

The most important thing is who gets priority, and here a clear preference continues for onshore applicants, with:

* 129,590 places allocated to people already living in Australia  
* Around 55,110 places are reserved for offshore applicants who are highly skilled migrants  
* An additional 300 places are given under Special Eligibility  

This means onshore applicants have a clear advantage due to higher allocation priority, while offshore applicants can still qualify, but only if they have a strong profile with competitive points, relevant experience, and an occupation that directly matches Australia’s long-term skill shortages.

## 2. Net Overseas Migration Forecast Adjusted 

The Budget has also revised Australia’s migration intake outlook. Net Overseas Migration (NOM) is now expected to be:

* 295,000 in 2025-26  
* 245,000 in 2026–27 (increased by 20,000 from earlier forecasts)  
* 225,000 in 2027–28  

The revised figures show that Australia will continue to experience strong migration intake driven by labour demand and international mobility, particularly from New Zealand citizens and skilled workers. 

## 3. Points Test Reforms – Focus on High Skill Selection

One of the most important long-term announcements is the planned optimisation of the Points Test system.

The Government has confirmed the intent to better prioritise:

* Highly educated applicants  
* Younger candidates  
* Higher-skilled professionals contributing to productivity  

At present, almost two-thirds of skilled migrants are selected through points-tested visas, which makes this system central to Australia’s PR pathway. However, no detailed rule changes have been released yet.

## 4. Major $85.2 Million Investment in Skills Assessment & Trades Pathways

A significant part of this Budget focuses on improving how Australia recognises overseas skills and integrates migrants into the workforce faster. The Government will invest $85.2 million over four years from 2026–27, allocated across three key areas:

### **New TRA Skills Assessment System – $75.1 million**

A modernised system under Trades Recognition Australia will be developed to:

* Speed up skills assessments for trade workers  
* Link assessment outcomes more directly to occupational licensing  
* Pilot streamlined pathways for priority trades such as electricians and plumbers  
* Work closely with states and territories to reduce delays in licensing processes  

### **Onshore Skills Recognition Program – $5.6 million**

This initiative will ensure that:

* Visa holders already in Australia can have their overseas qualifications and experience formally recognised  
* Skills mismatches are reduced  
* Employers can fill workforce shortages more efficiently  

### **Strengthening Assessing Authorities – $4.5 million**

To improve system integrity, the Government will:

* Increase transparency and accountability of assessing bodies  
* Require annual performance reporting from 2027  
* Improve regulatory oversight of skills assessment organisations  

Additionally, authorities will continue consultation on the potential introduction of a Skills Migration Commissioner. Overall, this is a clear push to reduce skill recognition delays and accelerate workforce integration for migrants.

## 5. Education and Qualification Recognition Reform

The Government will also develop a National Credit Recognition Framework through the Australian Tertiary Education Commission.

This reform aims to:

* Improve recognition of VET qualifications  
* Reduce duplication in education pathways  
* Shorten study duration and costs for students progressing to university with prior vocational training  

This is a positive step for migrants who transition from VET to higher education pathways in Australia.

## 6. Working Holiday Maker Program Tightened

The Working Holiday Maker (WHM) program will also undergo reforms to improve fairness and control.

Under the new changes, Australia plans to expand visa ballot systems, improve how places are allocated, and reduce unnecessary barriers that prevent WHM visa holders from taking up jobs. 

## 7. Stronger Border Security and Compliance Measures

The Budget also places strong emphasis on enforcement, integrity, and national security.

Key allocations include:

* $270 million supplementary funding for the Australian Border Force (2026–27)  
* $207.4 million over five years to address antisemitism, extremism, and hate-related risks  
* New visa refusal and cancellation grounds under updated migration and character provisions  
* Enhanced training for immigration officers in assessing hate-related conduct  
* Implementation of the prohibited hate group listing framework  
* Introduction of the Community Safety Order Scheme that allows court-supervised or detention orders for high-risk non-citizens  
* A new detention services contract was awarded to Secure Journeys Pty Ltd  

These measures clearly indicate a stricter compliance and character-based migration environment moving forward.

## 8. Adult Migrant English Program Reform

The Adult Migrant English Program will be restructured with a new model starting 1 January 2029.

The changes will target migrants most in need of English language support, improve employment outcomes through flexible learning models, and strengthen long-term social integration outcomes.

This reflects a shift towards outcome-driven migrant settlement support rather than broad access programs.

## 9. Support and Protection Programs Extended

Two key migrant support initiatives have been extended:

### Support for Trafficked People Program

* Extended until 30 June 2027  
* Provides case-managed legal, financial, medical, and housing support  
* Available without requiring cooperation with law enforcement  

### Protecting Migrant Workers Program

* $27 million funding over two years from 2026–27  
* Focuses on workplace rights education and migration law awareness  
* Aims to reduce exploitation and improve compliance awareness among migrant workers  

## What This Budget Really Means for Applicants

Australia’s migration system is moving towards:

* Stronger preference for onshore applicants  
* Increased focus on skills quality over volume  
* Faster recognition of priority trade skills  
* More structured Australia temporary visa programs  
* Stronger compliance, character, and security screening  
* Gradual refinement of the points-based system
