*Date: June 18, 2026*

## How the Invitation Process Works

The latest Canberra Matrix invitation round held on 11 June 2026 highlights increasing competition for ACT nomination, with limited places remaining and no invitations issued to overseas applicants.

## Sharp Decline in Opportunities for Overseas Candidates

One of the most notable outcomes of this round is that no [invitations were issued to overseas applicants under either subclass 190 or 491](https://www.growmore.one/news/act-state-nomination-2025-2026-latest-round-allocation-update/). This clearly shows that ACT is continuing to prioritise candidates already residing in Canberra.

For offshore applicants, this significantly reduces short-term chances of receiving ACT nomination.

## ACT Residents Continue to Dominate Invitations

**The data confirms a strong preference for local applicants:**

* Around 88% of approvals have gone to ACT residents

* Only 12% were granted to overseas candidates

This trend suggests that working and living in Canberra remains a key advantage for securing nomination.

## Subclass 491 Pathway Nearing Closure

Another critical insight is the very low number of remaining subclass 491 places (only 8 left).

**This indicates:**

* The 491 pathway for ACT nomination is almost exhausted

* Future invitation rounds may have little to no availability under this visa subclass

Applicants targeting 491 should reassess their strategy urgently.

## High Competition in Critical Skill Occupations

**While 66 invitations were issued for subclass 190 under critical skills, this does not indicate ease of selection. In fact:**

* High-demand occupations continue to require very strong Matrix scores

* The cut-off score fluctuates based on demand and monthly allocation

## Limited Overall Allocation Remaining

With only 82 total nomination places left, ACT is approaching the end of its annual allocation.

**This means:**

* Fewer invitations in upcoming rounds

* Increased competition for remaining spots

* Higher likelihood of prioritisation criteria being applied
