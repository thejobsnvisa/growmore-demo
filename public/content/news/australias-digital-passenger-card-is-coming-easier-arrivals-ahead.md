Australia is preparing to modernise its international arrival process by replacing the familiar yellow Incoming Passenger Card with a Digital Incoming Passenger Card. The new system is designed to make the arrival process faster and more convenient while continuing to support Australia's strict immigration, customs, and biosecurity checks.

To bring this change, an investment of AUD 56.1 million will be made over four years, with the Digital Passenger Card expected to be introduced across every international airport over the next 12 to 18 months.

So, whether you are just visiting Australia or arriving on a student visa, work visa, or permanent residency visa, this change will make your arrival smoother and more efficient without compromising border security.

## What Is the Incoming Passenger Card?

The Incoming Passenger Card (IPC) is an official declaration that every traveller arriving in Australia must complete.

The declaration collects important information such as:

- Personal and passport details
- Flight and travel information
- Customs declarations
- Biosecurity declarations
- Information about food, plant material, animal products, medications, and large amounts of cash being brought into Australia

## Australia Moves Towards a Paperless Arrival Process

For decades, international travellers arriving in Australia have been required to complete the yellow paper Incoming Passenger Card before landing. The card collects essential information used by Australian Border Force and biosecurity authorities to process passengers at the border.

With the new digital system, now travellers will be able to complete the same declaration online before travelling. This will eliminate the need to fill out the paper form during the flight or upon arrival.

## How Will the Digital Incoming Passenger Card Work?

Under the new system, eligible travellers will be able to submit their Incoming Passenger Card online up to 72 hours before departing for Australia.

Once completed, passengers will receive a QR code that can be presented to border officials at the time of arrival. The QR code remains accessible even if the traveller's device is offline which makes the process more convenient.

Importantly, the questions asked in the digital version remain the same as those on the existing paper card. The only difference is the method of submission.

## Successful Trial Already Underway

Australia has already been testing the digital Incoming Passenger Card through a pilot program involving more than 450,000 Qantas passengers arriving at:

- Sydney
- Melbourne
- Brisbane

The trial began in October 2024 and has demonstrated that the digital process can streamline passenger processing without changing Australia's border requirements.

However, the trial will expand to Adelaide and Perth before the system is introduced more broadly across additional airlines and airports.

## Nationwide Rollout Planned

The digital Incoming Passenger Card will be introduced in stages over the next 12 to 18 months.

Rather than implementing the system nationwide immediately, Australia is taking a gradual approach to ensure the technology works efficiently before extending it to every international airport and airline.

Once fully implemented, the digital declaration will help manage the arrival of approximately 8.5 million international visitors each year.

## What This Means for Australian Visa Holders

If you are travelling on a:

- Visitor Visa
- Student Visa
- Skilled Visa
- Employer-Sponsored Visa
- Permanent Resident Visa

the arrival declaration remains a mandatory part of entering Australia.

The Digital Passenger Card simply changes how the information is submitted. It does not replace Australia visa requirements, passport checks, customs inspections, or biosecurity declarations.
