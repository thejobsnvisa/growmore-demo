In the most awaited National Press Club speech on 17 September 2026, Minister for Home Affairs and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship Tony Burke announced a bundle of migration reforms which are aimed at reshaping Australia's migration system and responding to key workforce needs.

As part of these reforms, the Government has introduced new Subclass 482 visa processing priorities under Ministerial Direction 121 (MD 121).

The new Direction commenced on 19 September 2026, and it gives greater processing priority to applications linked to key sectors, including healthcare, construction, teaching, agriculture and resources, as well as Australia's law enforcement and defence interests.

## What Is Ministerial Direction 121?

Ministerial Direction 121 is the new direction that determines the order in which certain temporary skilled visa applications are considered by the Department of Home Affairs.

It replaced the previous processing priorities under Ministerial Direction 119 from 19 September 2026.

Under the new structure, Subclass 482 visa applications have been separated from the broader group of skilled visas that were previously dealt with under MD 119. Other specified provisional and permanent skilled visa applications are now covered by Ministerial Direction 122.

For Subclass 482 applicants, this means the Department now considers applications according to five broad priority categories.

This Ministerial Direction 121 covers Skills in Demand visa (Subclass 482) and relevant outstanding Temporary Skill Shortage (Subclass 482) applications.

## New Subclass 482 Visa Processing Priority Order

Under Ministerial Direction 121, temporary skilled visa applications are generally considered in the following order.

### Priority 1: Key Workforce Sectors

The highest processing priority applies to visa applications relating to:

- Construction
- Healthcare
- Teaching
- Agriculture
- Aquaculture
- Fishing
- Resources
- Australia's law enforcement interests
- Australia's defence interests

Importantly, the highest priority category is not restricted to applicants who were in Australia when they lodged their application. This means eligible applicants in the specified priority sectors may receive Priority 1 treatment whether they applied from Australia or overseas.

### Priority 2: Specialist Skills Stream

The second priority category applies to Skills in Demand visa applications under the Specialist Skills stream.

The Specialist Skills stream is designed for highly skilled workers who meet the applicable occupation and salary requirements.

Read our guide to know the difference between the [Core Skills and Specialist Skills streams](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-482-streams-core-skills-vs-specialist%20skills/).

### Priority 3: Other Onshore Applications

The third priority applies to other Subclass 482 applications where the primary applicant was in Australia when the application was lodged.

This category applies where the application does not fall within one of the higher priority groups.

### Priority 4: Other Offshore Applications

The fourth priority covers applications where the primary applicant was outside Australia when the application was lodged and the application was not combined with another person's application at any time.

### Priority 5: All Other Applications

The fifth category covers remaining applications that do not fall within the first four priority groups.

The Department of Home Affairs confirms this five-level processing order under Ministerial Direction 121.

## What Is the Difference Between MD 121 and MD 119?

The new system is not simply a renumbering of the previous Direction but there are several important changes.

### Subclass 482 Has Its Own Processing Direction

MD 121 specifically covers Skills in Demand visa applications, while other specified provisional and permanent skilled visas are now covered separately under MD 122.

### More Sectors Have Been Added to the Highest Priority

The new Priority 1 category includes Construction, Healthcare, Teaching, Agriculture, Aquaculture, Fishing, Resources, Law enforcement interests, and Defence interests without distinction between onshore or offshore applications.

### Specialist Skills Has a Dedicated Priority

MD 121 introduces a separate processing priority for eligible Specialist Skills stream applications.

## What Happens to Existing Subclass 482 Applications?

One of the most important points for applicants is that the new priorities are not only for applications lodged after 19 September 2026.

MD 121 also applies to relevant Subclass 482 applications lodged before the new Direction commenced that had not been finally determined at that time.

This means applicants who are already waiting for their Subclass 482 visa decision may have their applications considered under the new priority structure.

## Current Subclass 482 Processing Position

The Department of Home Affairs is already publishing different processing positions according to the new priority categories.

According to the Department's current information, it is processing applications lodged in:

- October 2025 for Priority 1 sectors, including construction, healthcare, teaching, agriculture, aquaculture, fishing and resources, as well as law enforcement and defence interests
- July 2026 for Specialist Skills stream applications
- January 2026 for other applicants who were in Australia when they lodged their application
- October 2025 for specified offshore applications
- October 2025 for other offshore applications

The Department has specifically warned that processing timeframes may fluctuate during the transition to the new MD 121 priorities, and some published timeframes may temporarily move backwards while applications are reprioritised.

## What Does This Mean for Offshore Subclass 482 Applicants?

The changes are particularly relevant for applicants applying for a Subclass 482 visa from outside Australia.

Previously, applicants and employers often focused heavily on whether the applicant was onshore or offshore when considering processing priorities.

Under MD 121, that is no longer the only consideration.

An eligible applicant in one of the Priority 1 sectors can receive the highest processing priority even if they are outside Australia when the application is lodged.

## Worried About How Long Your Subclass 482 Visa May Take?

Australia visa processing times are a major concern for applicants, particularly when Ministerial Directions change the order in which applications are considered.

Under Ministerial Direction 121, processing priority can depend on factors such as the visa stream, nominated position and sector, applicant location at the time of lodgement and applicable priority category.

If you are waiting for your Subclass 482 visa decision, understanding where your application sits under the new priority structure is important. However, processing priority does not guarantee a specific visa decision timeframe.
