Australia’s migration system could face major changes if the Opposition’s new plan is implemented. Whether it is about scrapping Temporary Graduate visas, tightening student visa rules or reducing the number of temporary visa holders, the proposed reforms could mainly affect people who are planning to study, work or build their future in Australia.

On 6 October 2026, the Liberal and National parties, led by Opposition Leader Angus Taylor, unveiled their Australia-First Migration Plan. They believe that this plan will cut net overseas migration while lifting the standard of skilled migration.

However, these are just proposed policy changes, not a confirmed new visa rule. So, applicants should understand that the current visa requirements remain in place unless and until the relevant changes are formally implemented.

## Key Changes Proposed Under the Australia-First Migration Plan

The Opposition’s plan includes several major migration targets and visa reforms.

* **Net overseas migration:** Reduce to 100,000 annually for the first two years, reaching around 160,000 in year four.
* **Temporary visa holders:** Reduce the number by approximately 650,000 over four years, excluding visitors and New Zealand citizens.
* **Permanent skilled migration program:** Maintain a cap of 185,000 places annually.
* **International students:** Reduce student commencements to 240,000.
* **Humanitarian program:** Halve the annual intake to 10,000 places, with flexibility to respond to emerging needs.
* **Budget impact:** The Opposition estimates that the package would deliver a small positive budget impact of around $260 million over four years.

The plan proposes scrapping Temporary Graduate visas and tightening the rules for secondary applicants applying for Student visas. It also includes changes to some temporary visa applications and plans to expand selected skilled migration pathways.

## Temporary Graduate Visa and Student Visa Changes

One of the most significant proposals is to abolish the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485), which currently allows eligible international graduates to remain in Australia temporarily after completing their studies.

The Opposition proposes post-study transitional visas for selected groups, including PhD graduates and students studying health, education or at regional universities. However, detailed eligibility criteria and visa conditions for these proposed pathways have not been established in the announcement.

The plan also proposes restricting most secondary applicants for Student visas and linking each Student visa to the institution in which the student is enrolled.

These measures could affect international students planning to bring family members to Australia, as well as graduates considering their post-study options.

## Stricter Rules Proposed for Temporary Visa Holders

The Opposition wants to reduce repeated transitions between temporary visas by requiring second and subsequent temporary visa applications to be made offshore in most cases.

Second- and third-year Working Holiday Makers would remain able to reapply onshore under the proposal.

The plan also proposes reversing the Working Holiday Maker ballot system and the caps on second- and third-year visa extensions. Instead, it would differentiate between regional and non-regional applicants by increasing visa fees while exempting regional areas.

## Proposed Changes to Protection Visa Applications and Review Rights

The plan proposes requiring people applying for protection visas onshore to lodge their applications within 30 days. It also proposes removing merits review for adverse character decisions, temporary visa refusals and offshore applicants.

## Skilled Migration: More Places for Selected Workers

While the Opposition proposes reducing overall migration, it also wants to increase the focus on highly skilled workers and occupations where Australia has genuine workforce needs.

The key proposed changes include:

* **Skills in Demand visa:** Expand the Specialist Skills stream to accommodate up to 5,000 additional workers annually.
* **Skilled Project Visa:** Introduce 15,000 visas a year for workers needed for major projects, tied to a defined project and period.
* **Skilled occupation priorities:** Revise the temporary skilled migration priority list.
* **Construction workforce:** Establish a strike force to clear the backlog of skilled construction applications.
* **Talent and Innovation:** Double the allocation to 7,000 places through a proposed AUKUS and Five Eyes visa.

The Opposition also proposes prioritising skilled migrants who meet its preferred criteria for age, income, English proficiency and full-time work, including skilled partners within the permanent migration program.

## Regional Australia and Employer-Sponsored Visas

Regional migration is another area addressed in the plan.

The Opposition proposes replacing the regional permanent migration program with Ministerial Directions that prioritise employer-sponsored applications in regional Australia. It also plans to revise regional definitions used for Working Holiday Maker visa extensions and introduce a new Agriculture Visa from 2030.

For skilled workers considering regional employment, this proposal could affect how applications are prioritised if implemented.

However, the announcement does not mean regional visas or existing employer-sponsored pathways have changed immediately. Applicants should assess their options against the current requirements for the relevant visa, including employer eligibility, nominated positions, occupation requirements and other applicable criteria.

## No Proposed Changes to Family Reunion Visas

The Opposition has stated that its Australia-First Migration Plan does not propose changes to family reunion visas.

This does not mean that all family-related visa pathways would be unaffected. The proposed restrictions on most secondary applicants for Student visas are a separate measure, and their potential impact would depend on the final rules.

## When Could the Proposed Migration Changes Take Effect?

The Opposition says many of its proposed measures could be implemented quickly using existing ministerial powers, including changes affecting Temporary Graduate visas, Student visa secondary applicants, offshore applications for further temporary visas and the proposed Skilled Project Visa.

However, the announcement itself does not implement these measures. For now, visa applicants should continue to follow current immigration requirements rather than assume the proposed reforms are already in effect.
