If you are planning to migrate to regional Australia or are already on a regional visa pathway, there's an important update you should understand. The Australian Department has clarified how regional permanent planning levels are counted under the 2026–27 Migration Program, providing greater certainty for applicants, employers, and migration professionals.

The clarification explains which regional visas are included in the Government's regional planning level and why some permanent visa grants are not counted separately.

## Which Regional Visas Count Towards the Planning Level?

The Department has confirmed that the regional permanent planning level for the 2026–27 Migration Program includes the following visa subclasses:

- Subclass 491 – Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa
- Subclass 494 – Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) Visa
- Subclass 187 – Regional Sponsored Migration Scheme (RSMS) Visa

These visas make up the regional planning allocation used by the Australian Government to manage permanent regional migration outcomes.

## Why Doesn't the Subclass 191 Usually Count?

One of the most important clarifications relates to the Subclass 191 Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) Visa.

Although the Subclass 191 is a permanent residency visa, most Subclass 191 grants do not count towards the regional planning level. This is because applicants are generally counted when they are first granted their Subclass 491 or Subclass 494 visa.

In other words, the Government does not count the same applicant twice. The first time is when they receive their provisional regional visa, and again when they transition to Australia PR through the Subclass 191 visa.

## When Does a Subclass 191 Visa Count?

There is a limited exception.

A Subclass 191 visa grant will only count towards the regional planning level if the applicant has not previously been granted a Subclass 491 or Subclass 494 visa.

For example, this may occur when a new family member is added to a Subclass 191 application, as that individual was not previously counted under the regional migration program.

## Employer-Sponsored Planning Level Explained

The Department also clarified that the Employer-Sponsored planning category only includes the Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) Visa. For the 2026–27 Migration Program, the Employer Sponsored category has a planning level of 58,040 places.

This clarification makes it clear that employer-sponsored permanent visas are not counted under the regional planning allocation, as they are reported separately.

## What Does This Mean for Applicants?

This explains how the Australian Government records and reports migration flows within its annual Migration Program.

Understanding this distinction is particularly important for applicants on the Subclass 491 or Subclass 494 pathway who intend to [apply for the Subclass 191](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/difference-between-subclass-491-and-subclass-190/) Permanent Residence Visa. While progressing to permanent residence remains available for eligible applicants, the Subclass 191 grant is generally not counted as an additional regional migration place because it has already been accounted for at the provisional visa stage.
