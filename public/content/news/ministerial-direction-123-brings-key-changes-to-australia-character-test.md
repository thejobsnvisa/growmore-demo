The Australian Government has introduced a series of Ministerial Direction changes recently, reshaping how different parts of the migration system are prioritised and assessed. Adding to these changes, **Ministerial Direction 123 (MD 123)** brings a new framework for decision-makers dealing with Australia’s character test.

MD 123 was signed on **18 September 2026**, replacing MD 110, and will begin on **31 October 2026**. It provides revised guidance for decisions about visa refusal, visa cancellation, and revocation of mandatory visa cancellation, with changes in relation to criminal conduct, domestic and family violence, community expectations, minor children and ties to Australia.

## What Is Ministerial Direction 123?

Ministerial Direction 123 provides guidance to decision-makers when applying the character test under **section 501(6) of the Migration Act 1958**.

The Direction applies to decision-makers, including relevant delegates of the Minister and the **Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)**, when considering whether a visa should be refused or cancelled under section 501 or whether a mandatory visa cancellation should be revoked under section 501CA.

MD 123 sets out:

- Principles decision-makers must apply
- Primary and other considerations
- Detailed guidance on the different grounds of the character test in Annex A

The Direction replaces **Ministerial Direction 110**, with several changes to how individual circumstances are assessed.

## Four Primary Considerations Under MD 123

One of the key changes is the reduction of the primary considerations from **five to four**.

Under MD 123, the primary considerations are:

1. **Protection of the Australian community from criminal or other serious conduct**
2. **Whether the conduct involved domestic or family violence**
3. **Expectations of the Australian community**
4. **The best interests of minor children in Australia, including child victims**

The strength, nature and duration of a person's ties to Australia, which was previously a primary consideration under MD 110, has now been moved to the **"other considerations"** section.

This does not mean that a person's Australian family, social or community connections are ignored. However, they will now be considered as an **other consideration** rather than one of the four primary considerations.

## What Are the Key Changes Under Ministerial Direction 123?

MD 123 makes a number of changes to the way character matters are considered. Some are technical updates, while others change the factors decision-makers will consider when assessing a person's circumstances.

### Decision-Maker Definition Updated

MD 123 removes the reference to the **Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT)** from the definition of a decision-maker. The Direction now refers to the **Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)**.

### Domestic and Family Violence Definition Expanded

The definition of domestic and family violence has been expanded.

It now includes a wider range of conduct, such as:

- Image-based abuse
- Stalking
- Breaching court orders

It also covers a broader range of relationships, including caregiving relationships and cultural family groups.

### New Principle on Migration Rule Compliance

MD 123 adds a new principle about compliance with Australia's migration rules.

It states that the community expects non-citizens to follow migration rules and leave Australia when their permission to remain has ended. The Direction also recognises that most non-citizens comply with these requirements.

### Ties to Australia Moved to Other Considerations

Under MD 110, the strength, nature and duration of a person's ties to Australia was a primary consideration.

Under MD 123, this has been moved to the **other considerations** section.

### Changes to Seriousness of Conduct

MD 123 adds **hate crime conduct** and **drug or property offences where the sentence is five years or more** to the conduct viewed very seriously.

The Direction also removes the previous exclusion of certain offence categories from the sentence-weighting factor. The sentence imposed can now be considered across all offence types.

### Changes for Temporary Visa Holders

The guidance for temporary visa holders has also been updated.

MD 123 removes the previous guidance about considering the duration and purpose of an intended stay and compassionate grounds for a short-stay visa.

The new guidance focuses on:

- The person's availability for removal
- The possibility of using risk-based visa conditions

### Impact on Victims and Witnesses

MD 123 adds a requirement to consider the effect of domestic or family violence on victims and witnesses, particularly where a minor child is involved.

### Community Expectations Expanded

The community expectations consideration now includes:

- Hate speech
- Incitement of violence or discord
- Related advocacy

The Direction also states that this consideration applies whether the non-citizen is expected to remain in Australia on a temporary visa or holds another visa intended to facilitate their removal.

### More Detailed Factors for Children's Best Interests

MD 123 changes the way the best interests of minor children in Australia are considered.

The previous factor covering family violence exposure and abuse has been divided into four separate factors. These cover:

- Current and future risks of abusive or neglectful conduct
- Current and future exposure to domestic or family violence

The types of abuse covered have also been expanded to include:

- Psychological abuse
- Economic abuse

### Australian Business Interests Removed

MD 123 removes the impact on Australian business interests from the other considerations.

This means business or employment impacts are no longer included as a factor under this part of the Direction.

### Visa Consequence Guidance Updated

The new Direction removes references to the **Bridging R (Class WR) visa exception** and the Minister's discretion under sections **195A and 197AB**.

The Bridging R exception was created under regulation 2.12AA of the Migration Regulations 1994. That regulation was later repealed, so the exception no longer exists.

### Changes to Removal Impediments

MD 123 adds guidance for people who cannot be removed to their home country.

Where a person has permission to enter a third country, decision-makers can consider any impediments they may face in that country.

For a person who has already returned to their home country, impediments there can also be considered. The Direction states that less weight should be given where the person returned voluntarily.

### Future Conduct Risk Threshold Changed

The future conduct provisions in Annex A have also been updated.

The wording has changed from a risk that a person **"would" or "will"** engage in the relevant conduct to a risk that the person **"might"** engage in it.

### New Character Test Ground

MD 123 adds guidance on a new character-test ground under **section 501(6)(baa)**.

It covers:

- Membership of or association with a terrorist organisation
- Association with a state sponsor of terrorism
- Association with a prohibited hate group
- Hate crime conduct
- Making, endorsing or encouraging hateful public statements

## What Does MD 123 Mean for Visa Applicants and Visa Holders?

The changes under Ministerial Direction 123 are particularly relevant to people involved in **section 501 visa refusal or cancellation matters** and applications to revoke mandatory visa cancellation under **section 501CA**.

The Direction does not mean that every person with a criminal history, family violence issue or other character concern will automatically have their visa refused or cancelled. The outcome of a character decision depends on the applicable legislation, the relevant Direction and the individual circumstances of the case.
