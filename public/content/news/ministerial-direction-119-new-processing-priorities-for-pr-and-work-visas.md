Will your visa be processed faster to get Australia PR? It might, if your occupation is present on Australia's new visa priority list. The Australian Government has introduced a new visa processing priority system under Ministerial Direction 119 (MD 119) that outlines how certain skilled visa applications will be processed. 

The biggest takeaway? Not all applications will be treated equally. Applicants working in Australia's priority sectors could see their visa applications processed ahead of others. So, if you are applying for Australia PR or an employer-sponsored visa, this change could influence when your application is considered for processing. 

So, let's find out whether Australia's new skilled visa priorities work in your favour or not. 

## What is Ministerial Direction 119? 

Ministerial Direction 119 (MD 119) is a new direction issued under Section 499 of the Migration Act 1958. It determines the order in which certain skilled visa applications and employer nominations are considered by the Department of Home Affairs. 

It commenced after being signed by the Minister for Home Affairs and replaces Ministerial Direction 105, which had been in place since December 2023. 

Importantly, MD 119 does not create new visa requirements or change Australia's Skilled Occupation Lists. It just changes the priority order in which eligible visa applications are processed. 

## What is Australia's New Skilled Visa Priority Order? 

Under Ministerial Direction 119, both employer nomination applications and skilled visa applications will generally be processed in the following order: 

**Highest Priority**

Highest priority is given to applications related to Australia's law enforcement or defence interests where the applicant or nominee is in Australia at the time of application. 

**Second Priority**

Second priority is given to applications related to Australia's law enforcement or defence interests where the applicant or nominee is outside Australia at the time of application. 

**Third Priority**

Third priority is given to applications for healthcare, teaching, and construction occupations where the applicant or nominee is in Australia at the time of application. These occupations have been prioritised to help address Australia's critical workforce needs. 

**Fourth Priority**

Fourth priority is given to all other nomination and skilled visa applications where the applicant or nominee is in Australia at the time of application. 

**Least Priority** 

The least processing priority is given to all other nomination and skilled visa applications where the applicant or nominee is outside Australia at the time of application. 

In short, the priority order is like:


*  Onshore Defence and Law Enforcement Occupations 
*  Offshore Defence and Law Enforcement Occupations 
*  Onshore Healthcare, Teaching and Construction Occupations 
*  Other Onshore Skilled Applicants 
*  Other Offshore Skilled Applicants 

## Which Australian Visas Are Affected? 

Ministerial Direction 119 applies to several popular Australian skilled visas, including: 
* Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme Visa 
* Subclass 482 Skills in Demand Visa 
* Subclass 482 Temporary Skill Shortage Visa applications still being processed 
* Subclass 494 Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional Visa 
* Subclass 189 Skilled Independent Visa 
* Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated Visa 
* Subclass 191 Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) Visa 
* Subclass 491 Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa 
* Subclass 489 Skilled Regional Visa 
* Subclass 887 Skilled Residence Visa 
* Subclass 888 Business Innovation and Investment (Permanent) Visa 
* Remaining Subclass 187 applications 

The Direction applies to both new applications and those already lodged but not yet finally determined.

## Defence and Law Enforcement Occupations 

**Defence occupations** include roles related to AUKUS Pillar I (Conventionally Armed, Nuclear-Powered Submarines) and AUKUS Pillar II (Advanced Capability Development), as well as the following occupations when nominated or sponsored by the Australian  

Defence Force (ADF) or the Department of Defence: 
* ANZSCO Unit Group 4411 – Defence Force Members (Other Ranks) 
* 111212 – Defence Force Senior Officer 
* 139111 – Commissioned Defence Force Officer 
* 139211 – Senior Non-Commissioned Defence Force Member 

**Law enforcement occupations** include ANZSCO Unit Group 4413 – Police when nominated or sponsored by Australia's recognised police forces, including the Australian Federal Police and State and Territory Police services. 

## What Does This Mean for Skilled Visa Applicants? 
Ministerial Direction 119 does not change visa eligibility requirements, but it could affect how quickly certain applications are considered for Australia PR. Applicants working in priority occupations such as healthcare, teaching, construction, defence, and law enforcement may benefit from higher processing priority, particularly if they are already in Australia.  

Understanding where your occupation falls under the new priority framework can help you make more informed decisions about your Australian migration plans.
