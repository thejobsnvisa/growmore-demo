Skilled migration applicants received a double update on 4 June 2026, as Australia has conducted a new Skilled Independent (Subclass 189) invitation round along with Tasmania's final state nomination invitation round for the 2025–26 program year. These invitations have created fresh opportunities for onshore and offshore skilled workers who are pursuing permanent residency in Australia.

In this [latest Subclass 189 invitation round](https://www.growmore.one/news/is-your-eoi-ready-subclass-189-invitation-round-confirmed-for-4-june-2026/), candidates are invited across several occupations, including healthcare, construction trades, science, engineering, and education.

## **Healthcare Occupations Continue to Receive Strong Support**

Healthcare occupations continued to feature prominently in reported invitations, including Registered Nurses across multiple specialisations, Occupational Therapists, Speech Pathologists, Medical Laboratory Scientists, General Practitioners, and Nuclear Medicine Technologists.

Most healthcare-related occupations were reported in the 80–85 points range, indicating sustained selection pressure in line with ongoing demand across hospital, aged care, disability, and community health sectors.

## **Trades Occupations Remain in Demand**

Trades-related occupations also continued to receive invitations across a lower-to-moderate points range in several cases. Reported outcomes include Carpenters at 65 points, along with Bricklayers, Solid Plasterers, Electricians, Welders, Metal Fabricators, Cabinetmakers, Glaziers, and Wall and Floor Tilers.

These occupations continue to remain in demand as Australia faces ongoing workforce shortages across housing construction, major infrastructure projects, and regional areas.

## **What This Means for Skilled Migration Applicants**

The latest reported data continues to reflect occupation-based selection under SkillSelect, where invitations are influenced by a combination of points score and Date of Effect priority rather than points alone.

Applicants with identical points may experience different outcomes depending on submission timing and ranking position within the pool. In several occupations, the Date of Effect continues to play a decisive role in selection priority.

Applicants who have not yet received an invitation should reassess their profile rather than wait passively. In many cases, improving English scores, gaining additional skilled experience, or restructuring claims in the EOI can make a measurable difference.

However, applicants can also explore the Skilled Nominated (Subclass 190) visa, the Skilled Work Regional (Subclass 491), or an [employer-sponsored visa](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/employer-sponsored-visa/) to significantly improve their chances of securing permanent residency.
