# Australian Permanent Residency Pathways for Qualified Hairdressers

If you are a qualified Hairdresser and dreaming of Australian Permanent Residency, there is good news: you may not necessarily need to spend years moving from one temporary visa to another.

Hairdresser is an eligible skilled occupation for Australian migration pathways, and there are two important pathways that can potentially lead directly to Permanent Residency:

* **Subclass 186 Direct Entry visa**
* **Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated visa**

There are also employer-sponsored pathways through the **Subclass 482 Skills in Demand visa** and **Subclass 494 Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional visa**. However, these are temporary/provisional visas rather than direct permanent visas.

So, if PR is your ultimate goal, let's look at the two pathways that deserve particular attention.

## Pathway 1: Subclass 186 Direct Entry – Employer-Sponsored Direct PR

The **Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186) – Direct Entry stream** is a permanent visa. It allows a skilled worker who is nominated by an eligible Australian employer to live and work permanently in Australia.

For a Hairdresser, this can be an extremely attractive pathway because it is **not a points-tested visa**. You do not need to wait for a state invitation or compete on points in the same way as a Subclass 190 applicant.

### What Does a Hairdresser Generally Need for 186 Direct Entry?

For most applicants, the important requirements include:

### 1. Relevant Hairdressing Qualification

You need appropriate qualifications relevant to the occupation and must be able to obtain the required positive skills assessment.

For Hairdressers, the qualification and skills assessment requirements need to be carefully considered based on where and how your qualification and experience were obtained.

### 2. Competent English

You generally need at least **Competent English** for the Subclass 186 Direct Entry stream.

### 3. At Least Three Years of Relevant Work Experience

Unless an exemption applies, the Subclass 186 Direct Entry stream requires at least **three years of relevant work experience** in the nominated occupation.

Your overseas experience can potentially count. This means that someone who has qualified and worked as a Hairdresser outside Australia should not automatically assume that they need to come to Australia and work for another three years before becoming eligible.

### 4. Positive Skills Assessment

Most Direct Entry applicants must have a **positive skills assessment** for their nominated occupation before lodging the visa application, unless an exemption applies.

### 5. Employer Nomination

This is the critical part.

You need an eligible Australian employer willing to nominate you for a genuine Hairdresser position. The occupation must also meet the applicable skilled occupation requirements for the 186 Direct Entry stream.

### 6. Age Requirement

Generally, you must be **under 45 years of age** when you apply, unless an applicable exemption is available.

If you meet the relevant requirements and find a suitable sponsoring employer, **Subclass 186 Direct Entry can provide PR directly** rather than requiring you to first hold a 482 visa.

## Pathway 2: Subclass 190 – State-Nominated Direct PR

The second major pathway is the **Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated visa**.

This is also a permanent visa, but unlike the 186 visa, you don't need an employer to sponsor you. Instead, you need nomination from an Australian state or territory government.

For Hairdressers who cannot secure employer sponsorship, this can be a very important alternative.

### What Do You Need for a 190 Visa?

Generally, you will need:

* A suitable skills assessment for Hairdresser
* Competent English or the applicable English requirement
* To be under 45 at the time of invitation
* An eligible Expression of Interest (EOI)
* Nomination from an Australian state or territory
* At least **65 points** in total to meet the visa threshold

Importantly, nomination for a **Subclass 190 visa gives you 5 additional points**.

For example, if you have **60 points** based on your own circumstances, state nomination can bring your total to the required 65 points.

But remember: **65 points is only the minimum eligibility threshold. It does not guarantee an invitation.**

Each state and territory has its own nomination requirements and determines which applicants it wishes to nominate according to its workforce needs and migration program.

## Why Should Hairdressers Consider Their PR Options Now?

Hairdressing remains a significant trade occupation in Australia, with Jobs and Skills Australia reporting approximately **65,400 employed Hairdressers nationally**.

For eligible Hairdressers, the important point is that you may have more than one migration strategy available.

You might qualify for **employer-sponsored direct PR through the 186 Direct Entry visa**.

Alternatively, your **age, English, qualifications, experience and points** may make the **190 Skilled Nominated visa** a better option.

And where direct PR is not immediately available, the **482 or 494 employer-sponsored pathways** may provide another strategy towards establishing yourself in Australia and potentially progressing towards permanent residence later.

## Qualified Hairdresser? Don't Assume You Have to Wait Years for PR

Many Hairdressers focus only on temporary employer sponsorship without first checking whether they may already be positioned for a direct permanent pathway.

If you have a relevant Hairdressing qualification, good English and substantial work experience, particularly **three years or more**, it may be worth assessing whether the **186 Direct Entry or 190 visa pathway** could work for you.

> **Important:** Australian visa requirements, occupation lists, skills-assessment rules, state nomination criteria and eligibility requirements can change. Always verify the current requirements before making a migration decision.
