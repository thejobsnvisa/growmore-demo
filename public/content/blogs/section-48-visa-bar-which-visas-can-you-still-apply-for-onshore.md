Receiving a visa refusal or cancellation can feel extremely stressful when you are already in Australia and have already built a plan around staying here. All of a sudden, you start asking yourself the following question: “Is there still a possibility to make another visa application?”

The answer is yes, but the options to apply for another visa become limited. Because if section 48 bar applies to you, you may be prevented from applying for most visas while living in Australia.

However, there are certain visa applications that may still be available onshore. So, it is important for you to understand when the Section 48 visa bar applies and what visa options are still open if it applies.

## What Is the Section 48 Visa Bar and When Does It Apply?

Section 48 of the Migration Act 1958 states that a person in Australia shall not be eligible to apply for further visas in case of visa refusal or cancellation.

There are three primary criteria that should be satisfied simultaneously for the Section 48 visa prohibition to take effect.

- You are in Australia.
- You do not hold a substantive visa. A substantive visa is a main visa, such as a student or visitor visa. A bridging visa is not a substantive visa.
- Since your last arrival in Australia, a visa has been refused or cancelled.

However, not every visa refusal or cancellation triggers the Section 48 bar. The type of refusal or cancellation and the relevant provisions under the Migration Act need to be considered.

For example:

- A refused bridging visa does not trigger the Section 48 bar.
- Refusals under sections 501, 501A or 501B, which relate to character grounds, are dealt with separately.
- Only certain visa cancellations can lead to section 48 applying. They include the cancellations made under sections 109, 116, 133A, 133C, 134, 137J, and 137Q.
- The cancellation made under section 501 cannot be considered the type of cancellation which activates section 48.

## Which Visas Can You Still Apply for Under Section 48?

This is where the section 48 visa bar becomes more complicated than many applicants expect. Regulation 2.12 of the Migration Regulations 1994 lists the visa types that may still be available to someone affected by section 48.

Some of the most relevant include:

## What About Subclass 494?

Subclass 494 deserves specific attention because sometimes it is missed in general explanations of section 48 bar.

There is a separate regulation of the Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) visa, subclass 494, mentioned in Regulation 2.12. It means that section 48 does not stop a person from applying for a subclass 494 visa.

However, the applicant shall fulfil the general criteria of subclass 494. This includes having a sponsor and satisfying all the visa conditions.

This is a good example of why you should not assume that every employer-sponsored pathway is closed just because section 48 applies.

## Does an ART Review Remove the Section 48 Bar?

No. Simply applying for review does not mean the original refusal disappears. The Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) can review eligible decisions, but the fact that a decision is under review does not automatically mean that section 48 no longer applies.

This creates an important distinction:

> Review of the original refusal and making another visa application are two different strategies.

If your refusal is reviewable and you are still within the applicable review period, you should consider the review option before rushing into another visa application.

## Does Leaving Australia Remove the Section 48 Bar?

Leaving Australia can remove the onshore Section 48 restriction, as Section 48 applies to certain visa applications made while you are in Australia.

However, leaving Australia is not a reset button. Your previous refusal or cancellation remains part of your immigration history, and any new visa application must still meet its requirements.

If you hold a bridging visa, leaving Australia can also have consequences for your visa status.

So, do not leave Australia simply because someone says it will “fix” a Section 48 issue. Get advice from a registered migration agent in Australia before making any decision.

## FAQs

### 1. What is the Section 48 visa bar in Australia?

The Section 48 visa bar can restrict certain people in Australia from making another visa application after a relevant visa refusal or cancellation.

### 2. Which visas can I apply for under the Section 48 visa bar?

Certain visas may remain available under Regulation 2.12, including Subclass 190, 491, 494, Partner visa 820 and Child visa 802, subject to eligibility requirements.

### 3. Can I apply for Subclass 494 visa if Section 48 applies?

Yes. Section 48 does not automatically prevent an eligible applicant from applying for a Subclass 494 visa, but all Subclass 494 requirements must still be met.

### 4. Does an ART review remove the Section 48 visa bar?

No. Applying for an Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) review does not automatically remove the Section 48 visa bar. Review rights depend on the specific decision.

### 5. Can I apply for an Australian visa after leaving Australia under Section 48?

Leaving Australia can remove the onshore Section 48 restriction, but it does not erase your previous visa refusal or cancellation. Any new visa application must meet its relevant requirements.
