Applying for Australian PR is not just about submitting documents and achieving enough points. The immigration authority carefully reviews each case to determine whether you genuinely meet every legislative requirement for the visa you have applied for and whether the evidence you provide supports every claim made in your application.

But the thing is, all mistakes are avoidable if you prepare your application precisely with the right strategy. So, let’s look at the most common Australia PR application mistakes and how you can avoid them to improve your chances of a successful outcome.

- State or Territory nomination
- Regional skilled migration

## Mistake 2: Claiming Incorrect Points in Your Expression of Interest (EOI)

Many skilled migration visas, including [Subclass 189](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/how-to-apply-for-subclass-189-visa-get-australia-pr), [Subclass 190](https://www.growmore.one/news/tasmania-weekly-invitation-round-42-invitations-issued-in-latest-round/), and [Subclass 491](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-491-visa-guide-your-path-to-australia-pr), are assessed under Australia's points-tested migration system. No matter higher points help you in getting an invitation, but each point you claim must be supported by valid evidence.

**Applicants commonly overclaim points for:**

- Skilled employment
- Overseas work experience
- Australian work experience
- Educational qualifications
- English language proficiency
- Partner skills
- Regional study
- Professional Year programs

### **How an RMA Can Help You Avoid This Mistake**

An RMA carefully reviews your [points calculation](https://www.growmore.one/points-calculator) against current migration requirements and verifies whether you have sufficient evidence for every claim. This reduces the risk of overclaiming points and helps ensure your EOI accurately reflects your eligibility.

## Mistake 3: Submitting Incomplete or Inconsistent Supporting Documents

One of the most common reasons for delays in Australia PR applications is submitting incomplete or inconsistent supporting documents. All information you have in your application, including your work experience, qualifications, English language proficiency, or relationship status, must be supported by appropriate evidence.

Even small inconsistencies, such as different employment dates, mismatched job titles, incorrect personal details, or missing documents, can lead to requests for additional information or further assessment.

### **How an RMA Can Help You Avoid This Mistake**

A Registered Migration Agent thoroughly reviews your supporting documents to ensure they meet immigration requirements, identify any gaps or inconsistencies, and verify that every claim is supported by appropriate evidence. This reduces the risk of delays and helps strengthen your Australia PR application.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring State or Territory Nomination Criteria

If you're applying for Australia PR through the Subclass 190 or Subclass 491 visa, meeting the visa requirements alone may not be enough. Every Australian state and territory has its own nomination criteria, and these requirements can change throughout the year.

For example, a state may prioritise certain occupations, require applicants to have specific work experience, or ask for evidence of commitment to living and working in that state. Some nomination programs may also open and close without much notice.

Ignoring state-specific requirements or failing to stay updated with nomination criteria can significantly reduce your chances of receiving a state nomination.

### **How an RMA Can Help You Avoid This Mistake**

A Registered Migration Agent stays updated with the latest state and territory nomination requirements and helps identify which state best matches your profile. They can also guide you on meeting specific nomination criteria and preparing a stronger application for state nomination.

## Mistake 5: Relying on Outdated or Unverified Information

Australia's migration program changes regularly. Occupation lists, invitation rounds, points requirements, state nomination criteria, and visa policies can all change based on Australia's workforce needs and government priorities.

Many applicants rely on information shared on social media, online forums, or by friends who migrated several years ago. While these experiences may be genuine, they may no longer reflect the current immigration requirements.

### **How an RMA Can Help You Avoid This Mistake**

A Registered Migration Agent provides advice based on the latest Australian immigration policy updates, not based on outdated or unverified information. They assess your eligibility against current requirements, recommend the right Australia PR pathway, and ensure your application complies with the latest immigration criteria.
