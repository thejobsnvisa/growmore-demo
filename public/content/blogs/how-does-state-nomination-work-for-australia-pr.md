## What is State Nomination for Australia PR?

The General Skilled Migration program of Australia has three main skilled visas: the Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189), the Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190), and the Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa (Subclass 491).

Although the Subclass 189 is an independent visa subclass, both the Subclass 190 and Subclass 491 need nomination by an Australian state or territory government.

State nomination is the procedure where the Australian state or territory nominates your application because your skills match its labor needs.

Each state has its own list of occupations and eligibility criteria depending on local labour market demand. For example, NSW may need construction workers, South Australia may require healthcare professionals, while Tasmania may be looking for Chefs.

If your occupation matches a state's current requirements, you may receive a nomination that allows you to apply for either the Subclass 190 or [Subclass 491 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-491-visa-guide-your-path-to-australia-pr/). In addition to opening these visa pathways, state nomination also awards extra points in the SkillSelect system that improves your chances of receiving an invitation to apply.

## How Does the State Nomination Process Work?

Now that you understand what state nomination is and why it plays such an important role in Australia's General Skilled Migration program, let's look at how the process actually works. Whether you are applying for a Subclass 190 or Subclass 491 visa, the process follows a similar path.

### **Step 1: Check Your Eligibility**

Before you start preparing for documents make sure that you fulfil all the eligibility criteria that comes under [Australia's skilled migration program](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/gsm-general-skilled-migration).

**Apart from that, all states and territories also have their own nomination criteria which applicants must meet. Some general requirements include:**

- Being under the eligible age limit
- Having an occupation listed on the relevant Skilled Occupation List
- Receiving a positive Skills Assessment
- Meeting the required English language standard
- Achieving the minimum points required under the Skilled Migration Points Test

> **Quick Tip:** Don't choose a state simply because someone else received a nomination there. Every state has different priorities, and the best option depends on your occupation, experience, and personal circumstances.

### **Step 2: Obtain a Positive Skills Assessment**

Obtaining positive skills assessment is one of the most important parts for every skilled visa. Different occupations are assessed by different assessing authorities, and each has its own requirements and processing times. So, based on your qualification and occupation obtain a positive skills assessment before moving forward with EOI submission.

This positive skills assessment helps in confirming that your qualifications and work experience meet Australian standards for your nominated occupation.

### **Step 3: Submit Your Expression of Interest (EOI)**

Once you meet the eligibility requirements, you can submit your [Expression of Interest (EOI)](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/queensland-skilled-migration-program-2025-26-state-nomination-guide/) through Australia's SkillSelect system. Your EOI includes information such as:

- Your nominated occupation
- Skills Assessment details
- English language test results
- Work experience
- Educational qualifications
- Points claimed
- Preferred visa subclass
- States or territories you wish to be considered for nomination

Remember, an EOI is not a visa application; it allows the Australian Government and participating states and territories to assess your profile for skilled migration opportunities. It remains valid for up to two years from the date of submission.

### **Step 4: Submit Your Registration of Interest (ROI) or State Nomination Application**

Some Australian states require applicants to submit a Registration of Interest (ROI) or a separate state nomination application after lodging their EOI.

**Requirements vary between states and may include:**

- Work experience requirements
- Priority occupations
- Residency requirements
- Employment or job offer requirements
- Commitment to live and work in the nominating state

Some states automatically consider eligible EOIs, while others require applicants to actively submit an ROI or nomination application. For example, Queensland requires an ROI for both onshore and offshore applicants, WA requires no ROI for either, while South Australia requires an ROI only for onshore applicants.

### **Step 5: Receive State Nomination and Invitation**

If your profile meets the state's requirements, you may receive state nomination.

**Once nominated:**

- Subclass 190 applicants receive an additional 5 points.
- Subclass 491 applicants receive an additional 15 points.
- Your SkillSelect profile is updated automatically.
- You may then receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) for your visa.

### **Step 6: Lodge Your Visa Application**

After receiving your invitation, you can submit your visa application along with all the required supporting documents.

**This includes:**

- Passport documents
- Skills Assessment document
- English language test results
- Employment evidence
- Educational qualifications
- Health examinations
- Character documents

Providing complete and accurate documentation can help avoid unnecessary delays during visa processing.

**In short, it is like:**

**Eligibility Check → Skills Assessment & English Test → EOI Submission → State Nomination Process (ROI/Application, if required) → Invitation to Apply → Visa Application**

## Common Reasons State Nomination Applications Are Not Selected

Many applicants assume that meeting the minimum requirements guarantees nomination. Unfortunately, that is not always the case.

**Some common reasons for non-selection include:**

- Occupation ceilings being reached
- Limited nomination allocations
- Lower points compared to other applicants
- Incomplete or inaccurate EOI information
- Failure to meet state-specific requirements
- Changes to state nomination priorities during the migration year

Strategic planning often makes a significant difference when choosing the right visa pathway and state nomination program.

## FAQs

### **1. How do I apply for state nomination in Australia?**

Get a positive Skills Assessment, submit an EOI through SkillSelect, and apply for state nomination if required. If nominated, you may receive an invitation to apply for your visa.

### **2. How can I get state nomination for the Subclass 190 visa?**

You must meet both the Australian Government's visa requirements and the nomination criteria of the relevant state or territory. If nominated, you will receive an additional 5 points towards your skilled migration score.

### 3. **What is the difference between the Subclass 190 and Subclass 491 visas?**

The Subclass 190 visa grants permanent residency, while the Subclass 491 visa is a regional provisional visa that provides a pathway to permanent residency.

### 4. **Do all Australian states require a Registration of Interest (ROI)?**

No. ROI requirements vary between states and territories. Some require an ROI or a separate nomination application, while others consider eligible EOIs directly.

### 5. **How many additional points do I receive through state nomination?**

State nomination provides 5 additional points for the Subclass 190 visa and 15 additional points for the Subclass 491 visa.
