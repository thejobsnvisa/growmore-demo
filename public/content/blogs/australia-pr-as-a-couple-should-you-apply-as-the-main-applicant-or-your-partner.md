“Should I be the main applicant for Australia PR, or should my partner?” We get this question many times from our clients, and if you are planning for Australia PR as a couple, then you may also have the same question in mind. 

But it is not always right to choose the partner with higher points because maybe you have better English, but your partner has a more relevant occupation. Maybe your partner has more work experience, but you have a better chance of meeting a state's nomination criteria. Or perhaps one of you has a genuine employer-sponsored opportunity that the other does not. 

This is why it becomes important to assess both profiles completely based on the Australian visa pathways available. So, let’s look at the key factors that you and your partner should consider before making a decision about Australian PR. 

## Start by Comparing Both Profiles Completely, Not Just the Points 

It is important to compare both partners against some important factors before you decide who should be the main applicant.  

Here are some of the key factors: 

| Factor | What to Look At |
| --- | --- |
| Age | Who can claim more age-related points? |
| Occupation | Is the occupation eligible for the visa and relevant state nomination pathways? |
| English | Does either partner have an English level that could improve the points score? |
| Skills assessment | Can both partners obtain a positive skills assessment? |
| Qualifications | Do the qualifications support the nominated occupation? |
| Work experience | Who has stronger relevant skilled employment? |
| Points | What is the final points score for each partner? |
| State nomination | Which partner has more suitable state or territory options? |
| Employer sponsorship | Does either partner have a genuine employer-sponsored opportunity? |
| Current visa | Does either partner have visa conditions or expiry dates that affect the strategy? |  

## Match Your Profile to the Right Visa Pathway 

Once you have compared all the above key factors for both partners, now you also need to look for suitable visa options depending on your unique circumstances. 

## Subclass 189: Skilled Independent Visa 

Subclass 189 is a points-tested visa that does not require any type of state or territory nomination, and the invitation comes solely based on your points. To apply for this visa, you need to compare partners on points, occupation, English, skills assessment and skilled employment.  

If one partner has a clearly stronger 189 profile with a higher points score, then they might be the better main applicant. 

## Subclass 190: Skilled Nominated Visa 

The comparison of main applicant changes for subclass 190 because this visa requires state or territory nomination. 

Each state or territory has its own nomination criteria, which may consider occupation, work experience, English, employment, residence and other factors. So, the partner with fewer points may still have the stronger 190 pathway if their profile better matches a state's current criteria. 

## Subclass 491: Skilled Work Regional Visa 

If your family is ok to live and work in regional Australia, subclass 491 may provide another option. For couples, do not assess this pathway based only on points or the potential pathway to subclass 191. Consider whether both partners are comfortable with regional living, including employment, housing and longer-term family plans. 

If one partner has a stronger 491 profile and the regional requirements work for your family, they may be the more suitable main applicant. 

However, the pathway from subclass 491 to permanent residence through subclass 191 is not automatic. You must meet the applicable eligibility requirements when applying. 

## Employer-Sponsored Visa Pathway 

Apart from skilled visa options, there may be employer-sponsored visa options available for a couple to get Australian PR.  If one partner has a job offer from an employer that can sponsor them, then you can also consider it.  

For example, one partner might have points but not be able to get invitations, while the other has an employer who is willing to sponsor them. In that case, look at the employer-sponsored option compared to other migration options. 

## Can Your Partner Help You Get More Points as the Main Applicant? 

Yes, sometimes your partner's profile can help add extra points to your application and make the whole application stronger. However, this depends on the visa requirements and the partner-related criteria you meet. 

For example, the points test for relevant skilled visas may allow you to claim partner points based on factors such as your partner's English proficiency, skills assessment and nominated occupation, depending on the applicable criteria. In some cases, a partner's English ability alone may contribute points, while other claims require additional conditions to be met. 

## Common Mistakes Couples Make 

**Choosing the main applicant before comparing both profiles**

You may overlook a stronger pathway available through your partner. 

**Looking only at the points score** 

Points matter, but occupation, nomination opportunities and employer-sponsored options can also influence the strategy. 

**Assuming the highest qualification should lead the application** 

A higher qualification does not automatically mean a stronger migration profile. 

**Ignoring the second partner's occupation** 

Your partner may have another viable skilled or employer-sponsored pathway that is worth assessing. 

**Using outdated state nomination information** 

State and territory criteria can change, so an occupation that was eligible previously may not necessarily be eligible under current settings. 

## So, Who Should Be the Main Applicant? 

There is no universal answer for every couple. The better approach is to compare both partners against the visa pathways that are realistically available to you. 

One partner may be stronger for subclass 189. The other may have better prospects for subclass 190 or 491. An employer-sponsored opportunity may change the entire strategy again. 

That is why the decision should be based on the overall migration pathway, not just who has the highest points. 

Before lodging an application, make sure you understand which partner has the stronger eligibility, which visa pathway is realistically available and how the choice fits your family's longer-term plans.

## FAQs 

**1. Who should be the main applicant for Australia PR as a couple?**

The partner with the strongest overall visa pathway should generally be considered the main applicant. Compare both partners' age, points, occupation, English proficiency, skills assessment, work experience, state nomination options and employer-sponsored opportunities before deciding. 

**2. Can my partner and I both apply for Australia PR separately?** 

Depending on your eligibility, both partners may be able to pursue their own skilled migration pathways. Assessing both profiles can help you compare the available options and decide which strategy is more suitable for your family. 

**3. Does the partner with more points have a better chance of getting Australia PR?** 

Not necessarily. A higher points score can strengthen an application for a points-tested visa, but it does not guarantee an invitation. Occupation, invitation settings, state nomination criteria and other eligibility requirements can also influence which pathway is more suitable. 

**4. Can I claim additional points based on my partner's skills or English?** 

Yes, partner-related points may be available under the relevant skilled migration points test if the applicable requirements are met. Depending on the criteria, these may relate to your partner's English proficiency, skills assessment and nominated occupation. Check the current requirements before claiming these points. 

**5. Which Australia PR visa should couples consider: subclass 189, 190 or 491?** 

The right visa depends on both partners' profiles and circumstances. Subclass 189 does not require state nomination, subclass 190 requires state or territory nomination, and subclass 491 is a provisional regional pathway. Compare your eligibility and realistic opportunities under each option before choosing the main applicant.
