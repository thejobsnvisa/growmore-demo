**What Is the Subclass 186 Visa?** 

The [Subclass 186 visa grants permanent residency](https://www.growmore.one/important-change-in-186-trt-experience-requirements-dont-miss-out/) to skilled workers who have been nominated by an approved Australian employer. It is specifically designed for highly skilled workers in in-demand occupations in Australia. 

**The visa has three distinct streams, and understanding which one applies to your situation is the first critical step**: 

### **1\. Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) Stream** 

If you have worked for your sponsoring employer on a Subclass 482 (TSS) or Subclass 457 visa for at least two years in the same nominated occupation, you may be eligible for this stream. It is the most commonly used pathway for existing temporary visa holders in Australia. 

### **2\. Direct Entry (DE) Stream** 

This stream is for skilled workers who have never held a TSS or 457 visa, or have not met the two-year requirement. Applicants must have their skills assessed by the relevant authority and must meet the points test or other requirements as applicable. 

### **3. Labour Agreement Stream** 

This stream applies to workers employed under a [labour agreement negotiated between an Australian](https://www.growmore.one/types-of-labour-agreements-in-australia-which-one-is-right-for-you/) employer and the Department of Home Affairs. It is less common and is used in specific industries where standard visa arrangements do not apply. 

Each stream has its own eligibility requirements, documentation needs, and processing timelines. This is where most self-applicants go wrong — assuming one pathway fits all. 

**Why Is the Subclass 186 Visa Application So Complex?** 

The Department of Home Affairs has strict and specific requirements for both the employer (as the nominating party) and the applicant. Consider some of the critical compliance areas: 

*   Employer eligibility and business turnover requirements 

*   Occupation compatibility with [ANZSCO codes](https://www.growmore.one/points-calculator/) 

*   Genuine position assessment for the nominated role 

*   Salary benchmarking against the Annual Market Salary Rate (AMSR) 

*   Health and character checks for all applicants and dependants 

*   English language proficiency evidence 

*   Skills assessment certificates (for Direct Entry applicants) 

*   Time-sensitive nomination and visa application submissions 

A single error — a wrong ANZSCO code, an expired skills assessment, an insufficient payslip, or a missing statutory declaration — can result in a refusal or significant delays. The stakes are high, and the consequences of mistakes are long-lasting.

**Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)**

****Q1: How long does the Subclass 186 visa take to process?****

Processing times vary depending on the stream and individual circumstances. The TRT stream typically processes faster — often within 6 to 12 months — while the Direct Entry stream can take longer, particularly if a skills assessment is required. Your consultant can provide a realistic timeline based on your specific situation. 

****Q2: Can my family members be included in my Subclass 186 application?**** 

Yes. Eligible family members, including a spouse or de facto partner and dependent children, can be included as secondary applicants. They will also need to meet health and character requirements. Your visa consultant will advise you on what documentation is required for each family member. 

****Q3: Do I need a skills assessment for the TRT stream?****

In most cases, no formal skills assessment is required for the TRT stream. However, some occupations may still require one. Your consultant will clarify this based on your nominated occupation and individual history.

****Q4: What if my employer is not an approved sponsor?****

If your employer is not yet an approved Standard Business Sponsor (SBS), they will need to apply for sponsorship approval before nominating you. A Subclass 186 visa consultant can assist your employer with this process concurrently to avoid delays.

****Q5: Can I change employers after lodging my Subclass 186 application?****

This is a complex situation, and it is strongly advised that you speak to your consultant immediately if your employment circumstances change during the processing period. The nomination is tied to a specific employer, and a change in employment may affect your application.

****Q6: What is the age limit for the Subclass 186 visa?****

Applicants must generally be under 45 years of age at the time of application. There are limited exemptions for certain high-value occupations. Your consultant can assess whether any exemptions apply to your situation.

****Q7: Is the Subclass 186 visa a pathway to Australian citizenship?**** 

Yes. Once you are granted the Subclass 186 visa, you will be a permanent resident of Australia. After meeting the residency requirements — typically four years of living in Australia, including at least one year as a permanent resident — you may be eligible to apply for Australian citizenship.
