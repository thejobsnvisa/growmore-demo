Many visa holders on employer-sponsored visas feel trapped when their employment situation changes. This is especially common where an employer reduces hours, stops providing work, or tells the employee that they may be reported to immigration if they leave.

This creates fear and confusion. However, the important point is this: a 482 visa holder may have options, but those options must be handled carefully and in accordance with visa condition 8607.

## What is Condition 8607?

Condition 8607 applies to many employer-sponsored visa holders, including Subclass 482 visa holders.

This condition requires the visa holder to work only in the occupation nominated in their most recently granted 482 visa. A visa holder cannot work in a different occupation unless a new Skills in Demand subclass 482 visa is applied for and granted.

The condition also generally requires the visa holder to work only for the business that nominated them, unless an exemption applies. In some cases, if the sponsoring employer is an Australian business sponsor, the visa holder may also work for an associated entity of the sponsor, depending on the relevant stream and circumstances.

## Changing Employer on a 482 Visa

A 482 visa holder cannot simply move to a new employer in the same way as an ordinary employee.

If a visa holder wants to change employer, the new employer must lodge a nomination application. The visa holder should not start working for the new employer before the required nomination process has been properly lodged and managed in accordance with the visa conditions.

This is one of the most common areas where mistakes happen. Many people think that because they have found a new sponsor, they can start work immediately. That is not the correct approach.

The change must be planned properly.

## What Happens If Employment Ends?

If the employment with the sponsor ends, the visa holder is generally permitted to stop working, or work outside the usual sponsor-related condition, for:

* up to 180 days in a single period; and  
* up to 365 days in total during the visa grant period.  

These periods are important because they may allow the visa holder time to find a new sponsor, arrange a new nomination, consider another visa pathway, or make appropriate plans.

However, this period should not be treated casually. The visa holder must still be careful about their visa conditions, work rights, occupation requirements, and future visa strategy.

## You Must Still Work in the Nominated Occupation

One of the most important parts of condition 8607 is the nominated occupation requirement.

Even where a visa holder has time after employment ends, they must be very careful not to work in a role that is inconsistent with their nominated occupation or visa conditions.

For example, if a person was granted a visa as a chef, they should not assume they can start working in a completely different occupation simply because they are between sponsors.

Any work arrangement must be reviewed carefully before the visa holder takes action.

## Licensing, Registration and Membership Requirements

Condition 8607 also requires the visa holder to hold any mandatory licence, registration or membership required for the nominated occupation.

If the occupation cannot legally be performed without a licence, registration or membership, the visa holder must not perform that work unless they hold the required authority.

The visa holder must also notify the Department in writing as soon as possible if the licence, registration or membership ends, is refused, cancelled or revoked.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Some common mistakes include:**

* assuming the visa holder can work for any employer during the 180-day period;  
* starting work with a new employer before the new nomination is properly lodged;  
* working in a different occupation without considering the visa condition;  
* ignoring licensing or registration requirements;  
* resigning without understanding the practical and visa consequences;  
* relying only on general information without seeking advice for the specific situation.  

These mistakes can create unnecessary risk and may affect future visa applications.

## Impact on Permanent Residency Planning

Changing employment may also affect a person’s overall migration planning.

This does not mean every employer change destroys a permanent residency pathway. However, it may affect the timing, strategy, employer support, nomination options, and evidence required for future applications.

For this reason, visa holders should obtain proper advice before making major employment decisions, especially if they are planning to apply for permanent residency in the future.

## Final Thoughts

Changing employer on a 482 visa is possible, but it is not just a normal job change. It is a visa-related process that must be handled carefully.

If your employment has ended, or if your employer has told you that they no longer have work for you, do not panic. There may be options available. However, the next step should be planned properly.

A well-managed transition can protect your visa position. A rushed or incorrect decision can create unnecessary complications.
