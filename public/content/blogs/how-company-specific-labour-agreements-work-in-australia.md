If you are an Australian employer struggling to fill specialised positions, or a skilled overseas worker looking for sponsorship opportunities outside standard visa pathways, a Company Specific Labour Agreement may provide an alternative solution. 

This arrangement allows businesses to sponsor overseas workers under customised conditions where genuine skill shortages exist, and standard migration programs are not suitable.

Unlike broader industry or regional agreements, Company Specific Labour Agreements are available for the specific workforce needs of an individual business. Employers must demonstrate strong recruitment efforts within Australia, while overseas workers must meet the required skills, qualifications, and licensing standards linked to the role.

## What is a Company Specific Labour Agreement?

A Company Specific Labour Agreement is an agreement between an Australian employer and the Australian Government that allows the business to sponsor skilled overseas workers under negotiated conditions.

**This pathway is generally considered when:**

* The required occupation is not available on the standard skilled occupation lists  
* No Industry Labour Agreement applies to the business  
* No Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) is available in the region  
* The employer can demonstrate a strong and compelling business case  

These agreements are created based on the specific workforce needs of an individual business.

## When Can a Business Apply for a Company Specific Labour Agreement?

**A business may consider this option where:**

* There is a genuine skill shortage  
* Recruitment efforts undertaken in Australia have not been successful  
* The required occupation involves niche or specialised skills  
* Existing visa pathways are unsuitable for the role  

However, this pathway is not intended to replace standard employer-sponsored visas where those options are already available.

## Key Requirements for Company Specific Agreements

**Businesses must demonstrate:**  **Genuine Skill Shortages**

**Employers need to provide evidence such as:**

* Detailed job descriptions  
* Recruitment efforts in Australia  
* Reasons Australian workers could not fill the position  
* Evidence of niche or specialised skill requirements  

### **Workforce Planning**
\
**Businesses must also show:**

* Overseas workers will not exceed one-third of the workforce  
* They have plans to train and employ Australians long term  

### **Business Eligibility**
\
**Employers must:**

* Be legally operating in Australia  
* Have operated for at least 12 months  
* Show financial viability  
* Have no adverse compliance history  
* Not be insolvent  

### **Skill and Occupation Requirements**
\
The positions requested under the agreement generally must fall within the Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations (ANZSCO) Skill Levels 1 to 4.

In limited and exceptional circumstances, businesses located in Category 3 regional areas may also request occupations equivalent to ANZSCO Skill Level 5.

**Overseas workers sponsored under the agreement must usually:**

* Meet the required ANZSCO skill standards  
* Hold relevant qualifications and work experience  
* Meet industry licensing or registration requirements where applicable  
* Satisfy English language requirements for the [Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482)](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/a-complete-guide-for-skills-in-demand-visa-subclass-482/)

## Can Employers Request Visa Concessions?

One of the unique features of company-specific labour agreements is that employers may request concessions to certain standard visa requirements.

**Potential concessions may relate to:**

* English language requirements  
* Age limits  
* Salary thresholds  
* Work experience requirements  

However, concessions are assessed carefully and require strong justification. The Department generally does not approve concessions that would create unfair employment conditions compared to Australian workers performing equivalent roles.

## Considering a Company Specific Labour Agreement in Australia?

Businesses considering a Company Specific Labour Agreement should first assess whether the occupation, salary package, and any proposed concessions are likely to meet current Department requirements. Applications supported by weak recruitment evidence or unclear business justification may face delays or lower chances of approval.

As labour agreements are assessed case by case, proper planning before lodging the request is important. Seeking guidance from a MARA [registered agent Australia](https://www.growmore.one/) (MARN: 2217941) can help employers better understand sponsorship obligations, required documentation, and whether a labour agreement is the right pathway for their workforce needs.

## FAQs

### **1. Who can apply for a Company Specific Labour Agreement in Australia?**

Australian businesses experiencing genuine skill shortages may apply if standard employer-sponsored visa pathways are not suitable and no industry agreement or DAMA applies to their situation.

### **2. Can a business request occupations that are not on the skilled occupation list?**

Yes. One of the key reasons businesses use Company Specific Labour Agreements is to request occupations that may not be available under standard skilled migration programs.

### **3. Are employers allowed to request concessions under a labour agreement?**

In some cases, yes. Employers may request concessions related to age, English, salary, or work experience requirements, but strong justification and supporting evidence are required.

### **4. Does a Company Specific Labour Agreement lead to permanent residency?**

Some labour agreement pathways may provide access to permanent residency options depending on the occupation, visa stream, and agreement conditions. However, this is assessed case by case.

### **5. Can small businesses apply for a Company Specific Labour Agreement?**

Yes. Small businesses may apply if they can demonstrate genuine labour shortages, financial capacity, lawful operations, and a strong business case for sponsoring overseas workers.
