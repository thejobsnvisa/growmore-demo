It is common for applicants to compare their Subclass 189 points with others and expect similar results when the scores match. But in reality, the outcome can still be different, where one applicant receives an invitation while another remains waiting.

That’s because SkillSelect does not only consider your points score; it also evaluates when you reached that score and how your Expression of Interest (EOI) is positioned in the live ranking queue.

Even a small difference in timing or updates to your profile can change your place in the order of selection.

To understand this properly, let’s take an example of one competitive occupation: Software Engineer. Two applicants have submitted EOI for the [Subclass 189 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/australian-visa-processing-times-update-february-2026-vs-april-2026/), and both of them have 90 points, positive skills assessment, similar qualifications, and similar overseas work experience. So, at first glance, both profiles look almost identical.

**But during the invitation round, only one receives an invitation.**

**Why?**

**The answer is: EOI ranking and date of effect.**

## How SkillSelect Actually Ranks Applicants

Australia’s SkillSelect system works like a live ranking queue. Before receiving an [invitation for Subclass 189](https://www.growmore.one/news/is-your-eoi-ready-subclass-189-invitation-round-confirmed-for-4-june-2026/), applicants must first submit an Expression of Interest (EOI). The system then ranks EOIs based on the information provided, including:

1. Age

2. English language ability

3.  Skilled work experience

4. Educational qualifications

5. Partner points

6. Other eligible factors

Here, generally, higher points rank above lower points. But when multiple applicants hold the same points score, SkillSelect uses a tie-break system called the “date of effect.” This means the applicant who achieved that score earlier is usually given priority.

So, if you improve your points by upgrading your English score, adding skilled work experience, claiming partner points, or obtaining NAATI points and update your EOI accordingly, your date of effect will be reset to the date those changes are submitted.

## Why One Software Engineer Received an Invitation, and the Other Did Not

Now, let’s go back to the two Software Engineer applicants.

### **Profile 1**

* Reached 90 points in November 2025

* Submitted the updated EOI immediately

* Maintained the same score since then

### **Profile 2**

* nitially had 85 points

* Improved English score later

* Reached 90 points in March 2026

* Updated EOI after the score increase

Both applicants now hold 90 points. But their “date of effect” is different. The first applicant entered the 90-point queue months earlier, which means the system ranks that EOI ahead when invitations are limited.

**As a result:**

* Profile 1 receives an invitation

* Profile 2 continues waiting despite having the same points

## Why 65 Points Does Not Mean You Are “Close”

One of the important parts of subclass 189 is meeting a minimum points requirement. For Subclass 189, Subclass 190, and [Subclass 491 visas](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/australian-visa-processing-times-update-february-2026-vs-april-2026/), 65 points is simply the eligibility threshold. It only allows you to enter the competition.

**It does not guarantee an invitation. A simple way to understand this is:**

* 65 points means you entered the race

* Higher-ranked applicants in the invitation pool are prioritised for selection

In competitive occupations like Software Engineer, Accountant, ICT Business Analyst, or Developer Programmer, the competition can become extremely crowded. That means invitation scores often move much higher than the minimum requirement.

## Occupation Demand Also Impacts Invitation Chances

Not all occupations move at the same speed. Some occupations receive thousands of EOIs every year, while others have significantly lower competition.

Which means 85 points may be enough in one occupation, while in another occupation, 95 points also stay on the borderline. That is why comparing your profile with someone from another occupation often creates unrealistic expectations.

## Why Waiting Only for Subclass 189 Can Be Risky

For highly competitive occupations, relying only on Subclass 189 can sometimes slow down migration plans significantly. Applicants are also advised to plan for alternative pathways, including:

* Subclass 190 state nomination

* Subclass 491 regional pathways

* Employer-sponsored visa options

So, if you are planning to [apply for Australia PR](https://www.growmore.one/) through Subclass 189 or any other skilled migration pathway, professional guidance can help you assess your real ranking position, identify practical point improvement opportunities, and avoid critical EOI errors that may negatively impact your invitation chances later.


## FAQs

### 1. **Why do two applicants with the same points get different results?**

Because SkillSelect also considers ranking position and the date you reached your points, not just the score.

### 2. **What is “date of effect” in SkillSelect?**

It is the date your latest points level is recorded after an EOI update, and it affects your ranking in the queue.

### 3. **Does improving points always increase the chances of an invitation?**

Yes, but only if your new score also improves your ranking compared to others in the pool.

### 4. **Is 65 points enough to get an invitation?**

No, 65 points only makes you eligible. Invitations usually go to much higher-ranked applicants.

### 5. **Why is waiting for Subclass 189 risky?**

Because high-demand occupations can take longer, so many applicants also consider 190, 491, or employer-sponsored options.
