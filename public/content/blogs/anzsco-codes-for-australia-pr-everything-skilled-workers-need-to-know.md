If you're exploring Australia PR as a skilled worker, you've probably come across the term "ANZSCO Code." It may look like just a number, but it plays a major role in determining whether your occupation is recognised for skilled migration and which visa pathways may be available to you.

Every skilled occupation in Australia is assigned an ANZSCO Code that describes the role, required qualifications, and typical job duties. In many cases, selecting the wrong occupation code can directly impact your skills assessment outcome, visa eligibility, and even your invitation chances.

In this guide, we explain what ANZSCO codes are, why they matter for Australia PR, and how to find the correct code for your occupation.

## **What is an ANZSCO Code?**

ANZSCO stands for the Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations. It is the occupation classification system used by Australia to group jobs based on skill level, qualifications, responsibilities, and typical tasks performed in the workplace.

Every occupation is assigned a unique six-digit ANZSCO code. For example, 254499 is used for Registered Nurse, while 261313 is used for Software Engineer.

## **What are the ANZSCO Skill Levels?**

ANZSCO occupations are divided into different skill levels based on the qualifications, training, and work experience typically required to perform the job.

### **Skill Level 1**

Skill Level 1 occupations generally require a bachelor's degree or higher qualification. In some cases, up to 5 years of relevant experience may be considered as an alternative pathway.

**Common examples include:**

* General Practitioner
* Accountant (General)
* Registered Nurse

### **Skill Level 2**

These occupations usually require an advanced diploma, associate degree, or diploma-level qualification with the Australian Qualifications Framework.

**Common examples include:**

* Construction Manager
* Paramedic
* Enrolled Nurse

### **Skill Level 3**

Skill Level 3 occupations generally require a Certificate III or Certificate IV qualification, often combined with at least 2 years of on-the-job training. In some cases, 3 years of relevant experience may be accepted instead.

**Common examples include:**

* Chef
* Electrician
* Carpenter

### **Skill Level 4**

These occupations typically require a Certificate II or Certificate III qualification or at least one year of relevant experience.

**Common examples include:**

* Retail Supervisor
* Security Officer
* Truck Driver

### **Skill Level 5**

Skill Level 5 occupations generally require secondary education, short-term training, or entry-level experience.

**Common examples include:**

* Cleaner
* Labourer
* Kitchen Hand

## **Australia ANZSCO Occupation Lists**

ANZSCO codes work alongside Australia's occupation lists, which identify occupations that may be eligible for [skilled migration programs](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/general-skilled-visa/).

**The main occupation lists include:**

* **Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL):** Includes occupations critical to Australia’s long-term workforce needs and generally offers stronger PR pathways.
* **Short-term Skilled Occupation List (STSOL):** Covers occupations needed for short-term demand, with limited or conditional PR pathways depending on visa subclass.
* **Regional Occupation List (ROL):** Focuses on occupations in demand in regional Australia, supporting regional migration pathways.
* **Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL):** Used mainly for employer-sponsored visas and reflects current labour market shortages under the Skills in Demand framework.

## **How to Find Your ANZSCO Code**

Finding the correct ANZSCO code is not about guessing your job title. It’s about accurately matching your actual job duties with the official Australian occupation classification.

**Follow these steps:**

### **Step 1: Visit the Official Website**

Go to the [Australian Department of Home Affairs Skilled Occupation List](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/working-in-australia/skill-occupation-list) or [ANZSCO classification portal](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/anzsco-australian-and-new-zealand-standard-classification-occupations) to start your search.

### **Step 2: Search Your Occupation**

Enter your job title or relevant keywords in the search bar. Try different variations of your role if needed.

### **Step 3: Match the Closest Occupation**

Go through the search results and select the occupation that most closely matches your actual job duties, not just your designation.

### **Step 4: Review Job Description Carefully**

**Check the official occupation description, including:**

* Key responsibilities
* Required qualifications
* Core tasks performed in the role

Make sure your experience aligns with most of these duties.

### **Step 5: Confirm and Note Your Code**

Once you identify the correct match, note down the six-digit ANZSCO code. This will be used in your skills assessment, Expression of Interest (EOI), and visa application.
