A relationship breakdown can be difficult enough on its own. When one partner’s Australian visa or visa application depends on the other person, it can also create serious immigration questions.

What happens next depends heavily on the type of visa involved, whether the visa has already been granted, whether the person is a primary or secondary applicant, and the individual circumstances of the relationship.

## Relationship Breakdown During a Partner Visa Application

If you have applied for an Australian Partner visa, such as the Subclass 820/801 or Subclass 309/100, you generally need to notify the Department of Home Affairs if your relationship with your sponsor ends.

The Department specifically provides a Notification of Relationship Cessation process through ImmiAccount. If you do not have access to ImmiAccount, notification can generally be made through the Partner Processing Enquiry Form together with the relevant supporting information.

However, a relationship ending does not necessarily mean that the Partner visa application must automatically end.

Depending on the circumstances, an applicant may still be eligible to continue with the Partner visa process. This may include situations where:

- the applicant or an eligible family member has experienced domestic or family violence;
- the sponsoring partner has died; or
- there is a child of the relationship and the relevant requirements concerning parental responsibility/access are satisfied.

These are important exceptions and should be assessed carefully before an applicant takes any action to withdraw their visa application.

## What About Student, 485, 482 and Other Temporary Visas?

Relationship changes can also have immigration consequences where one partner is included as a dependent or secondary applicant on another person’s temporary visa.

For example, this may arise where a person holds or has applied for a visa as the spouse or de facto partner of the primary applicant under a:

- Student visa;
- Temporary Graduate (Subclass 485) visa;
- Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) visa; or
- another temporary visa.

The Department expressly requires visa applicants and visa holders to notify it of relevant changes in circumstances, and its relationship-change guidance specifically covers dependants on Student visas and other temporary visas, including Subclasses 482 and 485.

## Does the Primary Applicant Lose Their Visa?

Generally, where the relationship ends and the former partner was included as a secondary applicant or dependent, the relationship breakdown does not by itself mean that the primary applicant’s own visa or visa application is cancelled or refused.

The more significant immigration issue usually arises for the secondary applicant whose eligibility was based on their relationship with the primary applicant.

If the secondary applicant’s visa application is still being processed, the Department will need to consider whether that person remains eligible to be included in the application.

If the person already holds a visa based on being a member of the primary visa holder’s family unit, the consequences can be different. A relationship breakdown does not necessarily cause the visa to disappear immediately. The Department may consider whether cancellation action is appropriate and, where applicable, provide the visa holder an opportunity to respond.

This distinction is extremely important because the consequences depend on whether the person has an undecided visa application or an already-granted visa.

## What If the Former Partner Is Outside Australia?

Where a secondary applicant is outside Australia and their visa application has not yet been decided, the end of the qualifying relationship may affect whether they continue to satisfy the requirements for inclusion as a family member.

The Department should be notified of the change so that it can determine the appropriate outcome for that applicant.

## What If the Former Partner Is Already in Australia?

If the former partner is in Australia, it is particularly important not to assume that they automatically become unlawful as soon as the relationship ends.

Their immigration status depends on the visa they currently hold and what action, if any, the Department subsequently takes.

If the Department considers cancelling an existing visa, procedural fairness requirements may apply and the visa holder may be given an opportunity to provide information or respond before a cancellation decision is made.

If a visa is ultimately cancelled and the person does not hold another visa that permits them to remain in Australia, they may become an unlawful non-citizen. This can have serious consequences and urgent migration advice should be obtained.

## Don’t Ignore a Relationship Breakdown

One of the biggest mistakes is simply doing nothing.

The Department of Home Affairs states that changes in circumstances, including changes to a relationship, need to be notified because they may affect a visa or visa application.

At the same time, applicants should not automatically withdraw an application or assume that their visa pathway has ended without first understanding whether an exception or another visa option may be available.
