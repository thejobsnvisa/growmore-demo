Working as a cook or chef in Australia is one of the most popular career pathways among skilled migrants. With ongoing demand across restaurants, hotels, cafés, and regional hospitality businesses, many skilled workers also see this occupation as a long-term Australia PR opportunity.

However, to get the benefit of this job market, applicants need to demonstrate that their experience and qualifications align with Australian occupation standards through an approved skills assessment. This assessment is a mandatory step for most skilled and employer-sponsored visa pathways and is used to verify whether an applicant’s skills match the requirements set under the ANZSCO occupation framework.

So, if you are planning to work as a cook or chef in Australia, let’s understand [how the skills assessment process works](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/steps-to-crack-the-skills-assessment-for-a-management-consultant-in-australia/) and what steps are required to move forward with your PR pathway.

## Cook vs Chef: Why This Difference Matters

In Australia’s hospitality sector, Chefs and Cooks are classified as two separate occupations under ANZSCO, and their roles differ mainly in responsibility level, planning authority, and kitchen management involvement.

### **Cooks (ANZSCO 351411) - Skill Level 3**
&nbsp;
Cooks are responsible for preparing, seasoning, and cooking food in dining and catering establishments.

**Their main duties include:**

* Examining foodstuffs to ensure quality  
* Regulating temperatures of ovens, grills, and cooking equipment  
* Preparing and cooking food  
* Seasoning food during cooking  
* Portioning food, plating dishes, and adding sauces or garnishes  
* Storing food in temperature-controlled facilities  
* Preparing food to meet special dietary requirements  
* May plan menus and estimate food requirements  
* May train other kitchen staff and apprentices  

### **Chefs (ANZSCO 351311) - Skill Level 2**
&nbsp;
Chefs are responsible for planning, organising, and overseeing the preparation and cooking of food in dining and catering establishments.

**Their main duties include:**

* Planning menus and organising food production  
* Estimating food and labour costs  
* Ordering food supplies  
* Monitoring quality of dishes at all stages of preparation and presentation  
* Discussing food preparation issues with managers, dietitians, kitchen and waiting staff  
* Demonstrating techniques and advising on cooking procedures  
* Preparing and cooking food  
* Explaining and enforcing hygiene regulations  
* May select and train staff  
* May freeze and preserve foods  

## Basic Qualification Requirements

**While requirements may vary slightly depending on the occupation and assessment pathway, common criteria include:**

### **For Cooks:**
&nbsp;
* An AQF Certificate III including at least two years of on-the-job training  
* Or an AQF Certificate IV  
* Or an equivalent overseas qualification  

Compared to chefs, cooks are generally assessed more on food preparation and operational kitchen duties rather than overall kitchen management and planning responsibilities.

### **For Chefs:**
&nbsp;
* An AQF Associate Degree, Advanced Diploma, or Diploma  
* Or an equivalent overseas qualification considered comparable to Australian standards  

## Skills Assessment Authority for Cook and Chef

Australia generally requires a positive skills assessment from an authorised assessing body for most skilled migration pathways. For both Cook and Chef occupations, Trades Recognition Australia (TRA) is the primary skills assessment authority.

TRA assesses whether an applicant’s qualifications, employment history, and practical skills meet Australian industry standards for the nominated occupation.

TRA offers several programs based on different visa types and applicant locations. Here, some may qualify through a Migration Skills Assessment (MSA), while others may need the Offshore Skills Assessment Program (OSAP) or the Job Ready Program (JRP) depending on their specific circumstances. Depending on the applicant’s country, qualification background, and visa pathway, the assessment may involve:

* Qualification verification  
* Employment verification  
* Technical interviews  
* Practical assessments  
* Documentary evidence review  

## Skills Assessment Process Step-by-Step

### **1. Choose the Correct Occupation**

Identify whether your experience aligns with Chef (ANZSCO 351311) or Cook (ANZSCO 351411), as TRA assesses both occupations differently based on duties, qualification level, and responsibility.

### **2. Prepare Required Documents**

Gather documents such as your passport, qualifications, employment references, payslips, tax documents, and resume that clearly support the claimed occupation duties under ANZSCO standards.

### **3. Choose the Right TRA Skills Assessment Pathway**

Applicants must apply under the correct TRA program based on their visa pathway, country, and experience level, such as MSA, OSAP, TSS, or JRP pathways.

### **4. Lodge the Skills Assessment Application**

Submit the application through the TRA Online Portal, upload all required documents, and pay the applicable assessment fee.

### **5. Complete Interview or Practical Assessment (If required)**

Depending on the pathway, applicants may need to attend a technical interview or practical cooking assessment to verify genuine industry skills and experience.

### **6. Receive the Skills Assessment Outcome**

Successful applicants receive a positive skills assessment outcome that can be used for skilled migration, employer-sponsored visas, and [Australia PR](https://www.growmore.one/) pathways.

## FAQs

### **1. Can I apply as a Cook without formal qualifications?**

Formal qualifications are usually important for TRA assessment. However, strong and properly documented work experience may help in some cases depending on the assessment pathway.

### **2. Is overseas work experience accepted for skills assessment?**

Yes. TRA accepts overseas experience if it matches the nominated occupation and is supported with genuine employment evidence.

### **3. Is Chef considered higher skilled than Cook?**

Yes. Chef is classified at a higher ANZSCO skill level and usually involves menu planning, supervision, and kitchen management responsibilities.

### **4. Can I move from a 482 visa to PR later?**

Yes. Many Cooks and Chefs first enter Australia on a [Subclass 482 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-482-visa-consultant-in-australia/) and later become eligible for [PR pathways like Subclass 186](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-186-visa-consultant-for-pr-success/).

### **5. Why do some skills assessments get rejected?**

Common reasons include incorrect occupation selection, weak employment evidence, unclear job duties, or documents that do not match TRA requirements.
