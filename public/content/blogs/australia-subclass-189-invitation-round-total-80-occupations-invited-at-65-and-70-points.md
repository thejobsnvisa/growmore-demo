Australia's latest Subclass 189 invitation round issued 20,000 invitations to skilled migration candidates, making it a significant round for applicants seeking an invitation for Australian permanent residency. 

The round was conducted on 15 October 2026, and 159 occupations are listed on the Department's website with their respective minimum invitation scores. These scores range from 65 to 100 points. Of these, 44 occupations received invitations at 65 points, while another 36 received invitations at 70 points. 

The results are particularly notable for trade professionals, construction workers, healthcare practitioners and some other skilled occupations that received invitations at these point thresholds. 

Want to know which occupations received invitations at 65 and 70 points? Here is a closer look at the results.

In the October 2026 Subclass 189 invitation round, 44 occupations received invitations at the lowest recorded score of 65 points. This category mainly includes trade and technical occupations across Australia's building, construction, electrical, mechanical and plumbing sectors. 

Occupations such as Carpenter, Bricklayer, Electrician (General), Plumber (General), Cabinetmaker and Wall and Floor Tiler were among those invited at 65 points. 

However, invitations at this score were not limited to trade professionals. Several professional occupations, including Solicitor, Economist, Educational Psychologist, Environmental Consultant and Valuer, also received invitations.

Applicants should therefore assess their invitation prospects based on the results for their nominated occupation rather than relying on the overall points threshold. 

## What Do the Tie-Break Dates Tell Us About the Invitation Results? 

Tie-break dates determine the ranking of candidates who have the same points score. The Department uses the date an EOI reached that score to decide which candidates receive invitations first. 

The October 2026 results show significant differences in tie-break dates across occupations, even when they share the same minimum invitation score. Some 65-point occupations had October 2026 cut-offs, while several 70-point nursing occupations had dates in November 2025. 

## What Should Applicants Do After the 15 October 2026 Invitation Round? 

Applicants who did not receive an invitation should assess their EOI based on their nominated occupation, points score and date of effect. 

The results also indicate opportunities for candidates in construction and healthcare, as several occupations from these sectors received invitations in the October round. Candidates working in these fields may have a valuable opportunity in future rounds, depending on their occupation, points score and the Department's selection priorities. 

Applicants should consider improving their points through English language proficiency, eligible skilled employment, additional qualifications or partner points. Subclass 190 and Subclass 491 nomination pathways may also be worth considering. 

Any changes to an EOI must be supported by valid evidence, and applicants must meet the relevant skills assessment and visa eligibility requirements.
