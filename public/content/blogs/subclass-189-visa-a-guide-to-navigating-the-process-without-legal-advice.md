## **Subclass 189 Visa: A Guide to Navigating the Process Without Legal Advice**

The Subclass 189 visa, also known as the Skilled Independent Visa, is a popular option for those looking to migrate to Australia permanently. This visa is for skilled workers who are not sponsored by an employer or family member and do not have a state or territory sponsorship.

While the process of [applying for a Subclass 189 visa](https://growmore.one/our-services/individual/general-skilled-visa/) can be complex, it is possible to navigate it without legal advice. In this blog post, we will provide an overview of the process and tips for a successful application.

### **1. Eligibility criteria:** The first step in the process is to determine if you meet the eligibility criteria for the Subclass 189 visa. This includes factors such as age, qualifications, work experience, and English language proficiency. You can [check your eligibility on the Department of Home Affairs](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/) website.
.
### **2. Points test:** Once you have determined that you are eligible, you will need to pass a points test. This test assesses your skills, qualifications, and work experience against a set of criteria. You can [calculate your points](https://growmore.one/points-calculator/) on the Department of Home Affairs website.
.
### **3. Expression of Interest:** If you pass the points test, you will need to submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) through Skill Select. This is a system that allows the Australian government to assess your skills and qualifications, and determine if you are a suitable candidate for the Subclass 189 visa.
.
### **4. Invitation to Apply:** If your EOI is successful, you will be issued an invitation to apply for the Subclass 189 visa. This is a formal invitation to submit a visa application, and you will have 60 days to do so.
.
### **5. Visa application:** Once you receive an invitation to apply, you will need to gather all of the necessary documents and information to support your application. This includes proof of your qualifications, work experience, and English language proficiency, as well as financial and character documents.
.
### **6. Processing time:** The processing time for a **Subclass 189 visa** can vary depending on several factors, including application volumes and how complex your case is. Some files move smoothly; others drag on because of missing documents or unclear claims. You can (and should) always check the latest processing times on the Department of Home Affairs website—anything else is just guesswork.
\.
