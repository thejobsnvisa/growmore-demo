## Western Australia Designated Area Migration Agreement (WA DAMA): A Complete Guide

Western Australia is experiencing strong demand for skilled workers, and to help businesses overcome this workforce shortage, the Australian Government introduced the Western Australia Designated Area Migration Agreement (WA DAMA). 

This agreement allows approved employers in WA to sponsor overseas workers for occupations that are not easily filled locally. In simple terms, it acts as a bridge between employers who are struggling to find staff and skilled migrants who are looking for opportunities in Western Australia. 

If you want to understand how eligibility, occupations, and sponsorship work under WA DAMA, keep reading the full blog below. 

## What Is the Western Australia DAMA? 

The Western Australia DAMA (WA DAMA) commenced in July 2024 due to strong employer demand to fill critical job roles across WA. The Department of Training and Workforce Development acts as the Designated Area Representative (DAR) and manages employer endorsements for WA DAMA. 

It operates under a labour agreement framework and allows approved employers to access overseas workers for identified skill shortages. 

The agreement covers two designated categories: 

## Category 2 – Metropolitan Perth 

This includes eligible businesses operating within specified Perth metropolitan postcodes. (6000 to 6038, 6050 to 6083, 6090 to 6182, 6208 to 6211, 6214 and 6556 to 6558) 

## Category 3 – Regional Western Australia 

Businesses operating outside the metropolitan Perth postcode boundaries fall under Regional WA.  

The annual allocation of WA DAMA places is shared equally, with 50% allocated to Category 2 and 50% allocated to Category 3. 

## Which DAMAs Are Available in Western Australia 

In Western Australia, several DAMA arrangements operate across different regions, including: 
* Goldfields (transitioning into the WA DAMA from 1 July 2026)  
* Kimberley  
* Pilbara  
* South West  
* Western Australia DAMA 

Each DAMA operates under separate regional agreements depending on local labour market needs. 

## How Does the Western Australia DAMA Work? 

The WA DAMA follows a structured endorsement process before an employer can sponsor an overseas worker. Here is the stepwise process: 

**Step 1: Check Eligibility** 

Confirm the business is operating in a WA DAMA area, the occupation is listed, and basic requirements are met. 

**Step 2: Register Through the Migration WA Portal** 

Create a profile and register with the Skilled Migrant Employment Register (SMER). 

**Step 3: Identify a Suitable Skilled Worker & Perform LMT**

Meet all skills, experience, licensing, and visa requirements. Conduct required Labour Market Testing (LMT) to prove no local worker is available.

**Step 4: Prepare Supporting Documents** 

Get ready business, job, and worker evidence in English, subject to verification. 

**Step 5: Submit the Endorsement Application** 

Once all process is done, submit the application in the Migration WA Portal. As of now, there are no fees to submit your endorsement application, and processing time is about 20 working days for decision-ready applications. 

**Step 6: Receive the Outcome** 

If your application is approved, a Letter of Endorsement is issued and is valid for 12 months. If refused, reasons are provided in the outcome. 

**Step 7: Apply for a Labour Agreement** 

After endorsement, a Labour Agreement is lodged with Home Affairs before moving to the visa stage under the WA DAMA pathway. 

## Concessions Available Under WA DAMA 

The WA DAMA offers several concessions that applicants may not get under standard visa programs. 

### Occupation Concessions 
Occupation concessions mainly vary based on location. 

#### Metropolitan Perth 
* Focuses mainly on Skill Levels 1–3 occupations 
* Includes some Skill Level 4 (semi-skilled) roles 
* Limited non-ANZSCO occupations in specific cases 

#### Regional Western Australia 
* Wider range of occupations across Skill Levels 1–4 
* Includes additional roles based on regional workforce needs 
* Offers faster PR pathways and lower work experience requirements compared to Perth 

### Work Experience Concessions 
Reduced experience requirements depending on visa pathway and location: 
* 1 year relevant experience (Skills in Demand visa); or 
* 2 years of experience for SESR/ENS visas in Regional WA; or 
* 3 years of experience for SESR/ENS visas in Metropolitan Perth. 

### Age Concession 
The standard age limit (45 years) can be increased up to 55 years for eligible applicants. 

### English Language Concessions 
* **Skill Level 1–2 occupations:** Standard English requirements apply (no concessions available). 
* **Skill Level 3–4 (Skills in Demand Pathway):** IELTS 5.0 overall or equivalent, with no minimum score required in each band. 
* **Skill Level 3–4 (ENS pathway):** IELTS 5.0 overall or equivalent, but each individual band must be at least 4.0. 

### Permanent Residency Pathway Concessions 
Eligible workers who were originally nominated under the WA DAMA may have access to a permanent residency pathway through the Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186).

## FAQs 

**1 . Is WA DAMA approval guaranteed if a business meets the criteria?**  
Not necessarily. Even if a business is eligible, approval still depends on whether the need for overseas workers is properly demonstrated and supported with evidence. 

**2 . Do WA DAMA occupation lists stay the same all the time?**  
No. The occupation list is reviewed from time to time and can change depending on labour market demand in Western Australia. 

**3 . Do I need a job offer for WA DAMA?**  
Yes. A valid job offer from an approved WA DAMA employer is required before any visa process can start. 

**4 . Can WA DAMA help me get a PR in Australia?**  
Yes. In many cases, WA DAMA can lead to permanent residency through employer-sponsored pathways like Subclass 186. 

**5 . What is the difference between DAMA and skilled migration?**  
DAMA is more flexible and region-specific, which allows access to occupations and concessions that are not available under standard skilled migration programs.
