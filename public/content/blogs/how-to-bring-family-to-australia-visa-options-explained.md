Starting a new life in Australia can be exciting, but being far from your family can make the journey difficult. After coming to Australia and settling in, the next step is bringing your loved ones closer. Whether it is about a partner reuniting with their spouse or partner, a parent wanting their children to join them, or children wishing to be closer to their parents, Australia offers different family visa pathways.

Each pathway comes with its own eligibility criteria, sponsorship obligations and application requirements. It is important that you understand which pathway fits your circumstances before starting the application process.

In this guide, we explain the key Australian visa options that can help you bring your family.

## What Are the Main Ways to Bring Family to Australia?

The Australian visa system provides several pathways that may allow family members to join or visit you, including:

* **Partner visas** – for spouses, de facto partners and prospective spouses.
* **Parent visas** – for eligible parents of Australian citizens, permanent residents or eligible New Zealand citizens.
* **Child visas** – for eligible dependent or adopted children.
* **Other Family visas** – including Carer, Remaining Relative, Aged Dependent Relative and Orphan Relative pathways.
* **Visitor visas** – for family members who want to visit Australia temporarily rather than migrate permanently.
* **Family-sponsored skilled migration** – where an eligible relative can sponsor a skilled applicant to live and work in regional Australia.
* **Subsequent entrant or dependent pathways** – where a temporary visa holder may be able to bring eligible members of their family unit.

The important point is that your family relationship alone does not automatically create a visa pathway. Each visa has its own legal criteria.

## 1. Partner Visa

The Partner visa pathway allows eligible spouses and de facto partners of Australian citizens, Australian permanent residents or eligible New Zealand citizens to live in Australia. The pathway includes both temporary and permanent visa stages, depending on where the applicant applies from.

There are four main Partner visa subclasses:

### If applying from outside Australia (Offshore Partner Visa)

* **Subclass 309 – Partner (Provisional) Visa**
* **Subclass 100 – Partner (Migrant) Visa**

### If applying from inside Australia (Onshore Partner Visa)

* **Subclass 820 – Partner (Temporary) Visa**
* **Subclass 801 – Partner Visa (Permanent)**

## 2. Prospective Marriage Visa Subclass 300

What if you are engaged but are not yet married?

At this time, the **Prospective Marriage visa (subclass 300)** may become a relevant option. This visa allows a prospective spouse to come to Australia for 9 to 15 months to marry their intended partner and then apply for a Partner visa.

The applicant must be outside Australia when applying for the Subclass 300.

## 3. Parent Visas

If you are an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen and want to bring your parent to Australia, there are several Parent visa pathways, including both non-contributory and contributory options.

Some of the main subclasses include:

### Non-contributory Pathways

* **Subclass 103 – Parent visa**
* **Subclass 804 – Aged Parent visa**

### Contributory Pathways

* **Subclass 143 – Contributory Parent visa**
* **Subclass 173 – Contributory Parent (Temporary) visa**
* **Subclass 864 – Contributory Aged Parent visa**
* **Subclass 884 – Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) visa**

### Temporary Parent Visa Pathway

* **Subclass 870 – Sponsored Parent Visa (Temporary):** Allows eligible parents to stay in Australia temporarily for an extended period, generally for up to 3 or 5 years.

## 4. Child Visas

If you are an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen and want to bring your child to Australia, different Child visa pathways may be available depending on the child’s location and circumstances.

Some of the main subclasses include:

### Child Visa Pathways

* **Subclass 101 – Child visa:** For eligible children who are outside Australia.
* **Subclass 802 – Child visa:** For eligible children who are already in Australia.

### Dependent Child Pathway

* **Subclass 445 – Dependent Child visa:** For certain dependent children of Partner visa applicants or holders. It allows the child to remain in Australia temporarily while the parent’s permanent Partner visa application is being processed.

## 5. Adoption Visa Subclass 102

The **Adoption visa (subclass 102)** is another Child category pathway.

It allows an eligible child adopted outside Australia to live in Australia with their adoptive parent. The child must be living outside Australia and must already be adopted or in the process of being adopted.

## 6. Other Family Visas

Not every family situation fits into a Partner, Parent or Child visa. So, Australia also has Other Family visa pathways for certain relatives, including:

### Aged Dependent Relatives

The **Subclass 114** (apply from outside Australia) and **Subclass 838** (apply from inside Australia) pathways are designed for certain single older relatives who rely on an eligible relative in Australia for financial support.

### Carer Visas

The Carer visa can apply where a person needs to move to Australia to provide substantial and continuing care to an Australian relative with a long-term medical condition.

Relevant pathways include:

* **Subclass 116 – Carer visa**
* **Subclass 836 – Carer visa**

A critical requirement is that the relative in Australia must have a long-term medical condition and there must be no reasonable access to adequate care options in Australia.

### Remaining Relatives

The **Remaining Relative visa** may be relevant in very specific family circumstances.

The main pathways include:

* **Subclass 115**
* **Subclass 835**

These visas are for people whose only close family members are in Australia and who meet the specific legislative requirements.

### Orphan Relatives

The **Orphan Relative visa** is designed for certain children whose parents are deceased, cannot care for them or cannot be found.

There are two relevant pathways:

* **Subclass 117 – Orphan Relative visa**
* **Subclass 837 – Orphan Relative visa**

## 7. Visitor Visas

If your family members want to visit you in Australia temporarily, there are also different Visitor visa options available depending on their passport, purpose of travel and circumstances.

Some of the main Visitor visa options include:

### Subclass 600 – Visitor Visa

This visa allows eligible applicants to visit Australia for tourism or to visit family and friends. Different streams are available depending on the applicant’s circumstances.

### Subclass 601 – Electronic Travel Authority (ETA)

This visa is available to eligible passport holders for short visits to Australia, including tourism and visiting family or friends.

It generally allows multiple visits within a 12-month period, with stays of up to 3 months per visit.

### Subclass 651 – eVisitor

This visa is available to eligible European passport holders travelling to Australia for tourism or to visit family and friends.

It generally allows multiple visits within a 12-month period, with stays of up to 3 months per visit.

## 8. Other Visa Options

Eligible family members may also be able to join applicants or visa holders under certain skilled and employer-sponsored visa pathways.

### Dependent Family Members

Eligible partners and dependent children may be included in certain skilled or employer-sponsored visa applications.

### Subsequent Entrant

Some temporary visa holders may be able to bring eligible family members to Australia after their visa is granted.

The requirements depend on the main visa subclass and the family member’s circumstances.
