Obtaining an Australian visa is often the result of months of planning, documentation, and preparation. However, many visa holders are unaware that a granted visa can still be cancelled in certain circumstances.

Australian visa cancellation can affect your ability to stay in Australia, apply for future visas, or even your return to Australia in the future. Here, understanding why a visa may be cancelled and what options are available if cancellation occurs is important for every visa holder.

So, in this guide, we will understand the common reasons for Australian visa cancellation and the review pathways that may be available.

## **What Is an Australian Visa Cancellation?**

Under Australian migration law, only the Minister for Immigration or a delegate of the Minister can grant, refuse, or cancel a visa.

**A visa cancellation may occur:**

* At the discretion of the Minister or a delegate
* As a mandatory cancellation under certain provisions
* By operation of law in specific situations

In some circumstances, visa holders may also request the voluntary cancellation of their own visa. But you should also be aware that if your visa is cancelled, visas held by family members linked to your visa may be cancelled as well.

## **Why Can an Australian Visa Be Cancelled?**

The Migration Act 1958 provides various grounds under which a visa may be cancelled.

**Some common reasons include:**

## **Failure to Comply With Visa Conditions**

Every [Australian visa](https://www.growmore.one/points-calculator/) comes with conditions that must be followed. The Department may consider cancellation if a visa holder does not comply with the conditions attached to their visa.

## **Providing Incorrect or Fraudulent Information**

**A visa may be cancelled if the Department discovers that:**

* Incorrect information was provided
* Fraudulent information was supplied
* Fake or misleading documents were submitted
* Circumstances have changed since the visa was granted

Providing accurate information throughout the visa process is essential.

## **Character Concerns**

**Australia has character requirements that visa holders must meet. A visa may be refused or cancelled if a person:**

* Does not satisfy character requirements
* Has been charged with an offence
* Has been convicted of an offence
* Is considered a risk to the Australian community

## **Non-Compliance With Work-Related Visa Conditions**

Certain work visas have conditions that visa holders must follow. Where work-related visa conditions are breached, the Department may consider visa cancellation after assessing the individual circumstances of the case.

## **Student Visa Issues**

Student visa holders may face cancellation if they are no longer enrolled in a registered course or fail to comply with student visa conditions.

**The Department may also consider cancellation where:**

* A student completes their course and does not leave Australia or apply for another visa within the required timeframe
* There are issues relating to course changes
* There are extended gaps between packaged courses

## **Biosecurity and Border Compliance Issues**

Australia has strict biosecurity laws designed to protect its agriculture, environment, and industries. Certain visitor, student, and work visas may be cancelled at immigration clearance if a traveller:

* Fails to answer questions about goods appropriately
* Does not follow directions from a biosecurity officer
* Provides false or misleading information

## **Will the Department Give You an Opportunity to Respond?**

**In many situations, yes.**

If you are in Australia or going through immigration clearance, the Department will usually notify you if it is considering cancelling your visa. You will generally be given an opportunity to explain your circumstances and provide reasons why your visa should not be cancelled before a final decision is made.

Responding within the specified timeframe is extremely important.

## **Can You Appeal a Visa Cancellation Decision?**

Depending on the circumstances, you may have review rights.

## **Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)**

The Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) can review certain visa cancellation decisions made by the Department.

If review rights are available, the Department will generally explain them in the decision notification.

## **Judicial Review**

If you believe a legal error occurred in the decision-making process, you may be able to seek judicial review through the Australian court system.

## **Ministerial Decisions**

Where a visa cancellation decision was made personally by the Minister, merits review is generally not available. In these cases, judicial review may be the only option.

## **FAQs**

### **1\. Who can cancel an Australian visa?**

Only the Minister for Immigration or an authorised delegate can grant, refuse, or cancel an Australian visa.

### **2\. Why can an Australian visa be cancelled?**

A visa may be cancelled if you breach visa conditions, provide incorrect information, fail character requirements, or do not follow visa rules.

### **3\. Will I get a chance to respond before my visa is cancelled?**

In most cases, yes. The Department usually informs you and gives you a chance to explain your situation before making a final decision.

### **4\. Can I appeal a visa cancellation decision?**

Yes, in many cases you may have review rights through the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) or judicial review, depending on your situation.

### **5\. Can visa cancellation affect my family members?**

Yes. If your visa is cancelled, visas linked to your family members may also be affected in some situations.
