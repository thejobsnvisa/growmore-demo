 If you are above age of 45 years and exploring options to get Australian visa, then you have definitely heard this sentence that your age makes you ineligible to apply Australia skilled migration. No doubt, your higher age becomes a significant barrier for most skilled visas, but it does not mean that every Australian visa option is closed to you. 

The key is understanding which visa pathways have an age limit, which may offer exemptions, and which do not use the same age criteria. Depending on your professional background, employment opportunities, family circumstances and eligibility for specific visa categories, there may still be some pathways worth exploring. 

In this guide, we will explore the Australian visa options worth considering after 45, the age exemptions that may apply, and common mistakes to avoid before applying for any of these visas.   

## Can You Get an Australian Visa After 45? 

Of course, it is still possible to receive an Australian visa after reaching the age of 45. What is crucial is the distinction between getting a temporary visa and being able to obtain Australian PR. 

Many of the points-tested skilled visas (Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189), Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190), Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 491)) require candidates to be younger than 45 at the time of invitation. However, other visa pathways have different age rules. 

Your eligibility for an Australian visa will depend on more than your age alone. Your qualifications, employment history, English proficiency, relationship status, family connections and current visa conditions may all affect which pathway is suitable.

Let’s examine these pathways in more detail.

### 1.Employer-Sponsored Visa After 45: Skills in Demand Visa (Subclass 482) 

If you are over 45 and have skills Australian employers need, the Skills in Demand (SID) visa (subclass 482) may be an option. There is no upper age limit for this visa, but you must find an eligible Australian employer who is willing to sponsor you for a suitable skilled position. 

You must also meet the relevant occupation, work experience, English language and other visa requirements.  

However, if your goal is to get Australian PR, then you need to plan carefully because the Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186), to which most applicants apply, requires applicants to be under 45. If you are over 45, you need to meet applicable age exemption criteria. Without an exemption, transitioning from a subclass 482 visa to subclass 186 PR is generally not possible. 

### 2.DAMA Options After 45 

For those individuals over 45 years of age, the Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) may be a helpful solution when it comes to receiving Permanent Residency in Australia. Currently, Australia has a total of 13 DAMAs, which enable companies in designated regions to hire overseas employees based on the arrangements made. 

Depending on the type of agreement, individuals who meet the eligibility criteria may get sponsorship via the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482), Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 494) or, if possible, the Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186). 

Some DAMAs offer age concessions for eligible applicants seeking permanent residency. However, occupation and DAMA area both matter because they can define whether the age exemption or exception is possible or not. You must also find an eligible employer willing to sponsor you. 

### 3.Australian Parent Visa Options for Applicants Over 45 

If you have a child who is an Australian citizen, Australian permanent resident or citizen of New Zealand, a parent visa may be another option to consider. 

However, being over 45 does not automatically make you eligible for a parent visa. The requirements depend on the particular subclass, your child’s status, your family circumstances and whether you are applying onshore or offshore. 

## Which Australian parent visas can you consider? 

**Parent visa (subclass 103)**

This is a permanent visa for eligible parents of settled Australian citizens, permanent residents or eligible New Zealand citizens. It is generally subject to a substantial processing queue. 

**Contributory Parent visa (subclass 143)**

This is a permanent visa for eligible parents who meet the relevant requirements. It generally involves higher charges than the non-contributory subclass 103 visa, although processing arrangements differ. 

**Aged Parent visa (subclass 804)** 

This is an onshore permanent visa for eligible parents who have reached Australia's qualifying Age Pension age and satisfy the other requirements. 

**Contributory Aged Parent visa (subclass 864)** 

This is another onshore permanent parent visa for eligible aged parents. It generally involves higher charges than the subclass 804 visa. 

**Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 884)** 

This temporary visa can form the first stage of a pathway to the permanent subclass 864 visa for eligible applicants. 

For the onshore Aged Parent visa subclasses 804 and 864, applicants must generally have reached the qualifying Australian Age Pension age, which is currently 67. 

### 4.Partner Visa After 45 

If you are over 45 and your spouse or de facto partner is an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen, a Partner visa may be an option because there is no general upper age limit. 

You can apply through the onshore Partner visa (subclass 820/801) or offshore Partner visa (subclass 309/100), depending on where you are applying from. However, you must provide sufficient evidence that your relationship is genuine, ongoing and meets the relevant visa requirements. 

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Applying for an Australian Visa After 45 

Applicants can lose time and money by choosing a visa based on assumptions rather than their actual eligibility. So, make sure to avoid these common mistakes: 

* **Assuming all Australian visas have a 45-year age limit:** Different subclasses have different requirements. 

* **Treating a subclass 482 visa as guaranteed PR:** A temporary work visa does not automatically qualify you for permanent residency. 

* **Assuming every DAMA offers the same age concessions:** Concessions vary by agreement, occupation and visa subclass. 

* **Choosing a parent visa without checking the requirements:** Age, sponsorship, family composition and the balance-of-family test can be decisive. 

* **Assuming a relationship automatically qualifies you for a partner visa:** You must meet the legal criteria and provide appropriate evidence. 

* **Waiting until your visa is about to expire:** Delays can restrict your options and create avoidable complications. 

* **Relying on outdated immigration information:** Visa criteria, salary thresholds, concessions and processing arrangements can change.

## FAQs 

### 1. Can I apply for an Australian visa after 45? 

Yes, you may qualify for certain Australian visas after 45, including employer-sponsored, partner and parent visas, depending on your circumstances and the relevant eligibility criteria. 

### 2. Can I get Australian PR after 45? 

Yes, it may be possible through certain employer-sponsored or family visa pathways. However, you must meet the applicable requirements, including any age exemptions. 

### 3. Is there an age limit for the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482)? 

No, the subclass 482 visa has no upper age limit. However, you must meet the sponsorship and other visa requirements, and PR eligibility is assessed separately. 

## 4. Can DAMA help applicants over 45 get Australian PR? 

Some DAMA arrangements offer age concessions for eligible applicants. Availability depends on the regional agreement, occupation, employer sponsorship and applicable visa requirements. 

## 5. Can I get an Australian partner visa after 45? 

Yes, partner visas have no general upper age limit. You must demonstrate an eligible relationship with an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen and meet the other visa criteria.
