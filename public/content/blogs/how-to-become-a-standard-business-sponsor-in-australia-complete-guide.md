Hiring skilled workers from overseas can help Australian businesses address skill shortages and meet their workforce needs. However, before sponsoring overseas employees, businesses may need to obtain approval as a Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) from the Department of Home Affairs. 

To become an approved Standard Business Sponsor, employers need to meet specific eligibility requirements that show the business is operating lawfully and is suitable to sponsor overseas workers. 

This guide walks you through what SBS approval means, how your business can apply, which visa pathways require it, and how standard sponsorship differs from accredited sponsorship. 

## What Is a Standard Business Sponsor in Australia? 

A Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) is a business approved by the Department of Home Affairs to sponsor eligible overseas workers under specific employer-sponsored visa programs in Australia. 

An approved Standard Business Sponsor can sponsor skilled workers under: 

* Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) 
* Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 494) 

**Note:** The Employer Nomination Scheme visa (subclass 186) is also an employer-sponsored visa. However, it does not require the employer to hold Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) approval. 

## Eligibility Requirements for Standard Business Sponsorship 

Businesses must meet the following requirements to become a Standard Business Sponsor. 

## 1.Legally Established and Currently Operating Business 

Your business must be legally established and currently operating. It can be based in Australia or overseas. The Department requires evidence that the business is genuine and has the legal status to operate in its country of establishment. 

## 2.No Relevant Adverse Information 

The Department must not be aware of adverse information about your business or anyone associated with it that affects your suitability as a sponsor. If such information exists, the Department must consider whether it is reasonable to disregard it. 

## 3.Additional Requirements for Australian Businesses 

Businesses operating in Australia must demonstrate: 

* A strong record of, or commitment to, employing local labour. 
* A commitment not to engage in discriminatory recruitment practices. 

Employers must provide written declarations covering these requirements as part of the sponsorship application. 

## Documents Required for Standard Business Sponsorship 

To apply for Standard Business Sponsorship, your business must provide evidence that it is legally established and currently operating. 

The documents required may vary depending on the business's structure, location and operating history. Supporting documents may include: 

* Business registration: ABN, ARBN or overseas business registration evidence. 
* Company details: ASIC company extract or business name extract, where applicable. 
* Business operations: Profit and loss statements, annual reports, tax returns, BAS or bank statements, depending on the business's operating history. 
* New businesses: Business plan, service contracts and business bank statements. 
* Business structure: Relevant trust, franchise or joint venture agreements, if applicable. 

## How to Apply for Standard Business Sponsorship in Australia 

Businesses can apply for Standard Business Sponsorship online through the Department of Home Affairs' ImmiAccount. 

The application process involves: 

**1.Create or log in to ImmiAccount:** Use your business's ImmiAccount to start the application. 

**2.Complete the application:** Select the Standard Business Sponsorship application and provide the required business details. 

**3.Attach supporting documents:** Upload evidence of your business's legal establishment, operations and eligibility. 

**4.Pay the application fee:** Submit the applicable fee to lodge the application. 

**5.Keep the Transaction Reference Number (TRN):** Use it to track the application and refer to it in any communication with the Department. 

## Is Standard Business Sponsorship the Same as Accredited Sponsorship? 

No. Standard Business Sponsorship and Accredited Sponsorship are different levels of sponsorship approval. 

A business must first meet the requirements to become a Standard Business Sponsor (SBS). It can then apply for accredited status if it meets the additional criteria set by the Department of Home Affairs. 

The key differences are: 

* Standard Business Sponsor: Approval to sponsor eligible overseas workers under subclass 482 and subclass 494 visa programs. 
* Accredited Sponsor: An eligible Standard Business Sponsor that receives additional accreditation status and priority processing for relevant nomination and visa applications. 

Accredited status is not automatic. Businesses must meet at least one of the Department's specified accreditation categories and provide supporting evidence.

## FAQs 

## 1. What is a Standard Business Sponsor in Australia? 

A Standard Business Sponsor is a business approved by the Department of Home Affairs to sponsor eligible overseas workers under specific employer-sponsored visa programs. 

## 2. Which employer-sponsored visas require Standard Business Sponsorship? 

SBS approval is required for employers sponsoring workers under the Subclass 482 and Subclass 494 visa programs. Subclass 186 does not require SBS approval. 

## 3. Can a new business apply for Standard Business Sponsorship? 

Yes. A new business can apply if it is legally established and can provide sufficient evidence that it is genuinely operating. 

## 4. How can a business apply for Standard Business Sponsorship? 

Businesses can apply online through ImmiAccount by completing the application, providing supporting documents and paying the applicable fee. 

## 5. What is the difference between Standard Business Sponsorship and Accredited Sponsorship? 

Standard Business Sponsorship allows eligible businesses to sponsor overseas workers, while Accredited Sponsorship provides eligible businesses with additional benefits, including priority processing for relevant applications.
