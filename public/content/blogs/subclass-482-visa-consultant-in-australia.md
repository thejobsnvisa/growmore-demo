**1\. Subclass 482 Eligibility Requirements – Do You Qualify?** 

The Subclass 482 visa has two distinct parts: the employer’s nomination and your individual visa application. Both sides carry eligibility requirements, and both must be satisfied before a visa can be granted. 

### **What is the Subclass 482 Visa?** 

The Skills in Demand (Subclass 482) allows Australian businesses to sponsor overseas workers to fill genuine skills gaps when suitably qualified Australians are not available. Depending on the occupation, it is granted for up to four years and can, in many cases, serve as a direct pathway to permanent residency. 

### **Employer Eligibility (Sponsor Requirements)** 

Your employer must be – or must become – an approved [Standard Business Sponsor (SBS)](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/employers/standard-business-sponsorship/).

**This requires them to demonstrate:** 

*   A lawfully operating business in Australia 

*   No adverse information on record regarding immigration, employment law, or character 

*   That the nominated position is genuine and cannot be filled by a suitably qualified Australian 

*   That the salary offered meets the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT), currently $76,515+, though this is reviewed regularly 

*   Payment of the Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy 

### **Individual Applicant Eligibility** 

*   Your nominated occupation must appear on the relevant skilled occupation list for your chosen stream 

*   You must hold a relevant qualification or a minimum of 1 year of recent, full-time work experience in the nominated occupation 

*   You must meet English language requirements (IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, OET, or CAE — exemptions may apply) 

*   You must satisfy health requirements — a medical examination by a panel physician is required 

*   You must satisfy character requirements — police clearances from all relevant countries are required 

*   You must be under 45 years of age for certain streams (not required for Subclass 482 itself, but relevant for future PR pathways) 

> **🔑 Age & PR Pathway Note: While the 482 visa itself has no age cap, planning your transition to**  Permanent residency via Subclass 186 does involve age considerations. If you are approaching 45, Seek professional advice sooner rather than later — timing matters significantly.

**3\. Subclass 482 Documents Required – The Full Checklist** 

One of the most valuable things a [482 visa consultant](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/employers/regional-certifying-body-approval/) provides is a personalised document checklist — because the standard list differs based on your occupation, nationality, whether you have family members, and whether you are applying onshore or offshore. The following is a comprehensive general checklist: 

### **A. Identity Documents** 

*   Current passport — all pages, including blank pages 

*   All previous passports held in the last 10 years 

*   National identity card (if applicable) 

*   Birth certificate 

*   Marriage certificate or divorce decree (if applicable) 

*   Deed poll or official name change documents (if applicable) 

### **B. Skills, Qualifications & Experience** 

*   Degree certificates, diplomas, or trade qualifications (certified copies + translations if not in English) 

*   Academic transcripts 

*   Skills assessment letter from the relevant Australian assessing authority — see examples below: 


|Occupation / Field                  |Assessing Authority                |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
|Engineering                         |Engineers Australia                |
|Information Technology              |ACS (Australian Computer Society)  |
|Nursing & Midwifery                 |AHPRA / ANMAC                      |
|Accounting & Finance                |CPA Australia / CAANZ / IPA        |
|Trades (Electrician, Plumber, etc.) |TRA (Trades Recognition Australia) |
|Management & Other Professions      |VETASSESS                          |


*   Employment reference letters — on company letterhead, signed, with employer contact details, covering all relevant roles 

*   Payslips or employment contracts (to corroborate reference letters) 

*   Updated CV / Resume 

*   Professional registration certificates (e.g., medical registration, engineering licence) 

### **C. English Language Evidence** 

*   IELTS Academic or General: minimum 5.0 overall (no band below 5.0) 

*   PTE Academic: Listening: 33 | Reading: 36 | Writing: 29 | Speaking: 24 

*   TOEFL iBT: Listening: 8 | Reading: 8 | Writing: 9 | Speaking: 14 

*   OET: Listening: 220 | Reading: 240 | Writing: 200 | Speaking: 270 

*   Exemption evidence: passport from UK, USA, Canada, New Zealand, or the Republic of Ireland 

### **D. Health & Character** 

*   Health examination completed by an approved panel physician (Home Affairs will issue a referral via HealthLink) 

*   Australian Federal Police (AFP) check — if you have lived in Australia for 12+ months in the past 10 years 

*   Police clearances from every country where you have lived for 12+ cumulative months in the past 10 years 

### **E. Employer / Nomination Documents** 

*   SBS approval confirmation from Home Affairs 

*   Signed employment contract — must specify salary, title, duties, and location 

*   Genuine position evidence — organisational chart, position description, and LMFT (Labour Market Testing) evidence where required 

*   SAF levy payment receipt 

*   Evidence of market salary rate compliance 

### **F. Dependants (if applicable)** 

*   Passports and birth certificates for all dependent children 

*   Proof of relationship for partner (marriage certificate, joint financial statements, shared lease, photographs) 

*   School enrolment records for school-age dependants 

*   Health examinations for all accompanying family members 

> **⚠️  Do not underestimate reference letter quality. Home Affairs officers read these carefully.** Vague, generic letters without specific duties, dates, and contact information are a leading cause of RFIs and refusals. A 482 visa consultant will review every letter before lodgement.

**5\. Step-by-Step Process – From First Contact to Visa Grant** 

Here is the complete roadmap, including what your 482 visa consultant should be doing at each stage: 

**Step 1 – Free Eligibility Assessment**. Your journey starts with an honest assessment. Before any money changes hands, a credible consultant will review your occupation, qualifications, experience, English level, and employer’s situation to confirm whether the Subclass 482 pathway is viable for you. Do not pay anyone who skips this step. 

**Step 2 – Employer Obtains Standard Business Sponsorship (SBS)** If your employer is not already an approved sponsor, they must apply for SBS. Your consultant should prepare and lodge this application on their behalf, ensuring all business documentation is correctly presented. SBS approval typically takes 1–4 weeks. 

**Step 3 – Skills Assessment (if required)** For most occupations and streams, a positive skills assessment from the relevant Australian authority is mandatory. Your consultant will identify the correct assessing body, explain the documentation required, and often review your application before submission. Allow 4–20 weeks for this stage. 

**Step 4 – English Language Test** Book your test early if you have not already sat one. Scores must be valid at the time of application lodgement (results typically expire after 3 years). Your consultant will confirm the minimum score requirements for your specific occupation and stream. 

**Step 5 – Labour Market Testing (LMT) Evidence** For most Short-Term and Medium-Long Term stream nominations, the employer must demonstrate that the position was advertised and no suitably qualified Australian applicants were available. This evidence must meet strict requirements — your consultant will advise your employer on compliant advertising and record-keeping. 

**Step 6 – Nomination Application Preparation & Lodgement** Your consultant prepares the nomination application, ensuring the position description, salary, and genuine vacancy evidence are airtight. The SAF levy is paid at this stage. Nomination and visa are typically lodged together. 

**Step 7 – Visa Application Preparation** All personal documents are collated, certified, and translated as required. Your consultant will cross-check every document against the current requirements and the specific concerns relevant to your nationality and occupation before a single page is submitted. 

**Step 8 – Lodge Application via ImmiAccount** Both the nomination and visa applications are submitted through the Department of Home Affairs’ secure online portal. Your Transaction Reference Number (TRN) confirms lodgement. Keep this safe. 

**Step 9 – Health Examination** Home Affairs issues a HealthLink referral. You book with an approved panel physician in your country. Results go directly to Home Affairs — you do not submit them yourself. Complete this promptly — delays here slow the entire application. 

**Step 10 – Police Clearances:** Obtain certificates from every relevant country. Processing times vary widely — some countries take 2 weeks, others take 3–4 months. Start this in parallel with other steps. Your consultant will advise which countries and what format is accepted. 

**Step 11 – RFI Response (if applicable)** Home Affairs may issue a Request for Further Information. Your consultant should respond comprehensively and quickly — typically within 28 days. A weak or incomplete RFI response is a common cause of otherwise avoidable refusals. 

**Step 12 -Visa Grant:** You receive your visa grant letter by email. Review all conditions carefully — particularly any work restrictions, travel conditions, and the requirement to notify Home Affairs of certain changes. Your consultant will walk you through post-grant obligations.

**6\. Frequently Asked Questions** 

****Q: Do I need a migration agent for a Subclass 482 visa?****

A: You are not legally required to use a migration agent or 482 visa consultant. However, given that government fees are non-refundable, that the occupation lists and policy settings change regularly, and that a refused application can have implications for future applications, most applicants find that professional help significantly improves both their success rate and their experience of the process.

****Q: Can my Subclass 482 visa lead to permanent residency?****

A: Yes. The most common pathway is the Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) via the Temporary Residence Transition stream. After working with your sponsoring employer for at least two years in the same occupation while holding a qualifying 482 visa, you may be eligible to apply for permanent residency. Your consultant should be helping you plan this from the outset — not just getting you the 482.

****Q: What happens to my visa if I am made redundant or my employer closes?**** 

A: You must cease working for that employer. You have 60 days (or until your visa expires if that is sooner) to find a new approved sponsoring employer, apply for a different visa, or arrange to depart Australia. Do not wait — contact your migration agent immediately if this happens. 

****Q: Can I bring my family on a Subclass 482 visa**?**

A: Yes. Your spouse or de facto partner and dependent children can be included as secondary applicants. They receive the same visa and can work and study freely in Australia during the visa period. 

****Q: Is Labour Market Testing always required?**** 

A: LMT is required for most Standard Business Sponsorship nominations, but exemptions apply in certain circumstances — including some high-salary positions, nationals of certain countries under trade agreements (e.g., USA, Japan, South Korea under the ChAFTA, JAEPA, and KAFTA agreements), and some Labour Agreement positions. A consultant will confirm whether LMT applies in your situation. 

****Q: What is the TSMIT, and how does it affect my application**?**

A: The Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) is the minimum annual earnings floor for all Subclass 482 positions. As of 2024–25, it sits at AUD 76,515+. Your nominated salary must meet or exceed this amount — regardless of the Award wage for the occupation. Some occupations carry higher specific salary requirements above the TSMIT. 

****Q: Can I apply for the 482 visa if I am already in Australia on a different visa?**** 

A: Generally, yes, if you are holding a substantive visa and meet all eligibility requirements. However, if your current visa carries a ‘No Further Stay’ (Condition 8503) restriction, you cannot apply onshore. Always check your current visa conditions before lodging a new application.
