## What is an Australia Partner Visa?

The Australia Partner Visa allows the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, Australian permanent resident, or eligible New Zealand citizen to live in Australia.

Depending on where you are applying from, the Partner Visa pathway Australia includes:

| Visa Type | Suitable For |
|-----------|--------------|
| Subclass 820 and 801 | Applicants applying while in Australia |
| Subclass 309 and 100 | Applicants applying while outside Australia |
| Subclass 300 | Fiancé(e)s intending to marry their Australian partner |

Partner Visas generally involve two stages: First a temporary visa followed by Australia permanent residency. The permanent stage is typically considered two years from the first application date, unless an exemption arises.

## Who Can Apply for an Australia Partner Visa?

**You may be eligible if:**

- You are married to an Australian citizen, permanent resident, or eligible New Zealand citizen.
- You are in a genuine de facto relationship.
- Your relationship is ongoing and genuine.
- You meet Australian character and health requirements.
- Your partner is eligible to sponsor you.
- You satisfy the relevant visa requirements at the time of application.

## Step-by-Step Process for Applying for a Partner Visa Australia

### **Step 1: Choose the Right Partner Visa Pathway**

- Determine whether you should apply for Subclass 820/801 (onshore), Subclass 309/100 (offshore), or Subclass 300 (Prospective Marriage Visa).
- Choosing the appropriate visa subclass from the beginning can save valuable time during the application process.

### **Step 2: Check Your Eligibility**

**Some of the common eligibility requirements include:**

#### **For Applicants**

- Must be in a genuine and continuing relationship.
- Must satisfy health requirements.
- Must satisfy character requirements.
- Must provide supporting documents for identity and relationship evidence.

#### **For Sponsors**

**Your sponsoring partner must generally be:**

- An Australian citizen
- An Australian permanent resident
- An eligible New Zealand citizen

Sponsors may also need to satisfy sponsorship requirements, including character-related obligations in certain circumstances.

### **Step 3: Gather Relationship Evidence**

**Relationship evidence is often the most important part of a Partner Visa application. Useful documents may include:**

| Evidence Category | Examples of Documents |
|-------------------|-----------------------|
| Financial Evidence | Joint bank accounts, Shared bills, Rental agreements, Insurance policies, Financial support records |
| Household Evidence | Joint lease agreements, Utility bills, Mail addressed to both partners, Evidence of living arrangements |
| Social Evidence | Travel records, Photographs together, Invitations addressed jointly, Statements from family and friends |
| Commitment Evidence | Relationship timeline, Communication records, Future plans together, Supporting declarations from both partners |

The Department assesses your relationship based on the overall evidence you provided, not just one single document.

### **Step 4: Prepare Supporting Documents**

Apart from relationship evidence, applicants may need other supporting documents like passport copies, birth certificates, marriage certificates (if applicable), police clearance certificates, health examination reports (if requested), identity documents, and sponsor documents.

Documents not written in English may require certified translations.

### **Step 5: Lodge Your Partner Visa Application**

Now that all documents are ready, complete the online visa application and sponsorship forms by creating an Immi account. Upload supporting documents and pay the applicable visa application charges. Carefully review all information before submission to avoid unnecessary delays.

### **Step 6: Complete Your Health and Character Requirements**

Undertake health examinations and obtain police clearance certificates as required by the Department of Home Affairs. Additional assessments may be requested based on your individual circumstances.

### **Step 7: Receive Your Temporary Partner Visa**

**If your application satisfies the relevant requirements, you may be granted:**

- Subclass 820 (Onshore Partner Visa), or
- Subclass 309 (Offshore Partner Visa).

This is the temporary partner visa stage that allows you to live, work, and study in Australia while progressing towards permanent residency.

### **Step 8: Transition to the Permanent Visa Stage**

Your temporary Partner Visa is an important milestone, but it is not the final step. In most cases, approximately two years after lodging your application, the Department of Home Affairs will assess whether your relationship remains genuine and ongoing before granting permanent residency. After that, you will receive a permanent partner visa.

| Temporary Partner Visa | Permanent Partner Visa |
|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Subclass 820 | Subclass 801 |
| Subclass 309 | Subclass 100 |

## Common Reasons for Partner Visa Delays

**Some of the most common issues include:**

- Inadequate relationship evidence
- Missing documents
- Incorrect information in forms
- Inconsistencies between supporting documents
- Delays in completing health examinations
- Failure to respond to departmental requests promptly

Careful preparation before lodgement can help minimise avoidable delays.

## FAQs

### **1. Can I work while waiting for my Australia Partner Visa?**

If you apply for an onshore Partner Visa, you may become eligible for a Bridging Visa that allows you to remain in Australia while your application is being processed. Depending on your visa conditions, this may include work rights.

### **2. How long does it take to process an Australia Partner Visa?**

Processing times vary depending on your circumstances, application completeness, and current processing volumes.

### **3. How much does an Australia Partner Visa cost?**

Visa application charges may change over time. Applicants should also budget for additional costs such as health examinations, police clearances, and document translations.

### **4. Can I include my children in my Australia Partner Visa application?**

Yes, eligible dependent children may be included in certain Partner Visa applications, subject to relevant visa requirements.

### **5. Can I apply for an Australia Partner Visa if I am in a de facto relationship?**

Yes. Eligible de facto partners can apply for an Australia Partner Visa if they satisfy the relevant relationship and visa requirements.
