As Australia's states and territories begin opening their skilled migration programs for the 2026–27 financial year, it is important for skilled visa applicants to understand how invitation rounds work. Whether you are applying for a Skilled Independent (Subclass 189) visa, a Skilled Nominated (Subclass 190) visa, or a Skilled Work Regional (Subclass 491) visa, knowing how candidates are selected can help you better prepare for your visa application. 


The main process begins with submitting an Expression of Interest (EOI) through Australia's SkillSelect system. After that, based on your visa pathway, the Australian Government or a state or territory government reviews eligible EOIs and invites the highest-ranking candidates who meet the relevant criteria. 


So, let’s understand how the Australia invitation round process works. 

## **What Are Australia Invitation Rounds?** 

Australia's invitation rounds are the process used by the Australian Government and state or territory governments to select skilled migration candidates from the SkillSelect system. Instead of giving visa outcomes on a first-come, first-served basis, Australia uses a point-based ranking system to issue invitations to those who best meet Australia's current labour market needs. 

The number of invitations issued during each round depends on several factors, including: 


- Annual migration planning levels 
- Workforce shortages across different occupations 
- Demand for specific skills 
- Number of available nomination places 
- Government migration priorities 


These invitation rounds are mainly used for skilled visas like Subclass 189 Skilled Independent Visa, Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated Visa, and Subclass 491 Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa. 


## **What Happens After Submitting an EOI?** 


Once you submit your EOI through SkillSelect, you join the pool of candidates who may be eligible for Australian migration. At this moment, your EOI is not a visa application, but it works just as a statement of your interest in applying for a skilled visa in Australia. 


Your EOI contains information like your nominated occupation, skills assessment, results of your English language test, qualifications, and working experience. All of these things are used to rate your EOI compared to other candidates who have also submitted their EOIs. 


If you apply for Subclass 189 visa, your EOI is assessed by the Australian authorities during the invitation rounds. However, if your aim is to get a Subclass 190 or Subclass 491 visa, the state or territory government will first assess your EOI. 


## **How Are Candidates Selected?** 


In Australia, invitation rounds are very competitive, and just because one meets the minimum requirements does not mean they can receive an invitation. 


Overall, the applicants are chosen based on various criteria like: 


- Points score: Applicants with higher points are generally more competitive than others. 
- Occupation demand: Occupations that have skill shortages tend to get more invitations. 
- Government priorities: The Government of Australia may give priority to different fields based on labour market requirements. 
- State or territory nomination criteria: Different states and territories have different eligibility requirements and nomination priorities for visa Subclass 190 and Subclass 491. 
- Tie-break date: In the case of multiple candidates with the same points, the applicant who achieved that score first would get priority to receive an invitation. 


This is why two applicants with the same occupation may receive invitations at different times. 


## **How Ministerial Direction 119 Affects Skilled Visa Processing** 


It is also important to understand that receiving an invitation does not necessarily mean your Australia visa application will be processed in the same order as others. 


From 25 July 2026, Ministerial Direction 119 was introduced that sets new priority arrangements for processing certain skilled visa nominations and visa applications. Under the new Direction, higher priority is generally given to onshore applicants, particularly those who are already living and working in Australia. 

If we see based on occupation, then healthcare, education, and construction occupations are generally processed ahead of other skilled occupations where they align with Australia's workforce needs. As a result, applicants in priority occupations or priority categories may receive faster processing than others, even if they were invited later.

## **How Often Do Australia Invitation Rounds Happen?** 

There is no fixed schedule for Australia PR invitation round. 

The Government of Australia carries out invitation rounds during the full migration program year, which are determined based on migration planning targets, visa availability, and workforce needs. Similarly, each of the states and territories of Australia has its own nomination system and decides when to send out the invitations for Subclass 190 and Subclass 491 visas. 

While certain states may choose to send invitations every week, like Tasmania, others may select their candidates once a month or at different intervals as per the availability of nomination spaces and the demand for applications. 

As the invitation patterns may often change, candidates are advised to constantly check for updates and maintain their EOI active and updated. 

## **What Happens After Receiving an Invitation?** 

Getting an invitation is a significant milestone on the Australian skilled migration path but still is not the last step. 

Once invited, you can lodge your skilled visa application with the Department of Home Affairs within the specified timeframe mentioned in your invitation. 

During this stage, you will need to provide documents to support every claim made in your EOI, including: 


- Passport and identity documents 
- Positive skills assessment 
- English language test results 
- Educational qualifications 
- Employment references 
- Police clearance certificates 
- Health examination results 
- Any other documents requested by the Department 

## **Tips to Improve Your Chances of Receiving an Invitation** 


### **1 .Maximise Your Points Score** 

Improve your English score, gain additional skilled employment, or claim partner and credentialed community language points where eligible. 

### **2. Submit a Decision-Ready EOI** 

Make sure that the assessment of your qualifications as well as English proficiency and points earned are valid before applying to avoid delays or incorrect claims. 

### **3. Target State Nomination Strategically** 

Apply in the states where your occupation is currently in demand for a nomination based on the latest criteria rather than applying everywhere. 

### **4. Keep Your EOI Updated** 

You must update your EOI immediately if you have any changes in your points, work experience, qualifications or English results as invitations are issued based on your current profile. 

### **5. Monitor Invitation Trends** 

Review recent invitation rounds, minimum points, and occupation demand to assess whether your current profile is competitive or needs improvement.

### **FAQs**  

### 1.  **What is the minimum points required for an Australia skilled visa invitation?** 

You need at least 65 points to be eligible, but higher scores usually have a better chance of receiving an Australia skilled visa invitation. 

### 2.  **Does submitting an Expression of Interest (EOI) guarantee an Australia invitation?** 

No. An Expression of Interest (EOI) only places you in the SkillSelect pool. Invitations depend on your points, occupation, and current migration priorities. 

### 3.  **Can I update my SkillSelect EOI after submitting it?** 

Yes. You can update your SkillSelect EOI if your points, English score, work experience, or qualifications change.

### 4.  **How are candidates selected in Australia invitation rounds?** 

Candidates are selected based on their points score, occupation demand, government priorities, state nomination criteria, and the SkillSelect tie-break date. 

### 5.  **Does Ministerial Direction 119 affect Australia skilled visa invitations?** 

No. Ministerial Direction 119 affects visa processing priorities after invitation, not how Australia skilled visa invitations are issued.
