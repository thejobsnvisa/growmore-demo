For many skilled workers in Australia, the Employer Nomination Scheme visa (Subclass 186) is one of the most important pathways to permanent residency. However, many applicants become confused when choosing between the Direct Entry (DE) stream and the Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) stream.

Although both pathways lead to Australian permanent residency, the eligibility requirements, evidence expectations, and applicant profiles are very different.

Understanding these differences properly is extremely important because selecting the wrong pathway or misunderstanding the requirements can create unnecessary risks, delays, or even refusal issues.

## What is the Subclass 186 Direct Entry (DE) Pathway?

The Direct Entry pathway is generally suitable for applicants who may not have worked for their sponsoring employer in Australia for an extended period but already have strong qualifications and skilled work experience in their occupation.

**This pathway is commonly used by:**

* Skilled professionals with strong overseas experience 
* Applicants working in Australia on other visa types 
* Workers who may not have completed the required TRT employment period with an employer  

**To apply under the Direct Entry stream, applicants usually need to:**

* Have at least 3 years of relevant work experience
* Obtain a positive skills assessment (if required for the occupation)
* Meet English language requirements
* Be nominated by an eligible Australian employer
* Be under 45 years of age, unless an exemption applies

## What is the Subclass 186 Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) Pathway?

The TRT pathway is mainly designed for temporary skilled workers who are already working in Australia for their sponsoring employer, usually on a [Subclass 482 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/how-to-work-as-a-retail-manager-in-australia-via-subclass-482/).

Instead of focusing primarily on overseas qualifications and external work experience, TRT focuses more on the applicant’s employment relationship with their Australian employer.

**Under this pathway, applicants are generally expected to:**

* Hold an eligible temporary sponsored visa  
* Work for their sponsoring employer in the nominated role for the required period  
* Continue working in a genuine ongoing position  
* Meet English and age requirements  
* Be nominated by their employer for permanent residency  

TRT is often considered a transition pathway from temporary sponsorship to permanent residency in Australia.

## Direct Entry vs TRT: Quick Comparison at a Glance

| Factor | Direct Entry (DE) | Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) |
|---------|-------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Skills assessment | Usually required | Usually not required, but may be requested by a visa officer during processing |
| Suitable for offshore applicants | Yes | Usually no |
| Main focus | Qualifications and skilled experience | Employment with sponsor |
| Work experience requirement | Independently assessed employment history | Australian sponsored employment history |
| Employer relationship history | Less important | Very important |
| Commonly used by | Offshore workers or new sponsored workers | Existing 482/457 visa holders |

## Common Things to Consider at the Time of Applying for Subclass 186

* Your documents should clearly support the story of your employment history without gaps or contradictions.  

* Not every employer or position will qualify for [Subclass 186 nomination](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/dreaming-of-aged-care-jobs-in-australia-start-with-subclass-482/), even if sponsorship is available.

* A higher salary alone does not guarantee approval if the role itself is not considered genuine.  

* Work experience claimed in the application must usually be skilled and relevant to the nominated occupation.  

* Small mistakes in duties, dates, payslips, or employment evidence can lead to delays or refusal concerns.  

While both Direct Entry and TRT can lead to [Australian permanent residency](https://www.growmore.one/), the pathway that works for you will depend on factors like your current visa, work history, employer relationship, and occupation requirements.

## FAQs

### **1. Which pathway is better: Direct Entry or TRT?**

There is no single “better” option. TRT is usually smoother for existing sponsored workers in Australia, while Direct Entry is more common for offshore or newly sponsored applicants.

### **2. Can I apply for 186 Direct Entry from overseas?**

Yes. Many skilled workers apply for Direct Entry while living outside Australia if they meet the experience, occupation, and employer sponsorship requirements.

### **3. Is skills assessment required for TRT?**

Usually no. Most TRT applicants do not need a separate skills assessment, unlike Direct Entry applicants, but it may be requested by a visa officer during processing.

### **4. Can a 482 visa holder apply directly for TRT?**

Not immediately. You generally need to complete the required employment period with your sponsoring employer before becoming eligible.

### **5. What is a common reason for 186 refusal?**

Incorrect or weak employment evidence is one of the biggest issues. Even small mistakes in duties, dates, or payslips can create problems during assessment.
