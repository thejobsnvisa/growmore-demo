Does having a job vacancy automatically mean you have a genuine position for an employer-sponsored visa? 

Not Always. 

The Department of Home Affairs can look beyond the job title and employment contract to assess whether the role is genuinely needed by the business. The employer may need to show the business need behind the role and provide evidence that supports its duties, structure and ongoing requirement. 

So, what does it take to prove that a position is genuinely required? Let’s look at the key factors employers should consider when preparing an employer-sponsored visa application. 

## What Does a “Genuine Position” Actually Mean? 
A genuine position is more than matching a job title to an occupation code. The role should be a real and ongoing part of the business, with genuine duties that align with the nominated occupation. 

The employer should also be able to show why the position is needed, how it fits within the business, and why the proposed salary and employment conditions are appropriate for the role. 

In simple terms, Home Affairs may want to see that the position exists because the business genuinely needs it, not simply because it supports a visa application. 

## Which Australian Visas Require You to Prove a Genuine Position? 

The genuine position requirement can be relevant across several Australian employer sponsored visa pathways, including: 

* Subclass 482 - Skills in Demand visa 
* Subclass 186 - Employer Nomination Scheme visa 
* Subclass 494 - Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional visa 
* Labour Agreement pathways 

The exact requirements can vary depending on the visa, stream, occupation and applicable legislative instruments. 

Here is the first key step employers should consider: 

## 1.Start With the Business Need 

One of the first questions an employer should be able to answer is: Why does the business need this position? 

A strong explanation should be specific to the business rather than simply saying, “The business is growing.”  

For example, a healthcare clinic may have experienced a steady increase in patient demand and its existing staff are already at capacity. The business may therefore need a full-time Registered Nurse to support patient care, manage clinical duties and meet its ongoing operational requirements. 

## 2.Make Sure the Duties Match the Nominated Occupation 

The nominated occupation should match what the employee will actually do in the role. It should not be chosen first and then duties shaped around the position. 

The position description, employment contract chart, salary details and business records should all support the same information. Most importantly, it should align with the nominated occupation and the way the role operates within the business. 

## 3.Make Sure Your Documents Tell the Same Story 

Your supporting documents should all present a consistent picture of the position. The position description, employment contract, organisational chart, salary details and business records should support the same information. 

For example, if the position description says the employee will manage five staff, but the organisational chart shows no one reporting to them, this could raise questions about the role. 

Depending on the business and position, evidence may include financial and payroll records, BAS and tax records, client contracts, project documents, business plans, recruitment records and evidence of increased workload or expansion. 

## 4.Meet the Labour Market Testing Requirements 

For employer-sponsored visas such as the Subclass 482 Skills in Demand visa and Subclass 494 visa, Labour Market Testing (LMT) may be required before the nomination is lodged. 

Employers need to meet the applicable requirements for where and when the position is advertised, how long the advertising runs, what information it includes and how recruitment outcomes are recorded. 

It is also important to understand that LMT and the genuine position requirement are separate. 

## 5.The Salary Should Make Sense 

Employers need to consider the applicable income threshold and Annual Market Salary Rate (AMSR) requirements, where relevant. The salary should also make sense when compared with the responsibilities of the position, the employee's experience, the industry and the business's existing salary structure. 

Salary information should be consistent across the employment contract, nomination and other relevant documents. As salary thresholds and requirements can change, employers should check the current requirements before lodging the nomination. 

## Can New or Small Businesses Sponsor? 

Being a new or small business does not automatically prevent an employer from nominating a position. However, the business must be able to show it is actively operating, it has a need for the position, and it can support the proposed position. 

The focus should be on the facts of the business rather than simply its size or age. 

## Common Mistakes That Can Weaken a Genuine Position Case 

Here are some common mistakes employers can avoid when demonstrating that a position is genuine in an employer-sponsored visa application. 

**1. Poor Labour Market Testing Records**

Incomplete or poorly maintained LMT records can make it difficult to demonstrate genuine recruitment efforts. 

**2. Not Providing Enough Business Evidence**

Without supporting evidence, employers may struggle to demonstrate the business need for the position. 

**3. Providing a Weak Organisation Structure** 

An unclear organisation structure can make it difficult to establish where the position fits within the business. 

**4. Failing to Explain Why the Position Exists** 

Employers should clearly explain the business need for the position rather than only relying on a job description.

## FAQs 

**1. What is a genuine position for an employer-sponsored visa?** 

A genuine position is a real, ongoing role that the business genuinely needs, with duties that align with the nominated occupation. 

**2. How can an employer prove a position is genuine?** 

Employers can provide business evidence such as financial records, contracts, payroll records, project documents and evidence of increased workload. 

**3. Can small or new businesses sponsor workers?**

Yes. Being a small or new business does not automatically prevent sponsorship, but the business must demonstrate a genuine need for the position. 

**4. Is Labour Market Testing the same as proving a genuine position?** 

No. LMT is a separate requirement where applicable, while the genuine position assessment focuses on whether the role is genuinely needed. 

**5. Can an unclear organisation structure affect a genuine position case?** 

Yes. Inconsistencies in the organisation structure can make it harder to demonstrate where the proposed position fits within the business.
