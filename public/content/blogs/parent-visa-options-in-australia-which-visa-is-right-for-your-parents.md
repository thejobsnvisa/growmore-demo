Each of them comes with different eligibility requirements, costs, waiting times and conditions. So, understanding these differences can help you identify a pathway that suits your and your parents’ circumstances.

So, let’s understand different Parent Visa Australia options, their key requirements, and what to consider before choosing a visa for your parents in this guide.

## What Are the Parent Visa Options in Australia?

Australia offers seven main parent visa subclasses for eligible parents of Australian citizens, Australian permanent residents and eligible New Zealand citizens. These include permanent visas, temporary visas and temporary-to-permanent pathways.

The main options are:

- Parent visa (subclass 103)
- Aged Parent visa (subclass 804)
- Contributory Parent visa (subclass 143)
- Contributory Aged Parent visa (subclass 864)
- Contributory Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 173)
- Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 884)
- Sponsored Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 870)

The key difference is that some visas allow parents to settle permanently in Australia, while others allow them to stay temporarily. Apart from this, the cost and processing priority also vary considerably.

Permanent parent visas have a limited number of places each year. As many people apply for these visas, waiting times can be long, even if applicants meet all the eligibility requirements.

## Parent Visa (Subclass 103)

The Parent visa (subclass 103) is a permanent visa for eligible parents who want to live in Australia with their children. It may suit families who prioritise lower visa application charges over faster processing. However, the waiting period is a major consideration. As at 31 August 2026, the Department of Home Affairs estimated a waiting period of approximately 42 years for new Parent and Aged Parent visa applications. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed timeframe.

### Key features of Subclass 103

- **Visa type:** Permanent residency
- **Age requirement:** No minimum age requirement
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Application location:** Generally applied for offshore, subject to applicable requirements

## Aged Parent Visa (Subclass 804)

The Aged Parent visa (subclass 804) is a permanent visa for eligible parents who meet Australia's Age Pension age requirement and have a settled, eligible child in Australia. It may suit aged parents who are already in Australia and meet the relevant application requirements. They must also consider how they will maintain lawful status while waiting for a decision.

### Key features of Subclass 804

- **Visa type:** Permanent residency
- **Age requirement:** Must meet the Australian Age Pension age requirement
- **Application location:** Must meet the applicable onshore requirements
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Assurance of Support:** Generally required

## Contributory Parent Visa (Subclass 143)

The Contributory Parent visa (subclass 143) is a permanent visa for eligible parents whose families can manage the higher costs of contributory migration. It may suit families seeking permanent residency for their parents who can afford the higher financial commitment. The visa generally receives higher processing priority than non-contributory parent visas, although waiting periods can still be lengthy.

### Key features of Subclass 143

- **Visa type:** Permanent residency
- **Age requirement:** No minimum age requirement
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Assurance of Support:** Required
- **Cost:** From AUD 49,900 over 2 instalments for a single applicant, which is higher than Subclass 103

## Contributory Aged Parent Visa (Subclass 864)

The Contributory Aged Parent visa (subclass 864) is a permanent visa for eligible aged parents who meet Australia's Age Pension age requirement and the applicable onshore criteria. It may suit parents who are already in Australia and want permanent residency through a contributory pathway. Their current visa conditions and eligibility to apply onshore should be checked before proceeding.

### Key features of Subclass 864

- **Visa type:** Permanent residency
- **Age requirement:** Must meet the Australian Age Pension age requirement
- **Application location:** Must meet the applicable onshore requirements
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Assurance of Support:** Required
- **Cost:** From AUD 49,900 over 2 instalments for a single applicant, which is higher than Subclass 804

## Contributory Parent (Temporary) Visa (Subclass 173)

The Contributory Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 173) allows eligible parents to stay in Australia for up to two years before applying for permanent residency through Subclass 143. It may suit families who want a contributory parent visa pathway but prefer to spread the financial commitment across two applications. However, the permanent visa is not granted automatically, and a separate Subclass 143 application is required.

### Key features of Subclass 173

- **Visa type:** Temporary
- **Stay period:** Up to two years
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Permanent pathway:** Subclass 143
- **Cost:** Contributory visa charges apply across both stages

## Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) Visa (Subclass 884)

The Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 884) allows eligible aged parents to stay in Australia for up to two years before applying for permanent residency through Subclass 864. It may suit aged parents who meet the eligibility criteria and prefer to spread the financial commitment across a temporary and permanent visa application. A separate Subclass 864 application is required, and the relevant eligibility criteria must be met.

### Key features of Subclass 884

- **Visa type:** Temporary
- **Stay period:** Up to two years
- **Age requirement:** Must meet the Australian Age Pension age requirement
- **Application location:** Must meet the applicable onshore requirements
- **Balance of Family Test:** Required
- **Permanent pathway:** Subclass 864

## Sponsored Parent (Temporary) Visa (Subclass 870)

The Sponsored Parent (Temporary) visa (subclass 870) allows eligible parents to stay in Australia for an extended period through an approved sponsorship arrangement. It may suit families who want their parents to stay for several years without pursuing permanent residency. However, applicants must consider sponsorship requirements, visa charges, health insurance and other conditions.

### Key features of Subclass 870

- **Visa type:** Temporary
- **Stay period:** Up to three or five years per visa
- **Maximum cumulative stay:** Up to ten years, subject to applicable rules
- **Sponsorship:** Approved sponsor required
- **Balance of Family Test:** Not required
- **Permanent residency:** Does not provide a direct pathway
- **Work rights:** Visa holders cannot work in Australia

## How to Choose the Right Parent Visa for Your Family

There is no single best parent visa for every family. The right choice depends on your parents’ circumstances, your budget and whether you want a temporary visit or permanent migration. Before deciding, consider the following questions.

### 1. Do your parents want to visit or settle permanently?

For permanent migration, consider Subclasses 103, 143, 804 and 864. If they want to stay with family for longer periods without permanent residency, Subclass 870 may be an option.

### 2. Do your parents meet the age requirement?

If they have reached Australian Age Pension age, Aged Parent and Contributory Aged Parent visas may be suitable, subject to other eligibility criteria.

### 3. Can your family meet the Balance of Family Test?

Most permanent Parent visas require this test. Check how many of your parents’ children live in Australia and overseas before proceeding.

### 4. Where are your parents currently living?

Their location, current visa conditions and immigration history can affect their options, particularly for onshore aged parent applications and Subclass 870.

### 5. What is your budget?

Non-contributory visas generally cost less but involve much longer waits. Contributory visas cost more and generally receive higher processing priority.

## FAQs

### 1. What are the different parent visa options in Australia?

Australia offers seven main parent visa subclasses, including permanent and temporary options. The right visa depends on your parents’ age, location, budget and migration plans.

### 2. Which parent visa is faster for Australia?

Contributory Parent visas, such as Subclasses 143 and 864, generally receive higher processing priority than non-contributory options. However, waiting times can still be lengthy.

### 3. Can I bring my parents to Australia permanently?

Yes. Eligible parents can apply for permanent residency through Subclasses 103, 804, 143 or 864, subject to the relevant eligibility criteria.

### 4. Can my parents stay in Australia while their parent visa is being processed?

It depends on their current visa, application type and visa conditions. Some applicants may be eligible for a bridging visa, but this is not guaranteed.

### 5. What is the difference between Subclass 143 and Subclass 870?

Subclass 143 grants permanent residency if approved, while Subclass 870 allows eligible parents to stay temporarily for up to three or five years per visa, subject to the applicable rules. It does not provide a direct pathway to permanent residency.
