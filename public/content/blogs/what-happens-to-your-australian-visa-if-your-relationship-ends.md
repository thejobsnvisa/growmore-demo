For many people living in Australia on a partner or dependent visa, a relationship breakdown can bring a lot of uncertainty along with emotional stress. One of the biggest concerns that comes during this time is what will happen to their visa after separation and whether they can continue to stay in Australia.

At this stage, the outcome depends on several factors, including the type of visa you hold, the stage of your visa application, and your personal circumstances. In some situations, a separation may affect your visa pathway. However, it is important to understand that not every relationship breakdown automatically leads to a visa cancellation or refusal.

So, let’s see what can happen to your visa based on different relationships and visa situations.

## 1. If You Are a Dependent on Your Partner’s Temporary Visa

This situation applies to people who are secondary applicants on visas such as Student visa, [Subclass 482 visa](https://www.growmore.one/how-to-work-as-a-retail-manager-in-australia-via-subclass-482/), Subclass 485 visa, or others. 

If your relationship ends, you are no longer considered a dependent under your partner’s visa.

**In this case, you generally have two options:**

* Apply for another visa if eligible or  
* Make arrangements to leave Australia

## 2. If You Hold or Previously Held a Prospective Marriage Visa (Subclass 300)

If your relationship ends before you apply for the Partner visas (Subclass 820/801), your visa pathway may become complicated.

**However, immigration law still allows some people to continue toward permanent residency in Australia in special situations, including:**

* Family or domestic violence  
* Having a child with the former sponsor  
* Death of your partner

**In these situations, applicants may still be eligible to apply for:**

* Temporary Partner visa (Subclass 820)  
* Permanent Partner visa (Subclass 801)

You must notify the Department through the “Notification of Relationship Cessation” form in ImmiAccount.

## 3. If You Hold a Temporary Partner Visa (Subclass 820 or 309)

If your relationship ends, you could still qualify for a permanent Partner visa without waiting for the usual two-year assessment period.

This may apply if you have applied for or already been granted either a Temporary Partner Visa (subclass 820) or Provisional Partner Visa (subclass 309).

**Here, you still continue toward permanent residency in situations such as:**

* Family or domestic violence  
* Having a child with your former sponsor  
* Death of your partner

Depending on your circumstances, you could still be eligible for any type of Permanent Partner visa.

## 4. If You No Longer Want to Sponsor Your Former Partner or Spouse

This situation applies to sponsors who no longer wish to continue sponsoring their former partner or spouse for Partner visas such as Subclass 300, 309, 820, 100, or 801.

### **If the Permanent Partner Visa Has Been Granted (Subclass 100 or 801)**

The sponsorship cannot be withdrawn after the permanent visa is granted. The visa usually remains valid unless there is evidence of visa fraud or false information.

### **If the Permanent Partner Visa Has Not Been Granted Yet**

**If the application is still processing, the sponsor can withdraw sponsorship by:**

* Submitting the Partner Processing Enquiry Form  
* Providing a signed written withdrawal statement

## 5. If You Are a Secondary Applicant on a Permanent Visa Application

If you are a secondary applicant on a permanent visa application and experience domestic or family violence, you may still be eligible for a permanent visa depending on the visa subclass. A perpetrator of domestic or family violence cannot cancel your visa.

However, if family violence provisions do not apply to your situation, you may need to apply for another visa subclass.

## Important Things You Should Do After a Relationship Breakdown

If your visa is tied to your partner or spouse, taking the necessary steps early is crucial. Many people make mistakes after separation simply because they panic, delay action, or rely on incorrect advice online.

**After a relationship breakdown, you should:**

* Understand your current visa conditions  
* Check whether you must notify the Department  
* Keep records of important documents and communication  
* Explore alternative Australia visa pathways if needed

So, if you are unsure how your relationship breakdown may affect your [Australian visa](https://www.growmore.one/), getting proper guidance early can help you avoid serious immigration complications later. Speak with a registered migration agent in Australia (MARA No. 2217941) to understand your available visa options and next steps.

## FAQs

### **1. Will my visa be cancelled if my relationship ends?**

Not always. Your visa outcome depends on your visa type and personal situation. Some people may still qualify for another visa or even permanent residency.

### **2. What happens if I separate while on my partner’s temporary visa?**

If you are a dependent on visas like Student, Subclass 482, or 485, you may need to apply for a new visa or prepare to leave Australia.

### **3. Can I still get PR after a breakup on a Partner visa?**

Yes, in some cases. You may still qualify for permanent residency if there was family violence, you have a child together, or your partner passed away.

### **4. Can a sponsor withdraw sponsorship after separation?**

Yes, but only if the permanent Partner visa has not been granted yet. Once PR is approved, sponsorship usually cannot be withdrawn.

### **5. Should I inform the Department after my relationship ends?**

Yes. You should update your relationship status through ImmiAccount as soon as possible to avoid future visa issues.
