## What Is the Australia Job Sponsorship Visa?

The Australia Job Sponsorship Visa allows approved Australian employers to sponsor skilled overseas workers when they cannot find suitable local talent. One of the most common options is the Skills in Demand Visa (Subclass 482), which enables businesses to fill critical skill gaps while allowing workers to live and work in Australia for an approved role.

There are also employer-sponsored pathways that can lead to permanent residency, such as the Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186) and the [Skilled Employer Sponsored Visa](https://www.growmore.one/top-employer-sponsored-visas-everything-you-need-to-know/) (Subclass 494).

## Who Is Eligible for a 482 Australia Job Sponsorship Visa?

To be eligible for the SID Visa (Subclass 482), you must meet specific requirements across three areas: the employer, your occupation, and you personally.

### Employer Requirements

*   Must be an Australian business actively operating in Australia
*   Must be an approved sponsor (Standard Business Sponsor) or apply to become one
*   Must demonstrate a genuine need to fill the position
*   Must pay the market salary rate (equivalent to what an Australian would earn)
*   Must contribute to the Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy

### Occupation Requirements

*   The nominated position must be on the Short-term Skilled Occupation List (STSOL) or Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL)
*   The occupation must match your qualifications and experience
*   Some occupations have additional licensing or registration requirements in Australia

### Applicant Requirements

*   At least 1 years of relevant full-time work experience in the nominated occupation
*   Skills assessment from the relevant assessing authority (for MLTSSL occupations and most others)
*   Sufficient English proficiency: IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, or equivalent
*   Meet health and character requirements (medical exam + police clearance)
*   Be under 45 years of age (for permanent visa pathways; no age limit for 482)

## Step-by-Step Process: How to Get an Australia Job Sponsorship Visa

The sponsorship visa process involves three parallel tracks. The employer must sponsor, then nominate the position, and you apply as a visa holder. Here is the complete roadmap.

**PRO TIP** – Steps 1–3 are often handled by the employer or their migration agent. As the applicant, your primary focus is on Steps 4–7.

### Step 1: Employer Becomes an Approved Sponsor

Your employer must first apply to become a Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) through the Department of Home Affairs. This is a separate application lodged online. Approval is typically valid for 5 years.

*   Lodge via ImmiAccount (employer’s account)
*   Provide ABN, business financials, and details of business operations
*   Pay the SBS application fee (no charge for most businesses, but labour agreements may differ)
*   Timeline: 1–4 weeks for most straightforward cases

### Step 2: Employer Lodges a Nomination Application

Once approved as a sponsor, the employer nominates the specific position they want you to fill. This includes proving the role is genuine, that no suitable Australian candidate was available (Labour Market Testing), and that the salary is appropriate.

*   Labour Market Testing (LMT) must be conducted within 4 months before nomination
*   The role must appear on STSOL or MLTSSL
*   Nomination fee: AUD $330 per nomination
*   Timeline: 4–8 weeks (can vary significantly)

### Step 3: Skills Assessment (If Required)

For many occupations on the MLTSSL and some STSOL roles, you must have your qualifications and experience assessed by the relevant assessing body before or alongside the visa application.

*   Engineers: Engineers Australia
*   IT Professionals: ACS (Australian Computer Society)
*   Accountants: CPA Australia / CAANZ / IPA
*   Healthcare: AHPRA, AMC, ANMAC (depending on profession)
*   Tradespeople: TRA (Trades Recognition Australia)
*   Assessment fees range from AUD $300 to $1,000+, depending on body

### Step 4: Gather Your Documents

This is the most preparation-intensive phase. Compile all required documents before lodging your application to avoid delays (see full document list in the next section).

### Step 5: Lodge Your Visa Application via ImmiAccount

Create or log in to your ImmiAccount on the Department of Home Affairs website and submit your Subclass 482 visa application online. All supporting documents must be uploaded in PDF or JPEG format.

*   Pay the visa application charge at the time of lodgement
*   Provide your passport, English test results, skills assessment, work experience documents, and health/character documents
*   Link your application to the employer’s nomination (using the nomination reference number)

### Step 6: Attend Medical Examination

The Department of Home Affairs will request a health examination from an approved panel physician. Arrange your health check promptly after lodging your application to avoid processing delays. Results are submitted directly to the Department.

### Step 7: Provide Biometrics (If Required)

Some applicants may be required to provide biometrics (fingerprints and a photograph) at a designated collection point. You will receive a request if applicable.

### Step 8: Visa Decision

Once all requirements are met, the Department of Home Affairs will make a decision. If granted, you will receive your visa grant notice electronically. Your visa conditions, work rights, and travel validity will be outlined in the grant letter.

Required Documents for Australia Job Sponsorship Visa

Preparing a complete and organised document pack significantly reduces processing delays. Use the checklist below for the Subclass 482 visa application.

### Identity & Personal Documents

*   Valid passport (all pages with visas/stamps)
*   National identity card (if applicable)
*   Birth certificate
*   Marriage/divorce certificate (if name differs from passport)
*   2 recent passport-size photographs

### English Language Evidence

*   IELTS Academic/General: Minimum score of 5.0 per band (varies by occupation)
*   PTE Academic: Minimum 36 per communicative skill
*   TOEFL iBT: Minimum 35 per section
*   Cambridge C1 Advanced / OET: Accepted for healthcare professions

**Note:** Exemptions may apply if you hold a passport from the UK, USA, Canada, NZ, or Ireland

### Work Experience & Qualifications

*   Resume/CV (detailed, listing employment history)
*   Employment reference letters (on company letterhead, signed, dated)
*   Payslips and employment contracts (past 2+ years)
*   Educational certificates and transcripts
*   Skills assessment outcome letter (from the relevant assessing authority)
*   Any Australian professional registration or license

### Health & Character

*   Police clearance certificates from all countries lived in for 12+ months in the past 10 years
*   HAP ID (provided after applying) for health examination
*   Completed health check at an approved panel physician

### Employer/Nomination Documents (Provided by Employer)

*   Copy of Standard Business Sponsor approval letter
*   Nomination approval letter / reference number
*   Signed employment contract or written offer of employment
*   Labour Market Testing evidence (job ads, shortlisting records)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

****Q: Can I find an employer to sponsor me without a job offer?****

A: You need a job offer from an approved Australian sponsor before applying for a Subclass 482 visa. However, you can proactively search for jobs on platforms like Seek, LinkedIn, and Indeed and apply to roles that offer visa sponsorship. Networking and using a registered migration agent can significantly increase your chances.

****Q: Does my employer have to be an approved sponsor before they can hire me?****

A: Yes. Your employer must either already hold Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) approval or apply for it. They can apply simultaneously with the nomination, but the nomination cannot be approved until the sponsorship is granted.

****Q: Can I change employers on a TSS visa?****

A: Yes, but you must find a new employer willing to sponsor you and a new nomination must be approved. You cannot work for a different employer on your existing visa without a new nomination.

****Q: Can my family come with me on a job sponsorship visa?****

A: Yes. Your spouse/de facto partner and dependent children can be included as secondary applicants on your Subclass 482 application. They will receive the same visa duration and work rights

****Q: What happens if I lose my job while on a TSS visa?****

A: You are generally given 60 days to find a new sponsor or make arrangements to depart Australia. It is critical to notify the Department of Home Affairs and begin seeking alternative sponsorship immediately.

****Q: What English score do I need for an Australia employer-sponsored visa?****

A: For most occupations, you need ‘competent English’ — typically IELTS 6.0 overall (no band below 5.0), PTE 50, or TOEFL iBT 64. Healthcare professionals may need higher scores. Check requirements specific to your occupation.

****Q: Can I apply for permanent residency through the employer-sponsored visa pathway?****

A: Yes. After working for your sponsor for at least 2 years on a Subclass 482 (MLTSSL occupation), you may be eligible to apply for the Subclass 186 ENS visa (Transition Stream), which grants Permanent Residency.

****Q: Do I need a registered migration agent to apply?****

A: It is not mandatory, but highly recommended — especially for complex cases. A Registered Migration Agent (RMA) can help you avoid common errors, ensure your documents meet requirements, and guide your employer through the sponsorship process.

Top Tips to Maximise Your Chances of Visa Approval

1.  Get your skills assessed early — skills assessments can take 4–8 weeks and are often on the critical path.
2.  Take your English test before applying for jobs — many employers will want to see your scores before offering sponsorship.
3.  Tailor your resume to Australian standards — results-oriented, 2-3 pages, with specific achievements.
4.  Use the ANZSCO code for your occupation when searching for jobs — employers often use this to check whether they can sponsor a particular role.
5.  Target sponsors with existing SBS approval — larger organisations in IT, mining, healthcare, and engineering are most likely to already be approved sponsors.
6.  Consider regional opportunities — some occupations qualify for the Subclass 494 regional visa which carries additional incentives and a streamlined PR pathway.
7.  Lodge a complete application the first time — incomplete applications are the single biggest cause of processing delays.
