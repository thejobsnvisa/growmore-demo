If you are an Australian permanent resident planning to travel overseas, there is one thing you should not overlook: your visa's travel facility. Before booking your flights or finalising your travel dates, make sure you know when your travel facility expires and whether it will remain valid for your planned return to Australia. 

One common misconception among permanent residents is that permanent residency means they can leave and re-enter Australia whenever they want. However, while your permanent visa allows you to stay in Australia for an indefinite period, whether you will be able to return after your trip to another country depends on the validity of your travel facility. 

This guide will help you understand how the travel facility works, what options you may have if it expires, and when a Resident Return Visa (RRV) may be required. 

## What Is a Travel Facility for Australian Permanent Residents? 

A travel facility is attached to an Australian permanent visa and allows the visa holder to travel outside Australia and return as a permanent resident during its validity period. 

When you receive your first Australian permanent visa, such as a Skilled or Family permanent visa, the travel facility is generally valid for five years from the date of the visa grant. 

During this period, you can travel in and out of Australia multiple times without applying for a separate travel visa, provided your permanent visa remains valid. 

However, once this five-year period ends, you may need to meet additional requirements to return to Australia as a permanent resident after travelling overseas. 

## What Should You Check Before Travelling Overseas? A 3-Step Guide for Australian PR Holders 

Before leaving Australia, permanent residents should complete three important checks to avoid unnecessary travel complications. 

**Step 1: Check Your Travel Facility Expiry Date**

First, confirm the expiry date of your permanent visa's travel facility.  

You can check this information through: 

**Your visa grant notification letter:** Review the details provided when your permanent visa was granted. 

**Visa Entitlement Verification Online (VEVO):** Use the Department of Home Affairs' online service to check your current visa details and travel entitlements. 

**Step 2: Explore Your Visa Possibilities Before You Depart** 

If your travel facility has already expired or is likely to expire while you are overseas, consider your options before leaving Australia. 

Depending on your situation or future plans, you have two options you can choose from: 

**You may apply for a Resident Return Visa (RRV):** In case you wish to return to Australia as a PR resident, you will need to apply for an RRV. 

**You can seek Australian citizenship:** If you wish to travel as an Australian citizen, you may consider applying for citizenship, provided you meet the relevant eligibility requirements. 

**Step 3: Be Sure About Your Passport**

Your passport must be valid, and your current passport details should be linked to your Australian visa record. If you have recently renewed your passport or received a new one, update your passport information with the Department of Home Affairs before travelling. 

If your new passport is not linked to your visa, you may experience delays or difficulties when checking in for your flight or travelling to Australia. 

## What Happens When Your Australian PR Travel Facility Expires? 

If your travel facility expires, what happens next depends on whether you are still in Australia or have already travelled overseas. 

**1.If Your Travel Facility Expires While You Are in Australia**

If your permanent visa's travel facility expires while you are in Australia, you can continue staying in Australia as a permanent resident because the expiry of your travel facility does not cancel your permanent visa or affect your right to remain in Australia indefinitely. 

However, if you leave Australia after the travel facility expires, your permanent visa will cease upon departure. You will need to apply for and obtain a Resident Return Visa (RRV) to return to Australia as a permanent resident. 

If you do not intend to travel overseas, you do not need to apply for an RRV simply because your travel facility has expired. 

**2.If Your Travel Facility Expires While You Are Outside Australia**

The situation is different if you are overseas when your travel facility expires. 

According to the Department of Home Affairs, your permanent visa will cease when its travel facility expires while you are outside Australia. In this situation, you will need to apply for and be granted an RRV to return to Australia as a permanent resident. 

This is why it is important to check your travel facility before departing Australia, particularly if you are planning an extended overseas stay. 

**Important:** If you attempt to travel to Australia without holding a valid visa, you may be denied boarding. Australian citizens are exempt from this requirement if they can provide acceptable proof of their Australian citizenship. 

## What Is a Resident Return Visa (RRV)? 

A Resident Return Visa (RRV) allows eligible current or former Australian permanent residents and certain former Australian citizens to travel to and return to Australia as permanent residents. 

There are two RRV subclasses: 

**Subclass 155: Resident Return Visa** 

Available to eligible permanent residents, former permanent residents and certain former Australian citizens. Depending on your eligibility, it can provide a travel facility of up to five years. 

**Subclass 157: Resident Return Visa** 

For eligible applicants who do not meet the requirements for a five-year RRV but qualify for a shorter travel facility. It generally provides a three-month travel facility. 

## Can Australian Permanent Residents Return on a Visitor Visa or ETA? 

This is one of the most important warnings for Australian permanent residents planning overseas travel. 

If your permanent visa's travel facility has expired, you should not assume that applying for an Electronic Travel Authority (ETA), eVisitor visa or Visitor visa is a suitable alternative for returning to Australia. 

The Department of Home Affairs warns that permanent residents who return to Australia on a temporary visa may face serious immediate and long-term consequences. 

**1.Impact on Permanent Residency and Citizenship** 

Your permanent residency status may be affected if you return to Australia on a temporary visa instead of the appropriate permanent resident travel facility. This may also affect your ability to meet the residence requirements for Australian citizenship. 

**2.Impact on Future RRV Eligibility** 

Your permanent residency status and immigration history may affect your ability to meet the eligibility requirements for a future Resident Return Visa (RRV). 

**3.Impact on Medicare, Work Rights and Government Entitlements** 

A change in your immigration status may affect your Medicare eligibility, unrestricted work rights and access to certain government benefits and entitlements. 

## Does Home Affairs Send Travel Facility Expiry Reminders? 

Yes. The Department of Home Affairs operates a Travel Facility Expiry Reminder (TFER) service to help eligible Australian permanent residents keep track of their travel facility expiry dates. 

Under this service, eligible permanent residents receive an email reminder approximately 60 days before their travel facility expires. 

However, these reminders are only sent when: 

* The permanent resident does not have a pending Resident Return Visa application. 
* Their personal email address is recorded in the Department of Home Affairs' system.

## FAQs  

**1. Can Australian permanent residents travel overseas without an RRV?** 

Yes. Australian permanent residents can travel overseas and return without an RRV while their permanent visa's travel facility remains valid. 

**2. What happens if my Australian PR travel facility expires while I am overseas?** 

Your permanent visa ceases when its travel facility expires while you are outside Australia. You will generally need to obtain a Resident Return Visa (RRV) to return as a permanent resident. 

**3. How can I check my Australian PR travel facility expiry date?** 

You can check your travel facility expiry date through your visa grant notification letter or the Department of Home Affairs' Visa Entitlement Verification Online (VEVO) service. 

**4. How long is an Australian Resident Return Visa (RRV) valid?** 

An RRV may provide a travel facility of up to five years under Subclass 155 or generally three months under Subclass 157, depending on the applicant's eligibility and circumstances. 

**5. Can I return to Australia on a Visitor Visa or ETA if my PR travel facility has expired?** 

Do not assume a Visitor Visa or ETA is a suitable alternative. Returning on a temporary visa may have serious consequences for your permanent residency, future RRV eligibility, citizenship pathway and other entitlements.
