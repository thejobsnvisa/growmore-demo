If you are a tradesperson and planning to migrate to Australia, your skills alone won’t get you there. They need to be officially assessed and verified by a specific authority. The assessment authority that handles this process is known as Trades Recognition Australia (TRA).

In migration, skills assessment is a mandatory step that confirms whether your qualifications and work experience meet Australian industry standards. Without a positive outcome from TRA, you cannot proceed with most skilled migration or employer-sponsored visa applications.

If you want to avoid rejections and delays, this guide will walk you through the TRA requirements and process in a clear, structured, and practical way based on how applications are actually assessed.

## **TRA Skills Assessment Programs**

TRA Skills Assessment Programs are designed to assess your skills based on your qualification, work experience, visa pathway, and whether you are applying from inside or outside Australia. Each program serves a specific purpose in your migration journey.

**Here’s how they differ:** 

**1. Provisional Skills Assessment (PSA):** For international graduates to verify their Australian qualification as the first step toward subclass 485 and the Job Ready Program.

**2. Job Ready Program (JRP):** For graduates who need to gain Australian work experience to complete a full skills assessment for migration.

**3. Migration Skills Assessment (MSA):** For applicants who are applying for skilled migration and must demonstrate that their trade experience and qualifications meet Australian standards.

**4. Temporary Skills Shortage (TSS) Skills Assessment:** For applicants applying under employer-sponsored visas like subclass 482 to confirm job-ready skills.

**5. Offshore Skills Assessment Program (OSAP):** For overseas applicants who need to demonstrate their skills meet Australian standards for migration.

**6. Migration Points Advice (MPA) Program:** For applicants with a positive TRA outcome who need official points advice for points-tested visa applications.

## **Step-by-Step TRA Skills Assessment Process**

### **Step 1: Check if TRA applies to your trade**
\
First, confirm that your occupation is actually assessed by Trades Recognition Australia and not another assessing body, such as Engineers Australia or VETASSESS.

You can verify that your occupation is clearly included in the trade list provided above. At this stage, you should also ensure that your nominated trade exactly matches your actual job duties and experience (not just your job title).

### **Step 2: Select the correct TRA pathway**
\
This is where many applicants make mistakes. So, make sure you select the correct route. Your pathway depends on your profile:

- Studied in Australia → PSA + JRP
- Employer sponsorship route → TSS Skills Assessment
- Applying from overseas → OSAP or MSA (based on trade and eligibility)

### **Step 3: Create your TRA account**
\
You register online and enter your details exactly as per your passport.

Even small errors such as spelling differences, incorrect date formats, or mismatched identity details can create verification issues later. Accuracy here is critical because TRA cross-checks identity and employment records strictly.

### **Step 4: Prepare and organise your documents**
\
You need to present strong, consistent, and verifiable evidence of your trade experience, including:

- Employment reference letters with detailed duties
- Payslips or salary evidence
- Bank statements (if required)
- Qualification certificates and transcripts
- Detailed resume aligned with your occupation

The key is not just “having documents” but ensuring they clearly prove your skills match Australian standards.

### **Step 5: Submit your application**
\
Once your documents are ready, you upload them through the TRA portal and submit your application.

**After submission:** 

- Your application enters assessment review
- You may be asked for additional evidence
- In some cases, clarification or verification checks are conducted

### **Step 6: Assessment review or technical evaluation**
\
**Depending on your pathway, TRA may conduct:**

- Document-based assessment only, OR
- Technical interview, OR
- Practical workplace assessment

This step is designed to confirm that your real-world skills match your nominated trade at Australian standards.

### **Step 7: Outcome decision**
\
**Finally, you receive your result:**

- Positive Skills Assessment → You can proceed with the visa application
- Negative Outcome → You may need to reapply with corrected evidence

## **FAQs**  

**1\. What is TRA Skills Assessment?**

Trades Recognition Australia Skills Assessment verifies if your trade skills and experience meet Australian standards. It is mandatory for most skilled and employer-sponsored visas.

**2\. Who needs TRA Skills Assessment?**

Anyone applying under a trade occupation (like electrician, plumber, carpenter, chef, mechanic, etc.) for Australian migration or sponsorship must complete TRA assessment.

**3\. What are the main TRA programs?**

Key programs include PSA, JRP, MSA, TSS Skills Assessment, OSAP, and MPA, each designed for different visa pathways and applicant profiles.

**4\. What documents are required?**

You need a passport, qualification certificates, employment reference letters, payslips, and a detailed resume matching your trade experience.

**5\. What if my TRA assessment is negative?**

You cannot proceed with visa application, but you can reapply after fixing errors like missing documents, wrong occupation selection, or weak work evidence.
