## The Minister's Interview: Current and Future Migration Plans

The Australian Government has provided further insight into its migration strategy through a recent interview with Immigration Minister Tony Burke. The discussion covered migration numbers, housing pressures, family visas, student visas, working holiday makers and skilled migration.

Below are the key areas that may affect current and future visa applicants.

## 1. Migration Numbers

The Government remains committed to reducing overall migration levels from the recent peak levels experienced after the pandemic.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Migration numbers have already reduced significantly from their peak.

* The long-term objective is to continue lowering net migration.

* Future migration settings will be closely linked to housing supply and infrastructure capacity.

## 2. Skilled Migration

The Minister confirmed that [skilled migration](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/queensland-skilled-migration-program-2025-26-state-nomination-guide/) remains essential to Australia's economy.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Australia continues to rely on overseas workers in healthcare, aged care and construction.

* Doctors, nurses, tradespeople and regional workers remain critical to workforce shortages.

* Skilled migration is expected to remain a priority despite lower overall migration numbers.

### **What this means:**

Applicants with skills in priority occupations are likely to continue benefiting from migration opportunities.

## 3. Student Visas

[Student visas](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/student-visa) remain a major focus of migration reform.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* The Government believes student numbers have a direct impact on housing demand.

* Student visa settings have already become stricter.

* Further measures may be introduced to manage future student intake.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
Prospective students should expect continued scrutiny of Genuine Student requirements and enrolment pathways.

## 4. Permanent Residency Pathways

One of the most significant policy directions discussed was the increasing focus on onshore applicants.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* A larger proportion of permanent visas will be allocated to people already living in Australia.

* The Government is encouraging temporary visa holders to transition to permanent residency.

* Offshore applicants may face greater competition for available places.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
[Existing temporary visa holders](https://www.growmore.one/news/australia-proposes-temporary-visa-restrictions/) may benefit from stronger permanent residency pathways.

## 5. Working Holiday Visas

The Government is considering further reforms to the [Working Holiday](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/working-holiday-work-holiday-visa/) Maker program.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Ballot systems may be introduced for certain visa streams.

* Greater controls may apply to second and third-year working holiday visas.

* The objective is to better manage visa numbers.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
Competition for some Working Holiday visas may increase in the future.

## 6. Partner Visas

The Minister acknowledged increasing processing times for partner visas.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Genuine partner visa applications are now taking approximately two years in many cases.

* Processing times have been gradually increasing.

* Family migration is currently not receiving the same level of priority as critical skilled migration programs.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
Partner visa applicants should prepare for longer waiting periods and ensure applications are decision-ready when lodged.

## 7. Parent Visas

The Minister also discussed [parent visa processing](https://www.growmore.one/news/australia-announces-parent-family-visa-grant-caps-for-2025-2026/).

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Parent visa waiting periods remain extremely lengthy.

* Demand continues to significantly exceed available places.

* Some visa categories have waiting periods extending for decades.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
Families considering parent migration should carefully assess available visa options and expected processing times.

## 8. Bridging Visas and Visa Integrity

The Government continues to focus on visa compliance and system integrity.

### **Key points:**
&nbsp;
* Genuine applicants awaiting decisions will continue to access Bridging Visas where appropriate.

* The Government is attempting to reduce misuse of the system by applicants repeatedly lodging weak applications.

* Administrative changes have been introduced to speed up certain decision-making processes.

### **What this means:**
&nbsp;
Greater attention is being placed on compliance, genuine applications and efficient processing.

## 9. Housing and Migration

Housing remains one of the strongest influences on migration policy.

### Key points:

* Migration settings are increasingly being linked to housing availability.

* The Government is attempting to balance population growth with housing supply.

* Skilled migrants remain important because many work in construction and infrastructure industries.

## Conclusion

The Minister's comments indicate that Australia is moving towards a migration system that prioritises skilled workers, manages student and working holiday numbers more closely, strengthens onshore [permanent residency pathways](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/pr-pathway-in-australia-right-now-2026/) and continues efforts to reduce overall migration levels.

While migration opportunities remain available, applicants should expect increased competition, stricter compliance measures and ongoing policy changes as the Government seeks to balance economic needs with housing and infrastructure pressures.
