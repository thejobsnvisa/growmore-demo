You have worked for many years and built your career, and now you are planning for Australian PR. But does all that experience count when meeting the requirements for a specific visa subclass?

The answer to this depends on which field or occupation you worked in, rather than simply how long you worked in it. We often see applicants come to us with 2, 5 or more than 8 years of work history, but they are not sure how much of that experience fits their nominated job or visa path.

So, what work experience actually counts towards Australian PR? Let’s break it down.

## How Is Work Experience Assessed?

There is no single work experience rule that applies to every Australian visa.

For points-tested skilled visas like subclass 189, subclass 190, and subclass 491, your experience may help you claim points, but it must meet the relevant requirements and generally needs to be in a closely related occupation.

For employer-sponsored visas, the assessment is different. Your work experience needs to support your ability to perform the nominated role and meet the requirements of the relevant visa stream.

The Training visa (Subclass 407) takes a different approach. Here, the focus is on whether your previous experience or study is relevant to the proposed training program. Generally, you need at least 12 months of relevant work experience or relevant study completed within the 24 months before applying.

Here are some important points that are considered when assessing your work experience.

## What Actually Counts as Employment?

When looking at your work history, the first thing to establish is whether the employment itself meets the requirements for your visa pathway. Having a job in the right industry is not always enough. Other factors that may be considered include:

### Was the work paid?

Generally, the employment you claim needs to be paid. Unpaid internships, volunteer work and full-time study are not normally treated as skilled employment for a points claim or skills assessment.

### How many hours did you work?

The number of hours can also affect whether your employment is recognised. For relevant skilled employment claims, the applicable requirement may include working at least 20 hours per week.

This means you should not assume that every part-time or casual role will automatically be counted in full. The actual hours worked and the requirements of the specific visa pathway need to be considered.

### Does casual or contract work count?

It can. Casual, part-time and contract employment may be recognised if the work meets the relevant requirements for hours, payment, occupation and evidence.

Self-employed experience can also be considered in some circumstances, but you need to be able to provide sufficient evidence showing the nature and duration of the work.

### Does the timing of your experience matter?

Yes. For points-tested skilled visas, the time span of your experience can affect whether you can claim it.

For example, the relevant employment may need to have been gained within the 10 years before your invitation or application, depending on the applicable requirements.

So even if someone has 8 years of work experience, they might not be able to count all 8 years. The reason for this is that some experience could be outside the required time frame.

### What about unpaid leave or career breaks?

A period shown as part of your employment history does not necessarily mean you were actively working throughout that period.

For example, periods of unpaid leave, including extended parental leave, may not be counted as qualifying employment. Gaps between jobs also cannot simply be added to your total experience.

The focus is on the period during which you actually performed the relevant work and met the applicable requirements.

## What Doesn’t Count as Work Experience for Australian PR?

Not every job you have held will automatically qualify as skilled work experience for an Australian PR pathway. Common examples include:

* **Work in an unrelated occupation:** Experience that has little or no connection to your nominated occupation may not be counted.
* **Duties that don’t match your ANZSCO occupation:** Your job title alone is not enough. The actual duties and responsibilities should align with the nominated occupation.
* **Unpaid or voluntary work:** For points-tested skilled employment, work generally needs to be performed for remuneration.
* **Insufficient hours:** Employment below the required minimum hours may not meet the relevant skilled employment criteria.
* **Experience outside the permitted period:** For points-tested visas, skilled employment generally needs to fall within the relevant 10-year period before invitation.
* **Unsupported employment claims:** If work experience cannot be clearly shown through documents like employment references, payslips, bank statements or tax records, it may not be accepted.
* **Experience that does not meet the visa-specific rules:** Employer-sponsored visa types like Subclasses 482, 186 and 494 have their own work experience requirements, so the same rules cannot be applied across every visa.

## Which Documents Are Required to Prove Your Work Experience?

Having work experience is one thing, while being able to prove it is another. Depending on your circumstances and visa pathway, useful evidence can include:

* **Employment reference letters** showing your position, employment dates, duties, hours and salary
* **Payslips** covering the relevant employment period
* **Bank statements** showing salary payments
* **Employment contracts or offer letters**
* **Tax records or income statements**
* **Superannuation records**, where applicable
* **Business registration and tax documents** for self-employed applicants
* **Other supporting documents** that can verify the nature and duration of your employment

## FAQs

### 1. How much work experience is required for Australian PR?

There is no fixed requirement. It depends on the visa subclass, nominated occupation and applicable eligibility criteria.

### 2. Does my work experience need to match my nominated occupation?

Generally, yes. Your actual duties and responsibilities should be closely related to the nominated occupation, not just your job title.

### 3. Does part-time or casual work count for Australian PR?

It can, provided it meets the relevant requirements for hours, payment, occupation and supporting evidence.

### 4. How do I prove my work experience for Australian PR?

You may need employment references, payslips, bank statements, contracts, tax records and other evidence supporting your employment history.

### 5. Does overseas work experience count for Australian PR?

Yes, overseas experience can count for eligible visa pathways if it meets the relevant occupation, employment and evidence requirements.
