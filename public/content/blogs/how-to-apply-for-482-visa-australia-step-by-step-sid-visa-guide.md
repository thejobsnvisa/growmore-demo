## 1\. What Is the Subclass 482 Visa? A Quick Orientation

Before you apply for a 482 visa in Australia, it helps to understand exactly what it is and how it works. The Subclass 482 Skills in Demand Visa allows Australian employers to sponsor overseas workers to fill positions where a suitably qualified Australian worker is not available. It replaced the former 457 visa in March 2018 and remains the government's primary [employer-sponsored temporary work visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/top-employer-sponsored-visas-everything-you-need-to-know/).

**The 482 visa has three streams, each designed for different situations:**

| **Stream** | **Occupation List** | **Visa Duration** | **PR Pathway?** |
| Short-Term | STSOL | Up to 2 years | Limited |
| Medium-Long Term | MLTSSL | Up to 4 years | Yes — via Subclass 186 |
| Labour Agreement | Agreement-specific | Up to 4 years | Case-by-case |

_Note: Visa rules and requirements are subject to change; please check official Department of Home Affairs information for accuracy._

Your occupation determines which stream you fall into, which in turn affects how long your visa lasts and whether it can transition to permanent residency. Getting this right from the start is one of the most important things a migration consultant does.

## 2\. 482 Visa Eligibility Requirements — Do You and Your Employer Qualify?

A Subclass 482 application is a joint process. Your employer must qualify as a sponsor and lodge a nomination, and you must qualify as the applicant. Both must pass. Here is what each side needs:

### **Employer Eligibility — Standard Business Sponsor (SBS) Requirements**

* The business must be lawfully operating in Australia with no adverse immigration or employment law history
* The nominated position must be genuine, and it must reflect a real, ongoing operational need
* The employer must demonstrate they have made reasonable attempts to hire Australians first (Labour Market Testing — LMT)
* The nominated salary must meet the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) — currently AUD $76,515 per annum.
* The employer must pay the Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy for the full visa period
* The employer must not engage in discriminatory recruitment practices

### **Applicant Eligibility — What You Need to Qualify**

* Your occupation must appear on the relevant skilled occupation list (STSOL or MLTSSL)
* You must hold a relevant qualification or have at least 2 years of recent, full-time paid work experience in the nominated occupation
* You must meet the English language proficiency requirements for your visa stream and occupation
* You must pass health requirements — a medical examination by a panel physician is required
* You must satisfy character requirements — police clearances from all relevant countries are required
* You must be nominated by an approved sponsor and the nomination must be approved before your visa is granted

### **Occupation List Check — This Is Critical: Your specific job title must be matched to an**

ANZSCO occupation code that appears on the relevant list. Generic job titles like 'manager' or 'consultant' are not sufficient — the duties of the role must genuinely align with the ANZSCO description. Mismatched occupation codes are a leading cause of 482 nominations being refused.

## 4\. 482 Visa Documents Required — The Complete 2025 Checklist

Document preparation is where most 482 visa applications either succeed or fail. The checklist below covers the standard requirements — your specific situation may require additional documents, which a registered migration agent will identify during your case review.

### **Identity & Civil Documents**

* Current passport — all biographical pages and visa stamps, and all pages including blank pages
* All previous passports held in the last 10 years
* Birth certificate (official copy)
* Marriage certificate or divorce decree (if applicable)
* Change of name deed / legal name change documents (if applicable)
* National identity card (where applicable)
* Two passport-sized photographs meeting Home Affairs specifications

### **Skills, Qualifications & Work Experience**

* All degree certificates and academic transcripts — certified copies; if not in English, include NAATI-accredited translations
* Skills assessment outcome letter from the relevant Australian assessing authority (see table below)
* Detailed employment reference letters for all relevant roles — must be on company letterhead, include your title, duties, dates, salary, and be signed by a supervisor with contact details
* Payslips, tax records, or contracts to corroborate reference letters
* Professional registration or licensing certificates (required for regulated occupations — AHPRA, Engineers Australia, APHRA, etc.)
* Updated CV or resume

### **English Language Evidence**

* IELTS (Academic or General): overall score of 5.0, with no individual band below 5.0
* PTE Academic: minimum score of 36 overall
* TOEFL iBT: minimum score of 35 overall
* OET (Occupational English Test): minimum Grade C in all four components
* CAE (Cambridge C1 Advanced): minimum 154 overall
* Exemption applies for passport holders of: United Kingdom, United States, Canada, New Zealand, and Republic of Ireland
* Results must be dated within 3 years of your visa application lodgement date

### **Health & Character Documents**

* Health examination completed at an approved panel physician — Home Affairs issues a HealthLink referral after application lodgement
* Australian Federal Police (AFP) National Police Check — required if you have lived in Australia for 12 or more cumulative months in the past 10 years
* Police clearance certificates from every country where you have lived for 12 or more cumulative months in the past 10 years
* Statutory declaration or explanation of any health or character concerns (if applicable)

### **Employer & Nomination Documents**

* Standard Business Sponsorship (SBS) approval confirmation from Home Affairs
* Signed and dated employment contract — must specify annual salary, job title, duties, work location, and hours
* Organisational chart showing the genuine position and reporting structure
* Labour Market Testing (LMT) evidence — advertisements, applicant records, and evidence that no suitably qualified Australians were available
* SAF levy payment receipt — must be paid in full before nomination can be approved
* Evidence that the nominated salary meets or exceeds the TSMIT (AUD 73,150 per annum as of 2024–25)

### **Documents for Dependant Family Members**

* Passports and birth certificates for all dependant children being included
* Proof of relationship for spouse or de facto partner — marriage certificate, joint bank statements, lease agreements, shared utility bills, photographs together
* Health examinations for all secondary applicants being included in the application

### **Reference Letter Quality Warning: This is the single most common reason Home Affairs**

issues a Request for Further Information (RFI). Reference letters must clearly describe your actual day-to-day duties in the nominated occupation. 

Generic statements like 'performed duties of an accountant' are not sufficient. A good migration agent willreview every reference letter before your application is lodged.

## 6\. Step-by-Step Process — How to Apply for a 482 Visa in Australia

Here is the complete, sequenced roadmap for applying for your 482 visa. Follow these steps in order, and make sure both you and your employer are moving in parallel where possible.

### **Step 01 Confirm Occupation Eligibility**

Look up your specific occupation on the STSOL (Short-Term Skilled Occupation List) or MLTSSL (Medium and Long-Term Strategic Skills List) at the Department of Home Affairs website. Your job title alone is not enough — the actual duties of your role must match the ANZSCO description for that occupation code. If you are unsure, this is the first thing a migration consultant will verify for you.

### **Step 02 Employer Applies for Standard Business Sponsorship**

If your employer is not already an approved Standard Business Sponsor, they must apply for SBS approval through ImmiAccount. This step requires business registration evidence, confirmation of lawful operation, and declarations regarding compliance. SBS approval typically takes 1–4 weeks and is valid for five years. Your migration agent should prepare and lodge this on your employer's behalf.

### **Step 03 Complete Skills Assessment (if Required)**

Most occupations require a positive skills assessment from the relevant Australian authority before the visa can be granted. This is one of the most time-consuming steps — assessments can take 4–20 weeks depending on the authority and the completeness of your documentation. Identify your assessing body early, understand their specific requirements, and lodge your application as soon as possible. Do not wait for this to complete before starting other steps.

### **Step 04 Sit Your English Language Test**

If you are not exempt by nationality, book your English language test as soon as possible. IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, OET, and CAE are all accepted. Test results are valid for three years from the test date. Confirm the minimum score for your specific occupation and stream — some occupations require higher scores than the standard minimum.

### **Step 05 Employer Conducts Labour Market Testing**

For Standard Business Sponsorship nominations (Short-Term and Medium-Long Term streams), the employer must demonstrate they advertised the position and were unable to source a suitably qualified Australian worker. LMT evidence must include proof of advertising (typically two job ads on relevant platforms), duration of advertising, and a record of Australian applicants considered. This must be done before the nomination is lodged, and within 4 months of lodgement. Your migration agent will advise on compliant advertising formats.

### **Step 06 Prepare and Lodge Nomination Application**

Your employer lodges the nomination application via ImmiAccount. This must include the position description, salary evidence, LMT documentation, and SAF levy payment. Your migration agent will prepare and review all nomination documents before lodgement. In most cases, this is lodged simultaneously with your visa application.

### **Step 07 Prepare Your Personal Visa Application Documents**

This is the most document-intensive stage for you personally. Gather all identity documents, qualifications, English evidence, employment references, and any family documents. Your migration agent will review every document against the specific requirements for your occupation, nationality, and stream — checking for gaps, validity periods, and format requirements before anything is submitted.

### **Step 08 Lodge Visa Application via ImmiAccount**

Both the nomination and visa application are submitted through the Department of Home Affairs' ImmiAccount portal. Pay the visa application charge at the time of lodgement. You will receive a Transaction Reference Number (TRN) confirming successful lodgement — keep this record safe. If your family members are being included, their details are added at this stage.

### **Step 09 Complete Medical Examination**

Home Affairs will send a HealthLink referral to the email address you provided in your application. Book your appointment with an approved panel physician as soon as you receive this referral. Medical results are submitted directly to Home Affairs — you do not need to send them yourself. Complete this promptly — delays in medical compliance slow the entire assessment.

### **Step 10 Obtain Police Clearances**

Request police clearance certificates from every country where you have lived for 12 or more cumulative months in the past 10 years. Processing times vary significantly — some countries issue clearances within two weeks, while others (including certain countries in South Asia, Africa, and the Middle East) can take 2–4 months. Begin this step in parallel with others, not after them.

### **Step 11 Respond to Any Requests for Further Information (RFI)**

Home Affairs may contact you or your employer via ImmiAccount requesting additional information or clarification. You typically have 28 days to respond. Your migration agent should draft all RFI responses, ensuring they are comprehensive, properly evidenced, and addressed to the officer's specific concerns. A weak or late RFI response is one of the most preventable causes of visa refusals.

### **Step 12 Receive Visa Grant and Review Conditions**

Once approved, you will receive your visa grant notification by email. This confirms your visa, its validity period, and all associated conditions. Read your visa conditions carefully — particularly any work restrictions (you must work in the nominated occupation for your approved sponsor), travel conditions, and obligations to notify Home Affairs of certain changes. Your migration agent will walk you through post-grant obligations and begin planning your pathway to permanent residency if applicable.

## 8\. Frequently Asked Questions — Applying for a 482 Visa in Australia

### **Q: Can I apply for a 482 visa if I am already in Australia on a student or visitor visa?**

A: Yes, in most cases. As long as your current visa is a substantive visa and does not carry a No Further Stay condition (Condition 8503), you can apply for the 482 onshore. If your visa carries Condition 8503, you must apply for a waiver before lodging, or apply offshore. Always check your current visa conditions in ImmiAccount before proceeding.

### **Q: Do I need a skills assessment to apply for a 482 visa?**

A: A skills assessment is required for most occupations and streams, but not all. Certain occupations and nationalities may be exempt — for example, some trade occupations with recognised qualifications, and certain positions under Labour Agreements. Your migration agent will confirm whether your occupation requires an assessment and, if so, which authority you must use.

### **Q: Can my family move to Australia with me on a 482 visa?**

A: Yes. Your spouse or de facto partner and dependent children can be included as secondary applicants on your 482 application. They receive the same visa, can live in Australia for the full visa period, work for any employer without restriction, and study in Australian schools and universities.

### **Q: What happens to my 482 visa if I lose my job or change employers?**

A: If your employment with your approved sponsor ends, you must either secure a new approved sponsor (who must lodge a new nomination for you), apply for a different substantive visa, or depart Australia — all within 60 days (or your visa expiry, whichever comes first). Contact your migration agent immediately if your employment situation changes.

### **Q: Can the 482 visa lead to permanent residency?**

A: Yes — particularly if you are in the Medium-Long Term stream. After working for your sponsoring employer for at least two years in the same occupation, you may be eligible to apply for the Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme visa (permanent residency) via the Temporary Residence Transition stream. Some Short-Term stream occupations have more limited PR pathways. Your consultant should be planning this with you from day one.

### **Q: What is Labour Market Testing and does my employer always need to do it?**

A: Labour Market Testing (LMT) requires your employer to demonstrate that they advertised the position and could not find a suitably qualified Australian worker. LMT is required for most Standard Business Sponsorship nominations but exemptions exist for some high-salary positions, some nationals covered by international trade agreements (e.g., USA under AUSFTA, Japan under JAEPA, South Korea under KAFTA), and certain Labour Agreement arrangements.

### **Q: How much does it cost to apply for a 482 visa in Australia?**

A: The government visa application charge for a primary applicant is AUD 3,115 (2024–25). However, the total cost including skills assessment, English testing, medical, police clearances, OVHC insurance, and SAF levy (paid by employer) can bring the combined investment to AUD 8,000–AUD 15,000 for a typical application. These are non-refundable once a decision is made.
