Securing a job in Australia under Subclass 482 or Subclass 494 is a significant achievement. It can bring new career opportunities, financial stability and the chance to build a future in Australia. But losing this job is equally stressful because your visa is directly linked to your employment.

However, losing your job does not automatically cancel your visa, and because of this, you do not need to leave Australia immediately. Eligible visa holders may have time to find another sponsoring employer, explore alternative visa pathways or make arrangements to depart Australia.

The thing is to understand how the 180-day regulation applies to your situation, what restrictions remain in place and how your visa expiry date affects your options.

So, let’s look at the steps you should take after losing your sponsored job.

## What Happens If You Lose Your Job on a 482 or 494 Visa?

Both visa subclasses are employer-sponsored pathways, which means your work arrangements are subject to specific visa conditions.

When your employment ends, you do not automatically have to leave Australia that same day. It allow eligible holders to stop working under their sponsorship conditions for a limited period while they arrange another eligible employment opportunity, consider another visa pathway or prepare to depart Australia.

The applicable visa condition depends on your subclass:

- **Subclass 482:** Condition 8607 generally requires you to work in your nominated occupation and for the permitted sponsoring employer or associated entity, subject to applicable exceptions.
- **Subclass 494:** Condition 8608 generally requires you to work in your nominated occupation for the permitted employer in regional Australia, subject to applicable exceptions.

## Understanding the 180-Day Condition for 482 and 494 Visa Holders

Under conditions 8607 and 8608 of Schedule 8 of the Migration Regulations 1994, the relevant limits are:

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| 180 consecutive days | A single period of not working in accordance with the relevant sponsorship conditions must not exceed 180 consecutive days. |
| 365 days in total | The total number of days spent outside the specified work arrangements must not exceed 365 during the visa period. |
| Visa expiry | It does not extend the expiry date of your existing visa. |
| Earlier employment gaps | Relevant periods before and after the changes commenced must be considered in accordance with the applicable transitional provisions. |

## Example: What If You Have Only 100 Days Left on Your Visa?

Suppose your subclass 482 visa expires in 100 days when your employment ends.

Although the rules allow a single qualifying period of up to 180 consecutive days, this does not extend your visa for another 80 days. You must consider your visa expiry date and arrange an appropriate immigration outcome before your current permission to remain ends.

Similarly, if you have already spent 150 relevant days outside your permitted work arrangements during your visa period, you should not assume that you have another full 180 days available. The 365-day cumulative limit and the 180-day consecutive limit; both of them must be considered.

## What Should You Do Immediately After Losing Your Job?

The decisions you make in the first few days can help you avoid unnecessary immigration complications. Instead of relying on assumptions or verbal assurances, start by checking your visa details and collecting the relevant records.

### Check Your Visa Conditions and Expiry Date

Use the Department of Home Affairs' Visa Entitlement Verification Online (VEVO) service and review your visa grant letter.

Confirm:

- Your current visa subclass and conditions.
- Your visa expiry date.
- The work restrictions that apply to your visa.
- Any regional residence and work requirements attached to your subclass 494 visa.
- Whether you have previous employment gaps that may affect your available period.

Do not rely on the 180-day rule alone. Your existing visa conditions and expiry date remain important.

### Collect Your Employment Documents

Keep copies of your termination letter, redundancy notice or resignation correspondence, your confirmed final working date, recent payslips and relevant employment records.

These documents can help establish when your employment ended and support an assessment of your available options.

If you believe your employer has not paid your wages or other entitlements correctly, you can also seek information from the Fair Work Ombudsman.

### Start Reviewing Your Options Early

Do not wait until the end of the 180-day period to explore another employer or visa pathway. A potential employer may need time to assess the role, meet sponsorship requirements and prepare the relevant nomination.

Starting early gives you more time to assess whether a proposed arrangement is suitable for both your employment needs and your immigration circumstances.

## Can You Work for Another Employer After Losing Your Job?

Potentially, yes. However, receiving a job offer does not automatically give you permission to start working for a new employer.

### Option 1: Find Another Eligible Sponsoring Employer

You may be able to move to another employer if the relevant sponsorship, nomination and visa requirements are met.

For a subclass 482 visa, the Department of Home Affairs states that a new employer must have the applicable nomination approved before you start working for them under the new arrangement.

For a subclass 494 visa, the proposed employment must also satisfy the applicable regional and nomination requirements.

Before accepting an offer, check:

- Whether the employer can sponsor the relevant visa subclass.
- Whether the proposed position and occupation are eligible.
- Whether the required nomination and visa steps have been completed.
- Whether the employment location complies with your visa conditions.
- Whether any applicable licensing, registration or other occupation-specific requirements are met.

Do not assume that a company is an eligible sponsor simply because it has offered you a job.

### Option 2: Consider a Different Visa Pathway

Depending on your circumstances, another Australian visa may be worth assessing.

Your options will depend on factors such as your qualifications, work experience, occupation, age, English proficiency, family circumstances and the requirements of the relevant visa.

For example, some people may wish to explore an eligible skilled migration pathway or another employer-sponsored option. However, eligibility for a different visa is not automatic, and meeting the requirements of one visa does not necessarily mean you qualify for another.

Before lodging an application, check the applicable criteria, application deadlines and whether you can lawfully remain in Australia while the application is being processed.

### Option 3: Make Arrangements to Leave Australia

If you cannot secure suitable employment or qualify for another visa, departing Australia before your lawful stay ends may be necessary.

Check your visa expiry date and any other conditions that affect your stay. Do not assume that the 180-day period gives you permission to remain beyond your visa's validity.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid After Losing Your Sponsored Job

A job loss can create pressure to accept the first available opportunity. However, making decisions without checking your visa conditions can create further problems.

Avoid these common mistakes:

- **Assuming you must leave immediately:** Losing your job does not automatically mean you must depart that day.
- **Treating 180 days as an automatic visa extension:** Your visa expiry date remains important.
- **Ignoring the 365-day cumulative limit:** Previous relevant employment gaps may affect your remaining allowance.
- **Starting work for a new employer too early:** A job offer alone does not satisfy the applicable nomination requirements.
- **Ignoring regional restrictions on a 494 visa:** Your new employment arrangements must meet the relevant regional requirements.
- **Waiting until the deadline approaches:** Sponsorship and visa applications can require preparation and supporting evidence.
- **Assuming permanent residency is guaranteed:** A job change or a period of sponsored employment does not, by itself, establish eligibility for permanent residency.
