Australia's community services sector includes a wide range of occupations, including community work, youth support, family services, disability services and welfare. These occupations open the door for skilled migrants who hold relevant skills and work experience to migrate to Australia.

However, before you start your migration plans, there is one key thing you need to know: a **positive skills assessment**. This is an important step in which the organisation called **Community Work Australia** checks your qualifications and work history to see if they match the job requirements.

In this guide, we explain the eligibility criteria, required documents, assessment fees and application process to help you understand your next steps towards Australia PR.

## What Is a Community Worker Skills Assessment?

A Community Worker skills assessment is an evaluation of your qualifications, English proficiency and relevant employment evidence against the requirements for your nominated occupation.

For eligible community work occupations, the assessing authority is Community Work Australia. It assesses applications against occupation-specific criteria, and if you get a positive skills assessment, the result supports your Australian skilled migration plans.

## Which Occupations Can Community Work Australia Assess?

Community Work Australia assesses a defined list of occupations for migration purposes. The list provided for this guide includes the following:

| Occupation | Available assessment pathway |

| Welfare Worker | General skilled migration and applicable DAMA pathways |

**Important distinction:** Social Worker (ANZSCO 272511) is a separate occupation with a different assessing authority. If your qualification or job title relates to social work, verify the correct assessing body at the time of the skills assessment.

## Community Worker Skills Assessment: Eligibility Requirements

The eligibility requirements for a Community Worker skills assessment depend on the pathway you intend to pursue. The general skilled migration pathway and the Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) pathway have different employment and currency requirements.

### Pathway 1: General Skilled Migration Pathway

| Eligibility criterion | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Relevant qualification | A Community Work Australia-accredited diploma or higher qualification; a recognised relevant qualification equivalent to an accredited AQF Level 7 degree or higher; or a relevant diploma or higher qualification related to community services. |
| English language proficiency | Meet the English language requirements through an accepted English test, eligible English-medium study or a qualifying previous skills assessment outcome, subject to the applicable criteria. |
| Employment | Applicants with an accredited or recognised relevant qualification are deemed to have sufficient industry experience through the qualification's field placement requirements. Applicants with a relevant diploma or higher qualification must generally have at least one year of full-time relevant employment, or part-time equivalent, within the previous four years. |
| Industry currency | Meet one of the following: hold a relevant qualification completed less than four years ago; have at least three months of full-time relevant employment, or part-time equivalent, in a current role at the required skill level; or have at least six months of relevant employment at the required skill level within the previous 18 months. |

### Pathway 2: Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA)

The following requirements relate specifically to the **Community Worker occupation under the Western Australia DAMA criteria** provided.

| Eligibility criterion | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Relevant qualification | A Community Work Australia-accredited diploma or higher qualification; a recognised relevant qualification equivalent to an accredited AQF Level 7 degree or higher; or a relevant diploma or higher qualification related to community services. |
| Employment | At least one year of full-time relevant employment, or part-time equivalent, within the previous five years. Employment must be paid, post-qualification and at the required skill level. |
| Industry currency | Hold a relevant qualification completed within the previous four years or have at least 12 months of relevant employment within the previous five years. |
| Additional work experience assessed | **Subclass 482:** at least one year of relevant work experience. **Subclass 494 or 186 in regional Western Australia:** at least two years. **Subclass 494 or 186 in metropolitan Perth:** at least three years. The applicable minimum of 30 hours per week or part-time equivalent must be met where specified. |

## How to Apply for a Community Worker Skills Assessment

The application is submitted online through Community Work Australia's skills assessment portal. The authority outlines the process in six steps.

1. **Review the available occupations:** Look through the occupation list and find the job that fits your qualifications, skills and experience.
2. **Nominate your occupation:** Read the relevant ANZSCO occupation description and assessment criteria. Community Work Australia allows one nominated occupation per application.
3. **Prepare your documents:** Collect the identity proof, qualification certificates, English language evidence and employment records needed for your assessment stream.
4. **Create an online account:** Sign up on the portal using an active email address.
5. **Complete and submit the application:** Select the appropriate assessment pathway, complete the form accurately, upload your supporting documents and pay the applicable fee.
6. **Monitor your application:** Check the portal and email for status updates, requests for documents and the final outcome.

## Tips for Preparing a Strong Skills Assessment Application

Before submitting your application, use this checklist:

- [ ] Confirm that Community Work Australia is the correct assessing authority for your occupation.
- [ ] Read the current criteria for your chosen occupation and assessment stream.
- [ ] Check that your qualification meets the relevant requirements.
- [ ] Verify your English evidence and test validity, if applicable.
- [ ] Obtain detailed employment references and supporting documents.
- [ ] Prepare course outlines and placement evidence where required.
- [ ] Check all translations and identity documents.
- [ ] Confirm the current fee and processing guidance.
- [ ] Keep copies of your application and all documents submitted.
