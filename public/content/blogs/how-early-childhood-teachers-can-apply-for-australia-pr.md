## Early Childhood Teacher in Australia: What Does the Occupation Involve?

The official occupation is **Early Childhood (Pre-primary School) Teacher**, with the **ANZSCO code 241111**.

Early Childhood Teachers plan and deliver learning activities for young children and support their development across areas such as:

* Language and communication
* Early literacy and numeracy
* Social and emotional development
* Motor skills
* Creativity, music and art
* Confidence and cooperative skills
* Health, safety and wellbeing

The occupation also involves observing children's development, communicating with parents and participating in staff and community programs. Under ANZSCO, the occupation is generally classified at **Skill Level 1**, which means a bachelor's degree or higher qualification is generally required for this occupation.

## Is Early Childhood Teacher in Demand in Australia in 2026?

There are several indicators that Early Childhood Teachers continue to have a strong place in Australia’s skilled migration system.

The occupation **241111 Early Childhood (Pre-primary School) Teacher** is included on the **Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL)** and **Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL)**. Along with this, the occupation has also been identified in Australian workforce shortage data, indicating continued demand for qualified Early Childhood Teachers.

Another positive development came on **19 September 2026**, when **Ministerial Directions 121 and 122** commenced. Teaching is included among the sectors receiving first-priority processing for certain skilled visa applications, covering eligible applications from both onshore and offshore applicants.

This means Early Childhood Teachers may benefit from priority processing under the applicable skilled visa pathways and employer-sponsored visa pathways.

## Early Childhood Teacher Skills Assessment: When Is It Required?

This is one of the most important distinctions for Early Childhood Teachers.

**ACECQA** is the relevant migration skills assessing authority for **ANZSCO 241111 Early Childhood (Pre-primary School) Teacher**.

However, you should not assume that every Early Childhood Teacher applying for every visa needs a skills assessment.

Whether you need one depends on the visa pathway you are pursuing. A skills assessment is generally required for skilled migration visas, including **Subclass 189, 190 and 491**, and may also be required for certain employer-sponsored pathways, such as **Subclass 186 Direct Entry** and **Subclass 494 Employer Sponsored**.

## English Language Requirements for Early Childhood Teachers

The English requirement depends on whether your visa pathway requires an ACECQA migration skills assessment.

### When a Skills Assessment Is Required

Applicants can meet English requirements through either:

* **IELTS Academic:** a score of at least 7.0 in Reading and Writing and 8.0 in Speaking and Listening
* **ISLPR Teacher-focused assessment:** Level 4 in all four skills, including Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing, undertaken at an approved testing site

### When a Skills Assessment Is Not Required

You still need to meet the English language requirement applicable to your specific visa. The required level and accepted evidence can vary between visa subclasses, so the visa-specific requirements should be checked before applying.

## What Are the Australia PR Pathways for Early Childhood Teachers?

Once your occupation, qualification, experience, English and skills assessment position are established, the next question is which visa pathway may fit your circumstances.

For an Early Childhood Teacher, the main pathways to consider can include:

* **Skilled Independent visa — Subclass 189**
* **Skilled Nominated visa — Subclass 190**
* **Skilled Work Regional visa — Subclass 491**
* **Skills in Demand visa — Subclass 482**
* **Employer Nomination Scheme visa — Subclass 186**
* **Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional visa — Subclass 494**

The right pathway depends on factors such as your age, points, English, work experience, skills assessment, state or territory nomination requirements and whether you have an eligible Australian employer willing to sponsor you.

## What Documents Should Early Childhood Teachers Prepare?

Before starting the migration process, it is useful to organise your documents carefully.

Depending on the pathway, these may include:

* Passport and identity documents
* Qualification certificates
* Academic transcripts
* Employment reference letters
* Contracts and payslips
* Evidence of relevant work experience
* English test results
* ACECQA skills assessment, where required
* Registration or licensing evidence, where applicable
* Police clearance certificates
* Health examination documents, where requested
* Evidence supporting your points claims
* Documents relating to your spouse or dependent family members, if included

Employment evidence is particularly important because the way your work experience is counted can affect both your skills assessment and visa eligibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can an Early Childhood Teacher apply for Australia PR without a job offer?

Yes. Subclass 189, 190 and 491 generally do not require employer sponsorship, but eligibility depends on visa and nomination requirements.

### 2. Is ACECQA skills assessment mandatory for all Early Childhood Teachers?

No. It depends on the visa pathway. ACECQA assessment is generally relevant to skilled visas and some employer-sponsored pathways.

### 3. Can work experience as an Early Childhood Teacher help with Australia PR?

Yes. Relevant skilled employment may support eligibility or points, depending on the visa pathway and applicable requirements.

### 4. Can Early Childhood Teachers apply through state or territory nomination?

Yes. They may be eligible for Subclass 190 or 491, subject to each state or territory's nomination requirements.

### 5. Does being an Early Childhood Teacher guarantee Australia PR?

No. An eligible occupation does not guarantee an invitation, nomination or visa grant. Eligibility depends on your individual circumstances.
