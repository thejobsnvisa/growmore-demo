Every year, the Migration Program reveals a story about the country’s ongoing priorities. It reflects where the economy needs skilled workers, which industries require long-term support, and the visa pathways the Government wants to strengthen. For anyone who is planning to move to Australia through any of the pathways, it reflects more than just numbers. They give important ideas about areas in which possibilities develop and where competition may become tougher to get Australia PR.

Looking back four years, the 2022–23 Migration Program marked a significant milestone in Australia's post-pandemic recovery. In that period, 195,000 migration places were allocated to support economic growth and address workforce shortages. However, if we look at current migration planning, then the 2026–27 Migration Program has reduced the overall planning level to 185,000 places.

Here, the story is not just about fewer places, but it is about how the Government has redistributed allocations across visa categories. It has increased numbers in some visa subclasses while significantly reducing them for others, which signals a clear shift in Australia's migration priorities and long-term workforce strategy.

Therefore, if you are one of those who want to migrate to Australia, let’s compare the Migration Programs of 2022-2023 and 2026-2027 to find out more about what changes across each visa category.

## **Employer-Sponsored Visas Receive the Biggest Boost**

One of the major developments in Australia Migration Program 2026–27 is the growth in the number of employer-sponsored visas from 35,000 spots in 2022-23 to 58,040 spots in 2026-27.

This increase reflects the Australian Government's continued focus on addressing skill shortages by encouraging businesses to sponsor overseas workers in occupations where local talent is insufficient.

- **What this means for applicants:** If you are eligible for the employer-sponsored visa, this is the right time to start looking for sponsorship options, as this pathway has become one of the most prioritised under the Australian migration scheme.

## **Skilled Independent (Subclass 189) Places Have Reduced**

Also, the number of places for the Skilled Independent [(Subclass 189)](https://www.growmore.one/news/is-your-eoi-ready-subclass-189-invitation-round-confirmed-for-4-june-2026/) visa has decreased from 32,100 places to 21,090 places.

Due to no requirement of employer or state sponsorship, this visa remains one of Australia's most important permanent residency pathways in Australia. However, with fewer planning places available, competition is expected to remain strong.

- **What this means for applicants:** While the Subclass 189 visa is still accessible, relying only on this route may be more difficult as compared to it was four years ago.

## **State and Territory Nomination Continues to Grow**

As opposed to the Skilled Independent program, State & Territory Nominated visas have grown from 31,000 to 35,500 seats.

This demonstrates the growing role of Australian states and territories in selecting skilled migrants based on their local workforce requirements.
