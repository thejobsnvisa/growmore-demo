Getting a PR in Australia after completing studies is one of the most common goals for international students. However, completing studies in Australia does not automatically lead to permanent residency. 

Instead, Australia PR works through a structured system where you must meet specific eligibility requirements, such as occupation demand, skills assessment, work experience, or English language proficiency.

So, if you are currently on a student visa and confused about how to move towards PR after completing your studies, then this blog will help you understand the real pathways available after graduation and how the system actually works.

## Starting Point After Student Visa: Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

After completing studies in Australia, the first thing you can do is move to the Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485), which allows you to stay in the country and gain full-time work experience after graduation. It is commonly used as the starting point for post-study planning, but not a permanent residency pathway on its own.

During this stage, you can focus on strengthening your profile through relevant work experience, improving your English proficiency scores, and completing any required skills assessments. After this, the PR options mainly split into three common pathways depending on your circumstances.

## Option 1: Employer-Sponsored Pathway (Job-Based PR Route)

If you want to apply for permanent residency through this pathway, you should start looking for an employer while you are on the [Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)](https://www.growmore.one/news/australia-suddenly-doubles-485-temporary-graduate-visa-fee-to-aud-4600/) who is willing to sponsor you. This step is important because the entire employer-sponsored route depends on securing a genuine job offer from an approved Australian employer.

This pathway is not based on a points system. Instead, it focuses on employment demand, your occupation, and an employer’s willingness to sponsor you. Once you secure employment with a sponsoring employer, you can move towards securing permanent residency.

**These are some common options available under an employer-sponsored visa:**

### **1. Subclass 482 – Skills In Demand Visa (Temporary PR Pathway)**

The [Subclass 482 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/how-to-work-as-a-retail-manager-in-australia-via-subclass-482/) allows you to live and work in Australia under an approved employer sponsorship. It is a temporary program and is primarily used to fill skill shortages in the Australian labour market.

While working on this visa, you are expected to maintain employment with your sponsoring employer and meet all visa conditions that may support your transition to permanent residency later.

### **2. Subclass 494 – Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional Visa (Temporary PR Pathway)**

The Subclass 494 visa is a regional employer-sponsored visa designed for skilled workers willing to live and work in designated regional areas of Australia.

It is a temporary visa, but if you meet the relevant requirements, it can provide a pathway to the Subclass 191 Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional visa).

### **3. Subclass 186 – Employer Nomination Scheme (Permanent Residency)**

The [Subclass 186 visa is a permanent residency visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-186-visa-consultant-for-pr-success) under the employer-sponsored stream.

In most cases, applicants transition to this visa after working with their sponsoring employer on a temporary visa, such as the 482 or, in some cases, they may directly apply for it if they meet eligibility criteria under specific streams.

## Option 2: Skilled Migration Pathway (Points-Based PR Route)

If you want to apply under this pathway, you do not need an employer to sponsor you. Instead, your eligibility is based on a points system in which you are assessed on factors such as age, English language skills, education, work experience, and additional points, such as state nomination or studying in regional Australia.

This route is generally more competitive because invitations are based on your points score and the demand for your occupation in Australia.

Once you secure enough points and meet eligibility requirements, you can submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) and receive an invitation to apply for permanent residency. **Here are some visa options:**

### **1. Subclass 189 – Skilled Independent Visa (Permanent Residency)**

The Subclass 189 visa is a permanent residency pathway based entirely on a points-tested system and requires your occupation to be listed on Australia’s Skilled Occupation List.

It is one of the most flexible PR options that allows you to live and work anywhere in Australia without employer or state sponsorship once granted.

### **2. Subclass 190 – Skilled Nominated Visa (Permanent Residency)**

The [Subclass 190 visa is a permanent residency](https://www.growmore.one/news/tasmania-weekly-invitation-round-42-invitations-issued-in-latest-round/) option where you must be nominated by an Australian state or territory government.

To be eligible, your occupation should match the skills shortage needs of that specific state, and you must first get their nomination.

Once you are nominated, you get extra points in the PR system, which increases your chances of receiving an invitation to apply. After your visa is granted, you are generally expected to live and work in the nominating state for a certain period.

### **3. Subclass 491 – Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa (Temporary PR Pathway)**

The [Subclass 491](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/subclass-491-visa-consultant-your-complete-guide) is a temporary visa designed for skilled workers who are willing to live and work in regional Australia.

**To apply under this visa, you require either:**

**1. State/territory nomination, or**  
**2. Sponsorship from an eligible family member living in a designated regional area**  

This provisional pathway can lead to permanent residency through the Subclass 191 Visa after meeting income and residency requirements.

## Option 3: Partner Visa Pathway (Relationship-Based PR Route)

If you are in a genuine and ongoing relationship with an Australian citizen, [Australian permanent resident](https://www.growmore.one/points-calculator/), or eligible New Zealand citizen, you may be able to pursue permanent residency through the partner visa pathway as well.

Unlike employer-sponsored and skilled migration visas, this pathway does not require a skills assessment, occupation list eligibility, or points test. Instead, the focus is on proving that your relationship is genuine, committed, and ongoing.

### **1. Subclass 820/801 – Onshore Partner Visa**

This visa is for applicants who are already in Australia and in a genuine relationship with an Australian citizen, Australian permanent resident, or eligible New Zealand citizen. The [Subclass 820 visa](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/australian-visa-processing-times-update-february-2026-vs-april-2026/) is granted first as a temporary visa, followed by the Subclass 801 permanent visa once the required eligibility requirements are met.

## Ready to Plan Your PR Journey After Studying in Australia?

There is no single pathway that every international student can take after studying in Australia. But based on the course you studied, your occupation, work experience, skills assessment outcome, English language proficiency, relationship status, and long-term goals, you may be eligible for an [employer-sponsored pathway](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/employer-sponsored-visa/), a skilled migration pathway, or a partner visa pathway.

## FAQs

### **1. Does studying in Australia guarantee permanent residency?**

No. Completing a qualification in Australia does not automatically lead to PR. Your eligibility depends on factors such as occupation demand, skills assessment, work experience, and English proficiency.

### **2. What is the most common pathway to PR after studying in Australia?**

Most graduates begin with the Subclass 485 Visa and later pursue an employer-sponsored, skilled migration, or partner visa pathway based on their eligibility.

### **3. When should I start planning for Australia PR?**

Ideally, you should start planning during your studies. Early preparation can help you build the experience, skills, and profile needed for PR.

### **4. Which is better for PR: Employer Sponsorship or Skilled Migration?**

No pathway is universally better. The most suitable option depends on your occupation, work experience, points score, relationship circumstances, and long-term migration objectives.
