If you are planning to apply for Australia Permanent Residency, the Expression of Interest (EOI) process through SkillSelect is one of the important steps you will face. 

**Here, most applicants get confused by one common question: “Should I submit multiple EOIs to increase my chances of Australia PR?”**

**The short answer is:** yes, you can submit more than one EOI, but only under specific conditions. This works when each EOI is built on a clear, separate strategy, such as different occupations, visa subclasses, or state nominations. Otherwise, it adds no real advantage to your PR chances.

**So, let’s understand how it actually works.**

## What Exactly is an EOI in Australia PR?

An EOI is not a visa application. It is simply a declaration you make through SkillSelect that you want to be considered for [skilled migration](https://www.growmore.one/). 

**In this application, you mainly need to enter details such as:**

**1.** Age  
**2.** English test score  
**3.** Work experience  
**4.** Educational qualifications  
**5.** Skills assessment outcome  
**6.** Occupation (ANZSCO code)  

Based on these details, you may receive an [invitation from the Australian Government](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/working-in-australia/skillselect/invitation-rounds) or a state authority to apply for PR under skilled visa pathways.

But here, an important point is that submitting an EOI does not guarantee an invitation. It only places you in the selection pool.

## Can One Person Submit More Than One EOI?

**Yes. The system allows multiple EOIs for the same applicant. However, there is a condition most people miss:**

Each EOI must serve a different strategic purpose. If you duplicate the same profile, you don’t gain any advantage.

## When Multiple EOIs Make Sense

**Multiple EOIs are only useful in specific scenarios:**

### **1. Different Occupations (ANZSCO Codes):** 

If you are genuinely assessed for more than one occupation, you can maintain separate EOIs.For example, one person can apply for Civil Engineer (233211) as well as for Engineering Manager (133211). 

But here the catch is that both occupations must be assessed under different assessment authorities. However, if your occupation is related to IT and if it is assessed by ACS, then multiple IT occupations can be assessed by the same authority (ACS).

**Tip:** Make sure each EOI clearly demonstrates relevant work experience, skills, and duties specific to that occupation. Avoid reusing the same generic profile across different occupations, as it weakens your credibility in both.

### **2. Different Visa Pathways:**

You can also submit different EOIs for different visa subclasses. For example, one EOI is for the [Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189)](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/how-to-get-an-australian-pr-as-a-skilled-painting-trade-worker/), [Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190)](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/queensland-skilled-migration-program-2025-26-state-nomination-guide/), and [Skilled Work Regional Visa (Subclass 491)](https://www.growmore.one/blogs/australian-visa-processing-times-update-february-2026-vs-april-2026/).

Each visa pathway has its own eligibility requirements, invitation trends, and selection criteria. While the 189 visa is based purely on your points score, the 190 and 491 visas require state or territory nomination. This may open additional opportunities based on your occupation and location preferences.

**You can either:**

**1.** Select multiple visa subclasses (189, 190, and 491) within a single EOI, or  
**2.** Submit separate EOIs to each visa pathway.

### **3. Different State Nomination Strategies:**

**Some applicants create separate EOIs for different states, like:**

**1.** Victoria  
**2.** New South Wales  
**3.** South Australia  

This is only useful when your occupation is genuinely in demand across multiple states, and you meet each state’s eligibility rules.

## What You Should NOT Do

This is where many applicants go wrong. So, if you do not want to become one of them, avoid:

**1.** Creating multiple EOIs for the same occupation and the same visa subclass  
**2.** Copy-pasting identical profiles  
**3.** Changing minor details just to “increase chances”  
**4.** Submitting EOIs without a clear eligibility reason  

## Do Multiple EOIs Increase Your PR Chances?

Not automatically.

**It can help only when:**

**1.** You are eligible under multiple occupations  
**2.** You have different valid visa pathways  
**3.** Your profile strength varies significantly across streams  

**It backfires when:**

**1.** There is no clear strategy behind duplication  
**2.** Your occupation choice is inconsistent with your assessment  

## Not Sure Which EOI Strategy Is Right for You?

If you are considering multiple occupations, different visa subclasses, or state nomination pathways, it is important to assess your options before submitting your EOI. For some applicants, multiple EOIs can be beneficial. For others, a single well-optimised EOI may be the smarter choice.

The key is understanding which strategy best aligns with your occupation, points score, skills assessment outcome, and migration goals. Here, taking the time to plan your EOI approach carefully can help you focus on the pathways that offer the strongest chances of receiving an invitation and achieving [Australia PR](https://www.growmore.one/).

## FAQs

### **1. Can I submit more than one EOI for Australia PR?**

Yes, you can. But each EOI should represent a different strategy, like occupation, visa subclass, or state, not just duplicates.

### **2. Will multiple EOIs improve my chances?**

Not by default. It only helps if each EOI is genuinely different and adds a new pathway to your PR options.

### **3. When does it make sense to have multiple EOIs?**

When you’re eligible for different occupations, targeting different visas (189, 190, 491), or applying to multiple states.

### **4. Can I include multiple visas in one EOI?**

Yes. You can select 189, 190, and 491 in a single EOI, or split them into separate EOIs if your strategy differs.

### **5. What should I avoid while submitting EOIs?**

Don’t duplicate the same profile or tweak small details just to game the system. It won’t help and can create confusion in your strategy.
