There has been a noticeable increase in compliance and monitoring activity by the Department of Home Affairs and the Australian Border Force in relation to employer-sponsored visa programs.

This article is intended as a general reminder to sponsoring employers. It is not directed at any specific business, nor does it indicate any issue with any organisation. However, given the current regulatory environment, it is appropriate for employers to review their obligations and ensure that they remain fully compliant at all times.

## Ongoing Sponsorship Obligations

Employers often assume that their responsibilities conclude once a visa is granted, particularly under programs such as the [Subclass 482 visa](https://www.growmore.one/our-services/individual/employer-sponsored-visa/). In practice, sponsorship obligations continue for the duration of the employment relationship.

Employers must be able to demonstrate compliance at any time. This may occur through site visits, audits, or formal requests for information, which can be initiated without prior notice.

## Key Compliance Areas

**Employers should ensure that the following records and obligations are accurate, up to date, and readily accessible:**

### **1. Employment and Payroll Records**
&nbsp;
* Employment contracts consistent with the approved nomination  
* Payslips and salary payments aligned with approved remuneration  
* Superannuation contributions  
* Taxation and payroll records  

### **2. Workplace Records**
&nbsp;
* Timesheets and attendance records  
* Leave records in accordance with Fair Work requirements  

### **3. Position and Duties**
&nbsp;
* Duties performed by the employee must align with the nominated occupation  
* Evidence supporting genuine employment arrangements  

### **4. Sponsorship and Visa Monitoring**
&nbsp;
* Sponsorship approval and nomination documentation  
* Monitoring of employee visa conditions and validity  
* Ensuring that employees work strictly within the approved nomination parameters  

## Common Areas of Risk

**Based on practical experience, compliance concerns commonly arise from:**

* Salary payments that do not match the approved nomination  
* Employees performing duties outside the nominated occupation  
* Inadequate or incomplete record keeping  
* Changes to employment arrangements without proper assessment  

Even where there is no intention to breach obligations, these issues can result in serious consequences if identified during a compliance review.

## Compliance Monitoring and Inspections

Officers from the Department or the Australian Border Force may conduct inspections of business premises, interview staff, and request access to employment and payroll records. They may also assess whether the employee’s actual duties are consistent with the approved nomination.

Maintaining accurate and organised records is essential to demonstrate compliance and to respond effectively to any such requests.

## Recommended Approach

**Employers are encouraged to adopt a proactive approach to compliance by:**

* Conducting periodic internal reviews of sponsorship obligations  
* Maintaining well-organised and accessible records  
* Regularly reviewing employment arrangements  
* Seeking professional advice prior to implementing any significant changes  

A structured compliance approach can significantly reduce risk and provide assurance in the event of an audit or inspection.

## Professional Perspective

The increased compliance activity reflects a broader regulatory focus on maintaining the integrity of Australia’s migration program, ensuring that sponsorship arrangements are genuine, and protecting the rights of sponsored employees.

While this is a necessary development, it also means that even genuine and well-intentioned employers must exercise a high level of diligence in meeting their obligations.
